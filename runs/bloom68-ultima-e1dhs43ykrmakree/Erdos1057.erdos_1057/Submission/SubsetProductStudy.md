# Erdős 1057: an exponent-free subset-product route and its obstructions

## Status and scope

**No unconditional proof or disproof of Erdős 1057 was obtained.** In particular,
none of the counterexamples below is a counterexample to the Carmichael-number
conjecture. They falsify specific proposed *combinatorial inputs* to a route to
that conjecture.

`Submission/Spec.lean` was not edited, imported, or used as a theorem source.
Its original SHA-256 is
`bcf9eaa1edcfe52ec8bb8fc8329635273b082e6a2132eb34f1f73308cb937b9a`.
The target remains the stated logarithmic counting limit, not a modified notion
of Carmichael number.

The candidate investigated here is:

1. replace the Davenport/small-exponent step by an exponent-free, **fixed-size**
   Fourier estimate;
2. certify its character gap using local CRT distribution and the distinct
   divisor signatures of a common-multiplier prime pool;
3. retain enough sparse-subset entropy to approach exponent 1.

Step 1 has a rigorous sufficient criterion. The proposed local-to-global input
in Step 2 is false, even after retaining the distinct-signature condition.
Step 3 also requires substantially more prime supply than the existing exponent
benchmark. No claim of priority for the general Fourier method is intended;
the contribution here is the explicit criterion and the independently derived,
progressively strengthened stress tests.

## 1. A true exponent-free fixed-size Fourier criterion

Let `G` be a finite abelian group of order `g > 1`, and let `a_1,...,a_N` be an
indexed sequence in `G`. Define

\[
\delta=1-\max_{\chi\ne1}\left|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\chi(a_i)\right|,
\qquad 0\le\delta\le1,
\]

where the maximum is over all nonprincipal complex characters of `G`.
Let `Z_t` count the `t`-element subsets of indices whose product is the identity.
For `1 <= t < N`,

\[
Z_t\ \ge\ \frac{\binom Nt}{g}
 \left(1-(g-1)(N+1)
 e^{-\delta t(1-t/N)}\right).                                      \tag{1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\delta t(1-t/N)\ge\log\bigl(2(g-1)(N+1)\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z_t\ge\frac{\binom Nt}{2g}.              \tag{2}
\]

There is no group-exponent hypothesis. This does **not** assert that the needed
character gap holds for Carmichael prime pools.

### Proof

Character orthogonality gives

\[
Z_t=\frac1g\sum_\chi[z^t]\prod_{i=1}^N(1+z\chi(a_i)).
\]

Put `rho=t/(N-t)`, and let `z=rho exp(i theta)`. For `|w|=1`,

\[
|1+\rho w|^2=(1+\rho)^2-2\rho(1-\Re w),
\]

and `sqrt(1-u) <= exp(-u/2)` for `0 <= u <= 1`. Thus for nonprincipal `chi`,

\[
\left|\prod_i(1+\rho e^{i\theta}\chi(a_i))\right|
\le(1+\rho)^N
 \exp\left(-\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho)^2}
          \sum_i(1-\Re(e^{i\theta}\chi(a_i)))\right)
\le(1+\rho)^N e^{-\delta t(1-t/N)}.
\]

The last inequality is uniform in `theta` because
`Re(exp(i theta) sum chi(a_i)) <= |sum chi(a_i)| <= N(1-delta)`.
Cauchy's coefficient formula therefore bounds the absolute value of the
nonprincipal coefficient by

\[
\rho^{-t}(1+\rho)^N e^{-\delta t(1-t/N)}.
\]

For a binomial random variable `B` with parameters `N,t/N`, the integer `t` is a
mode. Hence `Pr(B=t) >= 1/(N+1)`, since there are `N+1` possible values. Equivalently,

\[
\rho^{-t}(1+\rho)^N\le(N+1)\binom Nt.
\]

The principal character contributes `binom(N,t)`. Subtracting absolute values
of the other `g-1` terms proves (1), and (2) follows. This proof handles sampling
**without replacement** and an exact cardinality; an unrestricted `2^N/g`
subset count would not provide the required sparse entropy.

## 2. Exact Carmichael consequence and the remaining entropy requirement

Suppose `k,L >= 1`, `(k,L)=1`, and `p_1,...,p_N <= z` are **distinct primes** with

\[
p_i=kd_i+1,\qquad d_i\mid L,\qquad (p_i,L)=1.
\]

Take `G` to be the subgroup of `(Z/LZ)^*` generated by their residues, and define
`delta` using all characters of that subgroup. If (2) holds for `t >= 2`, then

\[
 C(z^t)\ge \frac{\binom Nt}{2|G|}.                                 \tag{3}
\]

Indeed, a counted product `n` is squarefree and composite; it is 1 modulo `L`
and also 1 modulo `k`. Coprimality gives `kL | n-1`, and then
`p_i-1=kd_i | n-1` for every selected prime. This is exactly Korselt's criterion.
Unique factorization makes different selected prime subsets give different
integers.

Since `binom(N,t) >= (N/t)^t`, (3) implies

\[
\frac{\log C(z^t)}{t\log z}
\ge \frac{\log(N/t)}{\log z}-\frac{\log(2|G|)}{t\log z}.             \tag{4}
\]

Thus a sufficient entropy regime is

\[
 N=z^{1-o(1)},\qquad t=z^{o(1)},\qquad
 \log|G|=o(t\log z),
\]

together with the Fourier hypothesis. To obtain the **eventual bound for every
large X**, rather than a subsequence bound, the constructed scales also need
coverage, for example `log X_(j+1)/log X_j -> 1`.

This does not follow merely by replacing the group-theoretic step. At the
certificate scale `X=z^t`, even declaring *every* `t`-subset successful yields
at most `binom(N,t) <= (eN/t)^t` outputs. If `N=z^(alpha+o(1))` and
`t=z^(beta+o(1))`, this certificate has exponent at most `alpha-beta+o(1)`.
This is a statement about this size certificate, not a universal upper bound
on Carmichael numbers or on constructions using smaller primes.

In particular, feeding in only the known `EB` prime-pool lower bound does not
turn it into exponent 1. Moreover `N <= (z-1)/k`, so a common-multiplier pool with
`N=z^(1-o(1))` must have `k=z^o(1)`. A new arithmetic supply theorem is needed in
addition to any group-theoretic improvement.

## 3. Symmetrizing the actual prime pool by inverse pairs is impossible

**Arithmetic lemma.** If `(k,L)=1`, `d|L`, `e|L`, and

\[
(kd+1)(ke+1)\equiv1\pmod L,
\]

then `d=e`. Neither primality nor squarefreeness of `L` is needed.

**Proof.** Expand and cancel `k` modulo `L`:

\[
L\mid k(kde+d+e)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L\mid kde+d+e.
\]

Since `d|L`, reducing the latter divisibility modulo `d` gives `d|e`.
Similarly `e|d`, and therefore `d=e`.

Consequently two distinct primes in a common-`k` divisor pool cannot be mutual
inverses modulo `L`. Freely adjoining inverse residues, or replacing signed
subset relations by positive products using inverse partners, is not an
available arithmetic operation. Allowing a repeated prime instead would
violate squarefreeness. This lemma is checked in Lean.

A related necessary structure is

\[
\gcd(p_i-1,L)=\gcd(kd_i,L)=d_i.
\]

For squarefree `L`, distinct pool elements therefore have distinct patterns of
CRT coordinates equal to the identity. The next stress test deliberately
retains that necessary condition; it is not just a repeated-element example.

## 4. Strong counterexample: local mixing plus distinct divisor signatures

Here is the refined counterexample to the proposed certification step.
There are groups

\[
G_r=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathbb Z/\ell_j\mathbb Z
    \simeq \mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z
\]

and sets `A_r` with all of the following properties:

* each `ell_j` is a prime in `[r^2,2r^2]`, so every component order is
  `O((log |G_r|)^2)`;
* `|A_r|` exceeds every fixed power of `log |G_r|`;
* the map `a -> {j : a_j=0}` is injective on `A_r`;
* `A_r` generates `G_r` (indeed its differences generate `G_r`);
* projections onto **any at most `floor(r^(1/8))` coordinates** have total
  variation distance `o(1)` from uniform, uniformly over those projections;
* every proper subgroup coset contains only `o(|A_r|)` elements of `A_r`;
* nevertheless **there is no nonempty zero-sum subset at all**.

All assertions are unconditional. Existence of the required `r` primes for
large `r` follows from the ordinary prime number theorem. The rest is an
elementary finite probabilistic construction, as follows.

### Construction and the deterministic assertions

Put

\[
s=\lfloor\sqrt r\rfloor,\quad N=\binom rs,\quad
M=\prod_{j=1}^r\ell_j,\quad H=\lfloor\sqrt M\rfloor.
\]

For each `s`-element subset `S` of `{1,...,r}`, put `b_S=prod_(j in S) ell_j`.
Independently choose an integer `a_S` uniformly from

\[
\{1\le a\le H:\gcd(a,M)=b_S\}.                                   \tag{5}
\]

These sets are nonempty for sufficiently large `r`. To see this, write
`a=b_S u`, `1 <= u <= T_S=floor(H/b_S)`. It suffices to exclude `u` divisible by
any `ell_j` with `j notin S`. The fraction excluded is at most
`sum_(j notin S) 1/ell_j <= 1/r`, while

\[
T_{\min}\ge\left\lfloor\frac{H}{(2r^2)^s}\right\rfloor\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Different `S` give different gcds and hence different `a_S`, with exactly the
stated zero-coordinate signatures. Also, for every coordinate `j` there are
sets `S` containing `j` and sets not containing `j`. Thus the elements, and
also their differences, are not all divisible by any `ell_j`; they generate the
cyclic squarefree group `Z/MZ`.

Most importantly,

\[
0<\sum_{S\in\mathcal T}a_S\le NH<M
\quad\text{for every nonempty family }\mathcal T,
\]

because `N <= r^s < r^r <= sqrt(M)`. Therefore **every possible selection in
(5) is zero-sum-free**. Also
`log N >= s log(r/s) ~ (sqrt(r)/2) log r`, whereas
`log M = 2r log r + O(r)`, giving the asserted size comparison.

### Proof that the choices can have the claimed local distribution

Write `b=floor(r^(1/8))`, `R_max=(2r^2)^b`, and fix a set `J` of at most `b`
coordinates. A fraction at most `|J|s/r` of the index sets `S` intersect `J`.
For these indices use the trivial total-variation bound 1.

If `S` is disjoint from `J`, multiplication by `b_S` is invertible modulo
`R_J=prod_(j in J) ell_j`. The uniform distribution on `1,...,T_S` reduces
modulo `R_J` to within `R_J/(4T_S)` of uniform. Indeed if
`T_S=aR_J+c`, its exact total variation is
`c(R_J-c)/(T_S R_J)`.

Conditioning this uniform integer on avoiding the other primes changes total
variation by at most the probability removed, at most `1/r`. Thus, by convexity
of total variation, the *expected empirical distribution* on projection `J`
is within

\[
\varepsilon_r=\frac{bs}{r}+\frac1r+
             \frac{R_{\max}}{4T_{\min}}=o(1)                      \tag{6}
\]

of uniform, uniformly in `J`.

For completeness, expectation is not being confused with existence of a single
good set. Put `eta=r^(-1/8)`. For a fixed projection and residue cell, the
empirical frequency is an average of `N` independent `[0,1]` variables, so
Hoeffding's inequality bounds a deviation exceeding `eta/R_max` by

\[
2\exp(-2N\eta^2/R_{\max}^2).
\]

The number of all such cells over projections of sizes `1,...,b` is at most
`K_r=b(2r^3)^b`. A union bound therefore bounds the probability of any failure by

\[
2K_r\exp(-2N\eta^2/R_{\max}^2)=o(1).
\]

Here `log N` has order `sqrt(r) log r`, while `log R_max` and `log K_r` have
order `r^(1/8) log r`. On the successful event every projection differs from
its expectation in total variation by at most `eta/2`, and hence from uniform
by at most `epsilon_r+eta/2=o(1)`. This proves existence of a single set with
all the required local properties simultaneously.

Every coset of a proper subgroup of cyclic `Z/MZ` is a residue class modulo
some divisor `D>1` of `M`, hence is contained in a fixed residue class modulo
some prime `ell_j|D`. The one-coordinate estimate bounds its mass by
`1/ell_j+o(1)=o(1)`, proving the coset assertion.

### The invisible high-order character

The faithful character

\[
\chi(a)=\exp(2\pi i a/M)
\]

has nontrivial support in **all** CRT coordinates. Since every `a_S <= sqrt(M)`,

\[
1-\left|\frac1N\sum_S\chi(a_S)\right|
\le 1-\Re\frac1N\sum_S\chi(a_S)
\le \frac{2\pi^2 H^2}{M^2}\le\frac{2\pi^2}{M}.
\]

Thus the global gap can be tiny despite all the local estimates above. This is
precisely the sort of high-support, high-order obstruction that a local CRT
argument cannot simply discard. There are far more subsets than group elements,
but subset collisions only yield *signed* relations, not a nonempty positive
zero sum.

These groups can be embedded in squarefree unit groups: choose distinct primes
`q_j = 1 mod ell_j` and elements of order `ell_j` modulo `q_j`, and apply CRT to
`L=prod q_j`. The zero-coordinate signatures become identity-coordinate
signatures, with
`gcd(u_S-1,L)=prod_(j in S) q_j`.

**Essential limitation:** this does not realize the residues as primes
`u_S=kd_S+1` for a *common* `k`. The affine common-multiplier condition and
primality remain additional arithmetic structure. The theorem disproves the
local-to-global combinatorial lemma, not the conjecture or every possible
arithmetic Fourier argument.

## 5. A simpler exact-marginal obstruction, with a concrete instance

For an odd prime `ell`, `r>=2`, and `1<=h<ell`, put

\[
G=(\mathbb F_\ell)^r,\quad \sigma(x)=x_1+\cdots+x_r,\quad
A=\{x:\sigma(x)\in\{1,\ldots,h\}\}.
\]

Then `N=h ell^(r-1)`. Every projection onto `j<r` coordinates is **exactly
uniform**: each fixed projected value has `h ell^(r-j-1)` preimages in `A`.
Every proper affine subspace contains at most `N/h` elements of `A`:
if `sigma` is constant on it, its size is at most `ell^(r-1)`; otherwise its
intersection with `A` has exactly `h/ell` of its points. As additive subgroups
are linear subspaces here, this covers every proper subgroup coset.

But a nonempty subset of size `t` with `ht<ell` cannot sum to zero. Its image
under `sigma` lifts to an integer between `t` and `ht`, strictly between 0 and
`ell`. Taking `h=floor(sqrt(ell))` and fixed `r` rules out every prescribed
polylogarithmic subset length for large `ell`, despite vanishing coset
concentration and exact proper-coordinate marginals.

All characters not proportional to `sigma` have average zero on `A`. The
character `exp(2 pi i sigma/ell)` has average of modulus

\[
\frac{\sin(\pi h/\ell)}{h\sin(\pi/\ell)},
\]

so the global `delta` in Section 1 is at most
`pi^2(h^2-1)/(6 ell^2)`. This last inequality follows by centering the phases
and applying `1-cos u <= u^2/2`; the variance of `1,...,h` is `(h^2-1)/12`.

For `ell=101`, `h=10`, `r=2`, the set has 1010 elements in a group of order
10201, uniform one-coordinate marginals, and at most 10% in any proper subgroup
coset, but **zero** zero-sum subsets of sizes 1 through 10. The false uniform
prediction at size 10 would exceed
`floor(binom(1010,10)/(2*10201)) = 14268136447788564063`.

A concrete unit-group embedding uses `L=607*809=491063`, with generators `122`
modulo 607 and `89` modulo 809, both of order 101. Map `(x,y)` to the CRT residue
with coordinates `122^x` and `89^y`. This is again an ambient group example,
not an admissible prime pool for that modulus.
