# Regular circuit rounding: a cographic rank theorem, exact signed lifts, and an unsigned cone hole

## Status and main conclusions

**This does not solve the unrestricted graphic Erdős–Gallai problem.** No universal constant `C` with `c(G) <= c_f(G) + C r(G)` for all even simple graphs is proved, and no family with `c(G)/|V(G)|` unbounded is produced. The audited input `c_f(G) <= r(G)` from `ResearchFractional.md` is retained. The Petersen rings, support-only obstructions, and local-transition obstructions in `ResearchRounding.md` are not bypassed by a generic integrality assertion.

The complete auxiliary results developed here are:

1. **A global cographic rank bound.** If `M=M*(H)` is a nonempty simple Eulerian cographic matroid, and `b` is the number of edge-containing blocks of the representing graph H, then

   ```
   c(M) <= r(M)-b,             0 <= c(M)-c_f(M) <= r(M)-2b.           (A)
   ```

   A constructive blockwise-star proof uses the single account `sum_i(r_i-1)=r-b`. This is about **bonds of a bipartite H**, not cycles of an arbitrary even graph G.

2. **An exact regular orientation lift.** For a TU representation A of an Eulerian regular matroid,

   ```
   {z : Az=0, -1<=z<=1} = conv {s in {-1,1}^E : As=0}.              (B)
   ```

   Signed-circuit fractional partitions project onto exactly this polytope. However, moving to a vertex can increase their minimum cost. Conformal signed circuits do have an elementary integer-decomposition property, proved below; its immediate unit-vector count is **nullity**, not rank.

3. **The two cost losses in that lift are separately real.** Let `mu_f` be the minimum exact fractional cost over all coherent unit orientations. Then

   ```
   c_f <= mu_f <= c.
   ```

   For the simple Eulerian cographic matroid `M*(K_(a,b))`, `a,b>=3`,

   ```
   r=(a-1)(b-1),   c_f=ab/(r+1),   mu_f=c=2.                        (C)
   ```

   In particular, `M*(K_(3,3))` has `c_f=9/5 < mu_f=c=2`. For the regular atom `R10`, an explicit TU representation gives

   ```
   r=5,                  c_f=mu_f=5/3 < c=2.                       (D)
   ```

   Thus neither orientation coherence nor integer cost rounding is automatic from TU.

4. **Unsigned circuit normality is actually false inside the graphic target class.** There is a connected simple even graph Q on ten vertices, with

   ```
   c(Q)=c_f(Q)=2,
   ```

   and an explicitly given vector `w in {0,1,2}^{E(Q)}` lying in the cone and integer lattice generated by simple-cycle vectors, but not in their nonnegative integer monoid. All weighted vertex degrees of w are even. A five-ray face has an exact index-two lattice obstruction. This is an explicit counterexample to a *generic unsigned Hilbert-basis shortcut*, **not** to the unit-right-hand-side rounding target.

All proofs are at paper level, not Lean formalizations. No literature-priority claim is made. `ResearchRegularCheck.py` uses exact arithmetic and exhaustive finite checks, with no numerical LP/MILP. The specification and all 17 pre-existing Submission files were preserved.

## 1. Definitions: three different circuit objects

Let M be a finite binary matroid on E. A circuit is a nonempty inclusion-minimal dependent set. A binary cycle means a disjoint union of circuits, and need not itself be a circuit. M is Eulerian when E is a binary cycle. For Eulerian M set

```
c(M)   = minimum number of circuits in a partition of E,
c_f(M) = min sum_C x_C,  x_C>=0,  sum_{C containing e} x_C=1.
```

For a regular matroid choose a full-row-rank totally unimodular integer representation `A in Z^(r x m)`. All quantities involving signs below use this chosen representation; changing a reference edge direction merely relabels the sign vectors. Reduction modulo two represents the same matroid. Eulerianity is equivalent to `A 1=0 (mod 2)`. Every circuit C has two primitive signed kernel vectors `g_C,-g_C`, with entries in `{0,1,-1}` and support C. The entry bound follows from Cramer's rule and TU. In particular,

```
A g_C=0,       |g_C|=chi_C,
```

but **usually `A chi_C != 0`**. The signed vectors and their absolute values are not interchangeable configurations.

The two graph interpretations are:

| Matroid | Circuits | Eulerian condition | Matroid rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| `M(G)` | simple cycles of G | every vertex degree of G is even | `|V(G)|-kappa(G)` |
| `M*(H)` | bonds, i.e. minimal nonempty cuts of H | H is bipartite | `|E(H)|-|V(H)|+kappa(H)` |

For the second row, loops of H are automatically excluded by Eulerianity. Parallel edges of H are allowed even when `M*(H)` is simple. Simplicity there excludes bonds of sizes one and two, not parallel edges of H.

For a finite vector set X, distinguish

```
cone(X)    = nonnegative REAL combinations,
monoid(X)  = nonnegative INTEGER combinations,
lattice(X) = INTEGER combinations, with negative coefficients allowed.
```

The unsigned Hilbert-basis assertion would be `monoid(X)=cone(X) intersect lattice(X)`. Section 6 gives an explicit failure for simple-cycle vectors of an even simple graph.

## 2. What TU really gives: an exact orientation cube

### Lemma 2.1 — the Eulerian TU cube

Let A be TU, with `A 1=0 (mod 2)`, and define

```
P_A = {z in R^m : Az=0, -1<=z_e<=1}.
```

Then every vertex of `P_A` has all coordinates in `{-1,1}`. Consequently (B) holds. More generally, any feasible fixing of some coordinates to signs `+1` or `-1` extends to a full sign vector s with `As=0`.

**Proof.** The usual TU vertex-integrality argument, applied to A with unit lower and upper bounds, makes every vertex z integral. Thus its coordinates belong to `{-1,0,1}`. Let Z be its zero-coordinate set.

The columns `A_Z` are independent over the reals: otherwise a nonzero kernel direction supported on Z could be added and subtracted by a sufficiently small amount, keeping all coordinates in the cube and contradicting extremality. For a TU matrix, real column independence implies binary column independence, since a nonzero square minor is `+1` or `-1` and remains nonzero modulo two.

But `Az=0`, reduced modulo two, says `A chi_(E\Z)=0`. Combining this with `A 1=0 (mod 2)` gives `A chi_Z=0 (mod 2)`. Independence therefore forces `Z=empty`.

Every feasible sign vector is already a vertex of the cube, and hence a vertex of its intersection with the kernel. Compactness proves (B). Fixing coordinates to their bounds defines a face; every nonempty such face has a vertex of `P_A`, which proves the extension assertion. □

The Eulerian hypothesis is necessary. For example, the directed-incidence representation of K4 is TU, but `P_A` has vertices with zero coordinates and no feasible full sign vector.

### Lemma 2.2 — conformal signed integer decomposition, with the correct dimension

Fix an orthant and let `x in ker(A) intersect Z^m` lie in it. Then x is a nonnegative integer combination of primitive signed circuits in that same orthant.

For a feasible unit sign vector s, this gives a partition of E into conformal circuits, with

```
number of circuits <= dim ker(A) = m-r.                            (2.1)
```

**Proof.** Reorient coordinates so x is nonnegative. Inside its support choose a nonzero nonnegative kernel vector with minimal support. Its support is a circuit: if the restricted kernel had a second direction, perturbing in that direction until a coordinate first vanishes would give a nonzero nonnegative kernel vector with smaller support. Its primitive circuit generator has all nonzero coordinates equal to one, by TU and Cramer's rule.

Subtract the minimum positive coordinate of x on this circuit, times that generator. The multiplier is an integer, the residual remains a nonnegative integral kernel vector, and its support strictly shrinks. Iterate. Reversing the reorientation gives the signed assertion.

For a unit vector s, every multiplier is one, and the circuit supports are disjoint. Their nonzero kernel vectors are linearly independent, because every circuit has a coordinate used by no other one. Their number is therefore at most `m-r`. □

This proves a genuine signed-cone integer-decomposition statement; it is not an assumed unsigned Hilbert-basis theorem. In the graphic case the circuit supports in this argument are **directed simple cycles**. A closed trail with repeated vertices is not a minimal support and is not counted as one circuit.

Equation (2.1) is not the desired rank bound. For `G=K_(2s+1)`,

```
r=2s,       m-r=s(2s-1),       c_f(G)=c(G)=s.
```

Replacing the `m-r` in this argument by r would be an unjustified dimensional reduction.

### Lemma 2.3 — exact signed lift, but no cost-preserving vertex selection

Let `S(A)={s in {-1,1}^E:As=0}`. For `s in S(A)`, let `c_f(M,s)` be the exact fractional circuit-partition optimum using only circuits whose signed generator is conformal to s, and let `c(M,s)` be the corresponding integer optimum. Define

```
mu_f(M) = min_{s in S(A)} c_f(M,s).
```

Then

```
c_f(M) <= mu_f(M) <= c(M),       c(M)=min_s c(M,s).                (2.2)
```

There is an exact projection explanation. Introduce a variable `lambda_g>=0` for each of the two signed generators of every circuit, with

```
sum_g lambda_g |g| = 1,              z=sum_g lambda_g g.           (2.3)
```

The projection of (2.3) onto z is precisely `P_A`.

* Every projected z satisfies `Az=0` and `|z_e|<=1`.
* Conversely, express z as a convex combination of unit signs using Lemma 2.1. Decompose each sign using Lemma 2.2, and combine those partitions to obtain (2.3).
* The minimum value of `sum_g lambda_g` in the fiber `z=0` equals `c_f(M)`: split any unsigned optimum equally between the two signs of each circuit.
* In a vertex fiber `z=s`, equality `|z_e|=sum_g lambda_g |g_e|=1` forbids cancellation at every coordinate. Thus every used g is conformal to s, and the fiber cost is exactly `c_f(M,s)`.

Finally, orient the edge-disjoint circuits of any unsigned partition separately. Their signed sum is a feasible unit s, proving the integer equality in (2.2). □

Thus there are two distinct objective losses:

```
c-c_f = (mu_f-c_f) + (c-mu_f).                                    (2.4)
```

TU describes the projected polytope and gives integer feasibility within each orthant. It does **not** make either term in (2.4) vanish, nor bound their sum by O(r). Sections 4 and 5 give exact examples isolating the two terms.

## 3. A complete global rank theorem for the cographic class

### Theorem 3.1 — blockwise-star rounding

Let `M=M*(H)` be a simple Eulerian cographic matroid. Let `H_1,...,H_b` be the edge-containing blocks of H, with isolated vertices ignored. Two vertices joined by parallel edges are allowed as a block. Write

```
n_i=|V(H_i)|,   m_i=|E(H_i)|,   r_i=m_i-n_i+1,
A_i,B_i = a bipartition of H_i.
```

Then there is a circuit partition with at most

```
sum_i min(|A_i|,|B_i|) <= sum_i(r_i-1) = r(M)-b                 (3.1)
```

circuits. In particular,

```
0 <= c(M)-c_f(M) <= r(M)-b-c_f(M) <= r(M)-2b.                    (3.2)
```

The edgeless case has `c=c_f=r=b=0`.

**Proof.** First, every nonempty cut contains a bond. If B is a bond contained in a cut D, then `D\B=D triangle B` is again a cut. Induction decomposes every cut into disjoint bonds. The symmetric difference of cuts is a cut, so E is a disjoint union of bonds if and only if E itself is a cut. This is exactly the condition that H is bipartite.

Bonds are local to blocks. A bond of a block extends to a bond of H by putting each attached branch on the same shore as its attachment vertex. Conversely, a cut meeting more than one block contains such an extended nonempty bond from one of them, so cannot be minimal. This also proves that both `c` and `c_f` add over blocks. The rank identity is

```
r(M)=|E(H)|-|V(H)|+kappa(H)=sum_i(m_i-n_i+1)=sum_i r_i.           (3.3)
```

For a vertex v of a block `H_i`, its entire star in that block is a bond: one shore is `{v}` and the other is connected. This includes a two-vertex parallel-edge block. Because M is simple, each such bond has at least three edges. Hence every block has minimum degree at least three, and

```
2m_i >= 3n_i,              n_i/2 <= m_i-n_i = r_i-1.             (3.4)
```

Choose the smaller bipartition class of `H_i`. The stars centered there are disjoint bonds and cover every edge exactly once. Their count is at most `n_i/2`, which by (3.4) is at most `r_i-1`. Summing gives (3.1).

Every nonempty block has fractional cost at least one: the exact constraint at any one of its edges is bounded above by the block's total coefficient mass. Additivity gives `c_f(M)>=b`, proving (3.2). □

This is a **single global rank account**, not an O(r) payment at every recursion level. It constructs an integral partition independently of a specified fractional support and bounds its excess above the full fractional optimum.

### Scope of this theorem

This settles an additive-rank test on **all simple Eulerian cographic matroids**, not on all regular matroids. Its structural resource is `2m_i>=3n_i` in the *cut-representing graph*, equivalently the linear-size bound `m_i<=3(r_i-1)` for simple cographic blocks. Dense simple graphic matroids do not satisfy that size/rank bound.

In particular, one must not apply (3.1) to `M*(G)` and call its bonds cycles of G, or replace `r(M*(G))` by `r(M(G))`. The two rows of the table in Section 1 have different circuits, ranks, and Eulerian hypotheses.

## 4. Exact cographic costs and the loss from choosing an orientation

### Theorem 4.1 — complete bipartite bond partitions

Let `H=K_(a,b)`, with `a,b>=2`, and let `M=M*(H)`. Put

```
L=ab-a-b+2=(a-1)(b-1)+1=r(M)+1.
```

Then

```
c_f(M)=ab/L,             mu_f(M)=c(M)=2.                          (4.1)
```

The matroid is simple when `a,b>=3`. The formulas remain valid when one part has size two, but those cases are not examples of the simple-cographic rank theorem.

### 4.1 The unsigned optimum, with a certificate for every bond

If a shore S has x vertices in A and y in B, its cut size is

```
x(b-y)+(a-x)y = bx+ay-2xy.                                       (4.2)
```

A bond is a star, or has `1<=x<=a-1` and `1<=y<=b-1`, since both shores must induce connected subgraphs. The bilinear expression (4.2) is maximized on this rectangle at a corner. The possible corner values are `a+b-2` and L, with

```
L-(a+b-2)=(a-2)(b-2)>=0.
```

Also `L>=a,b`. Thus every bond has size at most L, and the uniform signed-dual candidate `y_e=1/L` is feasible for **all** circuits of M, with objective `ab/L`.

For `u in A, v in B`, define the large bond

```
D_(u,v) = delta({u} union (B\{v})).
```

It contains the edge uv and every edge between `A\{u}` and `B\{v}`, so has size L. Any given edge belongs to exactly

```
1+(a-1)(b-1)=L
```

of the ab indexed bonds. Coefficient `1/L` on each gives an exact fractional partition, proving `c_f=ab/L`.

For an integer partition take `delta({u,v})` and its edge complement `D_(u,v)`. Both are bonds and they partition E. E itself is not a bond: its bipartition shores are independent sets of size at least two. Therefore `c=2`.

### 4.2 All coherent unit orientations can be classified

Choose the standard TU cographic representation whose real kernel is the cut space of H. Concretely, if a spanning-tree normalization of a reduced incidence matrix is `[I | N]`, use `[-N^T | I]` with the corresponding column order. Relative to the reference orientation of H, a unit kernel vector is a gradient

```
s_(uv)=h(v)-h(u),             |h(v)-h(u)|=1.                       (4.3)
```

Fix one height to zero. Connectivity makes all heights integral. A signed bond is conformal to s precisely when it is a **directed bond** after orienting every edge from its lower endpoint to its higher endpoint.

For `K_(a,b)`, (4.3) has only two types, up to translating heights and interchanging the parts:

1. All vertices of A have one height and all vertices of B the next height.
2. One part is a middle level T, while the other part splits into nonempty low and high levels U,W. All arcs go `U -> T -> W`.

Indeed, if two vertices in one part have different heights, they must differ by two, and every common neighbour is forced to the unique intermediate height. If one part is constant, the other has only the two adjacent heights.

In type 1 the only directed bonds are vertex stars. Their exact fractional and integer partition costs both equal `min(a,b)`: use the stars in a smaller part, and certify the lower bound with uniform edge price `1/max(a,b)` on these directed-bond constraints.

In type 2 put `p=|U|`, `q=|W|`, `k=|T|`. The directed bonds are exactly:

* a star centered in U;
* a star centered in W;
* `delta(U union Y)` for a nonempty proper subset `Y of T`.

To see completeness, a source shore containing a vertex of T must contain all of U; if it contains a vertex of W, it must contain all of T as well. Requiring both shores to be connected gives precisely this list.

Give an edge between U,T price `1/(pk)` and an edge between T,W price `1/(qk)`. A star in U has price `1/p<=1`, a star in W has price `1/q<=1`, and every listed cross-bond has price

```
p(k-|Y|)/(pk) + q|Y|/(qk) = 1.
```

The total price is two. Conversely, for any nonempty proper Y the two directed bonds

```
delta(U union Y),        delta(U union (T\Y))
```

partition all edges. Thus every type-2 orientation has both costs exactly two. Type 2 is available whenever `a,b>=2`; type 1 never costs less than two. This proves `mu_f=2` and completes (4.1). □

### Consequences for regular rounding

For `a=b=3`, the orientation-projection lemma has center-fiber cost `9/5`, but **every vertex fiber has cost at least 2**. This explicitly refutes the claim that TU of the projected orientation polytope lets one select a coherent orientation without losing fractional objective.

For general `a,b>=3`, the exact coherence loss is

```
mu_f-c_f = (a-2)(b-2) / ((a-1)(b-1)+1).                           (4.4)
```

For t direct-sum copies of `M*(K_(3,3))`, costs and ranks add:

```
r=4t,       mu_f-c_f=t/5=r/20,       c-mu_f=0.
```

This is a genuine linear-rank loss in the first term of (2.4), but is comfortably consistent with an additive O(r) theorem. It is not a graphic counterexample.

## 5. A regular atom with a conformal integer COST gap

The standard regular matroid `R10` has the following TU representation. Label its columns `e_0,...,e_4,f_0,...,f_4`, with subscripts modulo five:

```
A=[I_5 | B],
B_(i,i)=-1,       B_(i,i-1)=B_(i,i+1)=1,       all other entries 0.
```

The binary version is the usual `R10` matrix, for example the matrix displayed in `/corpus/src/1807.05164/near-shortest-vectors-arxiv.tex:984-993`, with the two column blocks interchanged. The results here are checked directly from the displayed representation, not inferred from a decomposition theorem.

### 5.1 Regularity and circuit list

For completeness TU can be verified by the Ghouila-Houri row-signing criterion. Up to cyclic rotation and reflection, the selected row subsets and sufficient signs are:

| Selected rows | Signs on those rows |
|---|---|
| `{0}` | `+` |
| `{0,1}` | `+,+` |
| `{0,2}` | `+,-` |
| `{0,1,2}` | `+,+,+` |
| `{0,1,3}` | `+,+,-` |
| `{0,1,2,3}` | `+,+,-,-` |
| all five | all `+` |

Every resulting column sum of B is in `{0,1,-1}`; the identity columns satisfy this automatically. The empty subset is harmless. The checker independently verifies **all 3,002 nonempty square minors** to be `0,+1,-1`.

The rank is five because of the identity block. All ten binary columns are nonzero and distinct, so the matroid is simple. Each row sum of A is two, so it is Eulerian over the binary field.

Its fifteen four-element circuits are the following three five-element orbits:

```
Q_i^1 = {e_(i-1),e_i,e_(i+1),f_i},
Q_i^2 = {e_i,e_(i+2),f_(i+3),f_(i+4)},
Q_i^3 = {e_i,f_i,f_(i+2),f_(i+3)}.                                (5.1)
```

The other fifteen circuits are their six-element complements. Here is a short completeness proof. A subset of the five f-columns uniquely determines the e-part of a binary null vector. For f-part sizes zero through five, the resulting 32 null vectors have sizes:

```
0 f's: one empty vector;
1 f :  five of size 4;
2 f's: five of size 4 and five of size 6;
3 f's: the complements of the preceding ten;
4 f's: five of size 6;
5 f's: the whole ten-element set.
```

For the two-f case, adjacent f-indices give size four, nonadjacent indices size six. Complementation follows because the whole ground set is a null vector. There are no nonzero null vectors smaller than four. A size-six null vector cannot contain a size-four one, since their difference would be a size-two null vector. Thus all the size-four and size-six sets are circuits, and the ten-element set is not.

Every element occurs in six of the four-circuits in (5.1), and hence in nine of their six-element complements.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact two-stage costs on R10

```
c_f(R10)=mu_f(R10)=5/3,             c(R10)=2.                      (5.2)
```

**Proof.** Put coefficient `1/9` on all fifteen six-circuits. The occurrence count proves exact unit coverage, of cost `5/3`. All circuits have size at most six, so uniform edge price `1/6` certifies the same fractional lower bound. A four-circuit and its complement partition E, whereas E is not a circuit. Hence `c=2`.

For the coherent fractional assertion take the unit sign vector

```
s_(e_i)=-1,        s_(f_i)=1.
```

Since every row of B sums to one, `As=0`. The five circuits `Q_i^2` are conformal: their real signed relations are

```
f_(i+3)+f_(i+4)-e_i-e_(i+2)=0.
```

Their complements are conformal as well, since s is a kernel vector. Each element belongs to exactly three of these five six-circuits. Coefficient `1/3` on each therefore gives a coherent exact fractional partition of cost `5/3`. Combined with `c_f<=mu_f`, this proves (5.2). □

Thus the fixed-orthant circuit generators satisfy the integer-decomposition property of Lemma 2.2, but their unit-cost partition LP still has gap `1/3`. **Hilbert generation is feasibility, not optimality of the number of generators.** For t direct-sum copies, this second loss is `t/3=r/15`, while the orientation-coherence loss is zero.

The checker also enumerates all twelve unit kernel sign vectors of this representation. Each has five conformal four-circuits and five conformal six-circuits, and the same exact fractional and integer costs. Only the one explicit orientation above is needed for the proof.

This finite atom calculation and Theorem 3.1 do not establish closure under all regular-matroid 2- or 3-sums. More importantly, the graphic atoms of a regular decomposition still contain the entire original graphic problem.

## 6. An explicit unsigned cone hole in a simple even graphic matroid

### 6.1 The graph and the weighted vector

Use the Petersen graph P on vertices

```
a_i=i,        b_i=i+5,        i in Z/5Z,
```

with edges `a_i a_(i+1)`, `b_i b_(i+2)`, and the spokes `a_i b_i`. Let M be the five spokes and F the other ten edges. Define

```
w_e=2 on M,           w_e=1 on F.                                (6.1)
```

Every weighted degree is four and `w(E(P))=20`.

To place the obstruction inside an **even simple underlying graph**, add the disjoint complementary matching

```
N={09,18,26,37,45},
```

and call the resulting graph Q. None of these edges is in P. Q is connected, simple, 4-regular, with ten vertices and twenty edges. Extend w by zero on N.

### 6.2 An exact conic representation

Let `A_i` be the cyclic-index rotations of the following eight-cycle:

```
A_0=(1,2,7,9,4,3,8,6).                                          (6.2)
```

Each `A_i` alternates between spokes and nonspokes. Every spoke is in four of them, every F-edge is in two, and no N-edge is in any. Consequently

```
w = (1/2) sum_(i=0)^4 chi_(A_i).                                 (6.3)
```

This proves membership in the cone of simple-cycle vectors.

### 6.3 An explicit INTEGER lattice representation

Let

```
U_0=(0,1,6,8,5),
V_0=(0,4,9,7,5),
W_0=(0,1,6,8,5,7,9,4),
```

and rotate indices modulo five to obtain `U_i,V_i,W_i`. All are simple cycles of P. Their edge sets satisfy

```
U_i intersect V_i = {a_i b_i},       U_i triangle V_i = W_i.
```

Therefore

```
2 chi_{a_i b_i} = chi_(U_i)+chi_(V_i)-chi_(W_i).                   (6.4)
```

Let O and I be the outer and inner five-cycles of P. Equations (6.1) and (6.4) give the signed integer combination

```
w = chi_O+chi_I + sum_i (chi_(U_i)+chi_(V_i)-chi_(W_i)).           (6.5)
```

Thus w belongs to the **integer lattice generated by actual simple-cycle vectors**, not merely to a parity relaxation of that lattice.

### 6.4 No nonnegative integer circuit decomposition exists

Suppose w were a nonnegative integer combination of simple-cycle vectors of Q. All coefficients on cycles meeting N would vanish, since w is zero there. At a vertex of P, the incident spoke has load two and the two nonspokes each have load one.

Each occurrence of a simple cycle using the spoke must use another incident edge. There are two such spoke occurrences, and only two nonspoke incidences available. Therefore every used cycle must pair a spoke with a nonspoke at every one of its vertices. All used cycles are M-alternating simple cycles of P.

There are exactly five of these, namely the eight-cycles in (6.2). To verify this globally, classify the perfect matchings of P. A perfect matching has an odd number of spokes. Five spokes gives M. Three spokes are impossible: the two remaining indices would have to be adjacent both on the outer five-cycle, where their difference is `+/-1`, and on the inner five-cycle, where it is `+/-2`. With one spoke, each of the remaining outer and inner four-vertex paths has a forced perfect matching. This gives exactly five more perfect matchings `M_i`.

An M-alternating simple cycle toggles M to a different perfect matching. Each `M_i` differs from M on exactly eight edges; these are precisely `A_i`. Hence every circuit used by a purported integer decomposition has eight edges. But its total edge multiplicity would be 20, which is not a multiple of eight. Contradiction. □

Writing `X_Q={chi_C:C a simple cycle of Q}`, we have proved

```
w in cone(X_Q) intersect lattice(X_Q),       w notin monoid(X_Q). (6.6)
```

This disproves generic unsigned circuit normality even when the matroid is regular, graphic, simple, and Eulerian.

### 6.5 The exact five-ray geometry: an index-two face

The obstruction is visible on a face, without relying only on a search for one failed partition. For each vertex v of Q define the valid circuit-cone inequality

```
a_v(x)=sum_{e incident with v, e notin M} x_e - x_(spoke at v) >= 0.
```

On any simple cycle this slack is either zero or two. Intersect the tight faces `a_v=0` with the faces `x_e=0` for `e in N`. The only circuit generators on the resulting face K are the five `chi_(A_i)` by the preceding alternating-cycle classification.

For a coefficient vector `lambda in R^5`, the ten F-coordinates of `sum_i lambda_i chi_(A_i)` are exactly all ten pair sums

```
lambda_i+lambda_j,       0<=i<j<=4.                               (6.7)
```

Indeed, an outer edge gives a pair of nonadjacent indices and an inner edge a pair of adjacent indices, once each. The spoke at index i has coordinate `sum_(j!=i) lambda_j`. In particular the five generators are linearly independent.

All coordinates are integers if and only if

```
lambda in Z^5        or        lambda in Z^5+(1/2)1.             (6.8)
```

Necessity follows from the pair sums: the differences of any two coefficients are integral, and twice every coefficient is integral. Both cases suffice, including the spoke coordinates, which sum four coefficients.

Equation (6.5) shows that the half-integral coset is in the FULL circuit lattice. Therefore the lattice on this five-dimensional face has index two over the lattice generated by its five ray vectors. Its exact holes are

```
w + sum_i z_i chi_(A_i),           z_i in Z_(>=0).                 (6.9)
```

For a point on the face, a nonnegative combination cannot use an off-face circuit because all defining slacks are nonnegative. An integral **nonnegative circuit representation** of such a point must therefore have nonnegative integer coefficients on its independent five rays. This restriction does not apply to signed lattice combinations such as (6.5). Conversely every point from the integral coset with nonnegative coefficients is generated. This proves the full face description (6.9).

The absolute-value operation from signed circuits to unsigned incidence vectors has lost crucial lattice information. TU of A cannot recover it.

### 6.6 Why this is NOT a unit-rounding counterexample

The very same Q has the two Hamilton cycles

```
(0,1,2,3,7,5,8,6,9,4),
(0,5,4,3,8,1,6,2,7,9),
```

whose edge sets partition `E(Q)`. Uniform price `1/10` is feasible for every simple cycle of Q, by the ten-vertex length bound, and has total value two. Hence

```
c(Q)=c_f(Q)=2,        r(Q)=9.                                    (6.10)
```

The hole w has nonunit coordinates, including zeros and twos. Equation (6.6) refutes the proposed *general cone-to-integer shortcut*, not the existence of an integral partition of `1_E`, and certainly not additive-rank rounding for it.

## 7. What remains for the graphic problem

The regular structure examined here separates three logically different assertions:

1. **Orientation feasibility:** a bounded kernel vector with some saturated signs can be completed to a unit sign vector. This is true by Lemma 2.1.
2. **Conformal integer feasibility:** an integral vector in a fixed orthant of a TU kernel decomposes into conformal signed circuits. This is true by Lemma 2.2.
3. **Objective control:** choose the orientation and the conformal integer decomposition so that the number of actual circuits is at most `c_f+C r`. This remains unproved here.

The first two assertions cannot be silently upgraded to the third. In the exact lift, a sufficient remaining theorem would bound the **single sum**

```
(mu_f(M)-c_f(M)) + (c(M)-mu_f(M)) <= C r(M)                       (7.1)
```

on the graphic class. Equation (7.1) is a precise accounting identity/target, **not a newly proved uniform bound**. The examples above show that neither summand can simply be set to zero, even in simple Eulerian regular matroids. They do not show that either summand must ever be superlinear in rank.

The cographic theorem does give (7.1) globally on that class, with no repeated rank payment. Its proof relies on cographic sparsity and does not extend to dense graphic matroids. The R10 calculation disposes of a fixed finite atom but does not supply a theorem about arbitrary interfaces. Invoking regular-matroid decomposition leaves both the unrestricted graphic atoms and any required cost-sensitive gluing analysis unresolved.

The unsigned cone hole shows that a more general Hilbert-basis assertion is false, not merely unavailable. Even replacing it by the true fixed-orthant integer-decomposition theorem gives only feasibility and the nullity-sized elementary count. Finally, none of this converts closed trails to simple cycles for free: every circuit in every certificate above is an actual minimal support (and every graphic circuit is a simple cycle).

**Exact conclusion:** an additive O(rank) result is proved for the cographic test class, and several regular/signed-cone shortcuts are resolved exactly. The arbitrary simple-even-graph bound `c(G)<=c_f(G)+C r(G)`, and therefore the main Erdős–Gallai task via the audited fractional theorem, remain unresolved by this work.
