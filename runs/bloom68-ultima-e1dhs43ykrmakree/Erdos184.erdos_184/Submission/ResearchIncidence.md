# Global incidence exchanges: signed ring defects and a packet-rank bound

## Status and main results

**The unrestricted Erdős–Gallai assertion is not proved here.** No absolute constant `C` with `c(G) <= C|V(G)|` for every finite simple even graph is established, and no counterexample to that assertion is obtained. The results below are paper-level proofs, not Lean formalizations or literature-priority claims. `Spec.lean` and all 19 preexisting Submission files were preserved.

There are two positive structural theorems, sharp tests of the new exchange, and two global barriers.

1. **An arbitrary-ring exchange, allowing multiple intersections.** For `s>=3` old cycles on a Berge ring, two macro *trails* have an exactly computable collision cost

   ```
   B + f(z),
   B = sum_v (t_v-2)_+,
   ```

   where `t_v` is the number of selected old cycles through v and `f(z)` counts violated signed not-all-equal constraints on the arc-choice bits. If `mu=min_z f(z)`, the union has a simple-cycle partition of size at most

   ```
   2 + B + mu.                                                 (A)
   ```

   Consequently a **global minimum** partition must satisfy `B+mu>=s-2` on every such ring. There is also a block correction: for every choice z, subtract the number of non-cycle blocks of the two macrographs. In a tight minimum-partition application, both macrographs must be cacti. For multiplicity at most two, the constraints are signed XOR equations; consistency gives two simple replacement cycles despite arbitrarily many pairwise intersections. A weighted component-balancing version is proved as well.

2. **A global packet-rank bound that charges coincident supports once.** Group old cycles into connected, two-port-complete packets P with supports `S_P`. Suppose

   ```
   2|P| <= gamma (|S_P|-1).
   ```

   Let Q join two packets when their supports intersect in at least two vertices, and let `r(G)=n-kappa(G)`. If the old partition has no clean-ring improvement—in particular, if it is globally minimum—then

   ```
   |D| <= (gamma/2) chi_f(Q) r(G).                              (B)
   ```

   Here `chi_f` is fractional chromatic number. This is a proved, nonuniform structural bound, not an assertion that its parameter is universally bounded. For the canonical packets consisting of all old cycles with the **same vertex set**, `gamma=1` and the port condition is automatic. Thus a minimum partition whose distinct-support overlap graph is b-colourable has at most `b r(G)/2` cycles, with no bound on packet multiplicities or on the number of overlap components. In the linear-support case, global optimality is characterized exactly by a packet-incidence forest, and `c_f=c=|D|<=r(G)/2`.

3. **Multiple intersections can be genuinely frustrated in an optimum.** A 13-vertex, 19-edge simple even graph has a globally minimum three-cycle partition, a three-cycle Berge ring, no vertex on three old cycles, and `mu=1`. Thus `(A)` is sharp with `B=0`. A separate 10-vertex example is sharp with `B=1, mu=0`. These are global lower bounds, not bounded-exchange experiments.

4. **A laminar, locally paid hierarchy can still cost `Theta(n log n)`.** On `K_(3^h)` there is an explicit partition into `h 3^(h-1)` cycles whose supports are laminar. It has no clean ring, chordal-bipartite incidence, and incidence cycle space generated by 4-cycles. Every equal-support packet is itself optimally Hamilton-decomposed, with zero fractional gap. Nevertheless the whole graph has an optimal `(3^h-1)/2` Hamilton partition. Charging those packets separately loses a factor h. In fact the branching term of `(A)` is already at least `3(s-2)` on **every** Berge ring in a laminar family, so this collision certificate cannot perform the missing dense-packet amalgamation.

5. **Incidence and lengths do not determine global exchange cost.** For every `t>=2`, two explicit simple 4-regular graphs on `5t` vertices have supplied partitions with exactly the same vertex–cycle incidence matrix and length multiset: t disjoint 5-cycle supports and one spanning support. One supplied partition is globally minimum, of size `t+1`; the other graph has an explicit two-Hamilton-cycle partition. Their optimum costs have unbounded ratio. A proved two-slot insertion rule constructs the latter global recombination. The difference is cyclic order, not incidence rank.

The exact remaining gap is stated in Section 9. In particular, no rounding is inferred from the audited `c_f(G)<=r(G)` in `ResearchFractional.md`; no degree-bounded exchange or forgetful rainbow iteration is being used as a substitute for global optimality.

## 0. Conventions, heredity, and the direction of incidence rank

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Even graphs may have isolated vertices. A cycle is simple, of length at least three; a cycle partition is an exact **edge** partition. Set

```
c(G) = minimum size of a cycle partition,
r(G) = |V(G)| - kappa(G),
beta(G) = |E(G)| - |V(G)| + kappa(G).
```

The empty graph has cost zero. The elementary existence of a cycle partition follows by repeatedly deleting a simple cycle from a nonempty even graph. A nonempty even component contains a cycle, deletion preserves evenness, and the edge count decreases.

**Hereditary optimality.** If D is globally minimum and `A subseteq D`, then A is globally minimum on its edge union. Otherwise replace A by a smaller partition and leave `D\A` untouched. No bound on `|A|` appears in this observation. It is stronger than optimality under any fixed-size or fixed-degree exchange.

Let `I_D` be the bipartite graph with one node for each old cycle and one node for each original vertex, including isolated vertices. Join C to v exactly when `v in V(C)`. Since the old cycles partition the edges,

```
|E(I_D)| = sum_C |C| = |E(G)|,
|V(I_D)| = n+|D|,
kappa(I_D) = kappa(G).
```

Hence

```
beta(I_D) = beta(G)-|D|.                                      (0.1)
```

Thus **minimizing the number of old cycles maximizes incidence nullity**. Eliminating all incidence cycles is not a plausible global potential: a successful clean-ring replacement creates multiple intersections and increases `beta(I_D)`. Equation (0.1) is an accounting identity, not a solution of the counting problem.

Also distinguish `beta(I_D)` from the rank of the `n by |D|` zero-one **vertex–cycle matrix**. They are different invariants. Section 6 supplies failures of the corresponding shortcuts.

### A forest observation used below

For any cycle partition, if its incidence graph is a forest, its union is a cactus: each edge-containing block is one of the old cycles. One way to see this is to root each incidence tree. An outermost cycle can attach to the remaining cycles at only its parent vertex; a simple cycle cannot cross that articulation and return without repeating it. Remove outermost cycles successively.

Conversely, the cycle blocks of a cactus have forest incidence. In particular their edge partition is forced. This observation needs no optimality assumption.

For a globally minimum partition with pairwise intersections of size at most one, a shortest incidence cycle, if present, gives a clean ring of length at least three. The two-arc replacement contradicts optimality. Therefore its incidence is a forest, and

```
sum_C (|C|-1) = r(G),       |D| <= r(G)/2.                     (0.2)
```

For a nonempty graph this is strictly less than `n/2`. Such a minimum partition can occur only in a cactus. The new issues are therefore genuinely the multiple intersections, not a failure of the forest calculation.

## 1. An exact signed collision formula for arbitrary Berge rings

### 1.1 Ring data and the two macro trails

Choose **distinct** old cycles `C_1,...,C_s`, `s>=3`, and distinct vertices `x_1,...,x_s`, with

```
x_i in C_i intersect C_(i+1),                               (1.1)
```

where indices are cyclic. There are no other intersection restrictions. A connector may also lie on additional selected cycles. A pair of selected cycles may have arbitrarily many common vertices.

Cut `C_i` at `x_(i-1),x_i` into its two positive-length paths `A_i^0,A_i^1`, both regarded as running from `x_(i-1)` to `x_i`. For bits `z_i in {0,1}`, concatenate

```
T_0(z): A_1^(z_1),...,A_s^(z_s),
T_1(z): A_1^(1-z_1),...,A_s^(1-z_s).                         (1.2)
```

These are connected, nonempty, edge-simple closed trails. They partition all selected edges, but need not be simple cycles.

For every vertex in the selected union put

```
t_v = |{i : v in C_i}|,
B   = sum_v max(t_v-2,0).                                   (1.3)
```

At a nonconnector v, its occurrence in `C_i` lies internally on exactly one of the arcs. Write this arc label as `a_i(v)`. The occurrence goes to `T_0` precisely when `z_i=a_i(v)`. For a nonconnector with `t_v>=2`, impose the signed not-all-equal constraint

```
{ z_i XOR a_i(v) : v in C_i } is not constant.                (1.4)
```

Let `f(z)` count the failed constraints, and put `mu=min_z f(z)`. A vertex is counted once here, regardless of how large `t_v` is. Relabelling the two arcs of any old cycle just changes the corresponding bit variable; the minimum is invariant.

### Theorem 1.1 — ring exchange with exact collision cost

Let `H=union_i C_i`. Then

```
c(H) <= 2+B+mu.                                             (1.5)
```

More precisely, for any choice z the total repeat-visit excess of the two trails is exactly `B+f(z)`.

**Proof.** For a connected closed trail T, let `m_T(v)` be its number of passages through v, and define

```
D(T)=sum_(v used by T) (m_T(v)-1).
```

Across the two trails, the total number of passages at v is `t_v`.

* If v is a connector, each trail already has one passage there, formed by the two adjacent old-cycle arcs. The other `t_v-2` occurrences contribute exactly `t_v-2` excess passages.
* If v is not a connector and `t_v=1`, its excess is zero.
* If v is not a connector and `t_v>=2`, the excess is `t_v-2` if both trails use it, and `t_v-1` if only one does. The latter event is exactly failure of (1.4).

Consequently

```
D(T_0)+D(T_1)=B+f(z).                                      (1.6)
```

Now split a closed trail at two occurrences of a repeated vertex. The number of trails increases by one. The total excess decreases by one at that vertex, and also by one at every other vertex used on both resulting trails. Thus it decreases by at least one. Starting with T, at most `D(T)` splits are needed before every trail is a simple cycle. All resulting trails are nonempty and edge-simple; simplicity of G excludes lengths one and two. Hence T has a partition into at most `1+D(T)` simple cycles. Apply this to both trails and minimize over z. ∎

This is an **upper bound**, not a claim that the best partition of H has exactly `2+B+mu` cycles. Richly intersecting macrographs can decompose much more cheaply than their repeat-visit ledger.

### Corollary 1.2 — a global-optimum obstruction

If the selected cycles belong to a global minimum partition, then

```
B+mu >= s-2.                                               (1.7)
```

Indeed hereditary optimality gives `c(H)=s`, and (1.5) supplies the opposite bound. Thus any ring with `B+mu<s-2` gives a genuine strict decrease of the primary count. The ring size is unrestricted. Accepting only such count-decreasing exchanges cannot produce the period-two behavior in `ResearchOrientation.md`; the missing issue is whether enough exchanges exist.

The **shape** of this operation remains restricted: it cuts each participating old cycle at only two vertices. It is not a completeness theorem for all global recombinations.

### Corollary 1.3 — an explicit incidence inequality

Choose the bits independently and uniformly. At a nonconnector with multiplicity t, all its literals coincide with probability `2^(1-t)`. Therefore

```
mu <= sum_(v not a connector, t_v>=2) 2^(1-t_v),             (1.8)
```

and the right side plus B is at least `s-2` in a global optimum. If all selected multiplicities are at most two, and p is the number of extra shared vertices other than the s connectors, this gives

```
mu <= floor(p/2),                 p >= 2s-4                 (1.9)
```

for a ring in a minimum partition. This is a necessary structural bound, not a bound on the total number of cycles of G.

## 2. A block correction and the consistent-sign case

### 2.1 A useful strictness test at the collision threshold

For an even graph J, let `eta(J)` be the number of edge-containing blocks that are not single cycles. Then **every** cycle partition of J has at most

```
beta(J)-eta(J)                                             (2.1)
```

cycles.

**Proof.** Every simple cycle lies in one block. A cycle partition consequently also shows that every edge-containing block is even. In a block K that is not a single cycle, the incidence of any cycle partition cannot be a forest: otherwise K would be a cactus, and its 2-connectedness would make it one cycle. Its incidence nullity is at least one. Applying (0.1) inside K gives at most `beta(K)-1` cycles. A cycle block contributes one, which equals its nullity. Nullities add over the block-cut forest, proving (2.1). ∎

Let `R_j(z)` be the actual edge graph of `T_j(z)`, and set

```
eta(z)=eta(R_0(z))+eta(R_1(z)).
```

Since each macrograph is connected,

```
beta(R_j)=1+D(T_j).
```

Combining (1.6) and (2.1), for every z,

```
c(H) <= 2+B+f(z)-eta(z).                                  (2.2)
```

In a minimum partition this implies

```
B+f(z)-eta(z) >= s-2       for EVERY z.                    (2.3)
```

In particular, if `B+mu=s-2`, then **both macrographs are cacti for every assignment attaining mu**. If either had a non-cycle block, (2.2) would improve the count. This is an additional structural restriction, not just the inequality (1.7) restated.

We will also use a bound in the other direction on this correction. A non-cycle even block has nullity at least three: its partition has at least two cycles and positive incidence nullity. If a connected nonempty even J has b blocks, then

```
beta(J) >= b+2 eta(J),       2 eta(J) <= beta(J)-1.          (2.4)
```

### 2.2 Multiple intersections without frustration

Suppose `B=0`, so every selected vertex lies on at most two old cycles. At every extra shared vertex belonging to `C_i,C_j`, (1.4) is the XOR equation

```
z_i XOR z_j = 1 XOR a_i(v) XOR a_j(v).                     (2.5)
```

Make a signed multigraph F on the selected cycles with one labelled edge for each such vertex. Parallel constraints are retained. It is consistent exactly when the XOR sum of the labels around every closed walk is zero; this equivalence follows by propagating labels along a spanning forest and checking the remaining edges.

If consistent, take a solution. Equations (1.6) and `B=f=0` say each macro trail visits every one of its vertices just once. Thus it gives **two simple cycles**, despite the multiple intersections. In particular, every ring with `B=0` and consistent F is forbidden in a global minimum partition.

No cyclic-order compatibility has been silently assumed. Inconsistent parallel equations and inconsistent longer signed cycles really occur.

### Theorem 2.1 — weighted component balancing

Give edges arbitrary nonnegative additive weights. Suppose `B=mu=0`. Let the connected components of F, including isolated cycle nodes, be `K_1,...,K_a`. Fix one satisfying assignment and define

```
delta_j = sum_(i in K_j) [w(A_i^(z_i))-w(A_i^(1-z_i))],
T       = sum_i w(C_i).
```

There is a two-simple-cycle replacement `Q_0,Q_1` with

```
min(w(Q_0),w(Q_1)) >= (T-max_j |delta_j|)/2
                    >= (T-max_j sum_(i in K_j) w(C_i))/2.  (2.6)
```

**Proof.** Flipping every bit in one component preserves all equations and reverses the sign of its `delta_j`. Choose the component flips greedily, assigning the larger of that component's two arc totals to the currently smaller global total. If the current discrepancy is d, the next becomes `|d-|delta_j||`. Induction bounds the final discrepancy by `max_j |delta_j|`. The total weight is T, proving the first bound. Nonnegativity gives the second. ∎

For a clean ring every component is a single cycle, recovering the balancing lemma in `ResearchHeavy.md`. In the presence of multiple intersections, an entire component may have to flip together. A single large component prevents the old shortest-weight argument from being applied cycle by cycle. The cardinality improvement remains valid when the replacement has only two cycles.

### Realizability of the signatures

There is no automatic consistency theorem for F. Given any signed loopless multigraph on `s>=3` cycle labels, introduce s distinct ring connectors and one extra vertex for each signed edge. Put the extra vertex on the chosen arc of each of its two incident old cycles so that (2.5) has the desired label. Order the interior vertices on each arc arbitrarily and subdivide **every** segment with a private vertex. The resulting cycles are simple and edge-disjoint, the extra vertices have multiplicity two, and the prescribed constraints are realized exactly. The same construction realizes higher-arity signed NAE constraints. Thus the cyclic-order information in (1.4) is real data, not something that can be dropped from the proof.

## 3. Sharp global optima for the two defect terms

### 3.1 Pure frustration: `B=0, mu=1, c=3`

Take junctions `w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3`, indexed modulo four. Between `w_i,w_(i+1)` put two internally disjoint length-two paths through private vertices `a_i,b_i`. Let

```
C_0=(w_0,a_0,w_1,a_1,w_2,a_2,w_3,a_3),
C_1=(w_0,b_0,w_1,b_1,w_2,b_2,w_3,b_3).
```

Set `p=a_0`, `q=b_2`, add a new vertex z, and add the triangle

```
C_2=(p,q,z).
```

This is a simple even graph on 13 vertices and 19 edges. The junctions and p,q have degree four; all remaining vertices have degree two. The displayed three cycles partition it.

**Global lower bound three.** Suppose two simple cycles partitioned the graph. Both must visit every degree-four vertex. The edges pz and zq belong to one of the two cycles. If that cycle also used pq, it would be precisely the triangle, missing the junctions. Therefore pq belongs to the other cycle. Removing the two p–q routes of the triangle leaves two edge-disjoint simple p–q paths in `C_0 union C_1`, each visiting all four junctions.

Project either path onto the base four-cycle of junctions. Its first junction is `w_0` or `w_1`, and its last is `w_2` or `w_3`. Because it visits every junction once, the projection is a Hamilton path of the four-cycle. It must use the link `w_0 w_1`: the only spanning path omitting that link has endpoints `w_0,w_1`, which are not the possible pair of endpoint classes here. But the only whole `w_0`–`w_1` branch available internally to such a p–q path is the one through `b_0`; the other contains its endpoint p. Both projected paths would therefore use the two edges through `b_0`, contradicting edge-disjointness. Hence `c=3`.

Use the Berge ring with connectors

```
C_0--w_0--C_1--q--C_2--p--C_0.
```

The only extra shared vertices are `w_1,w_2,w_3`, all between `C_0,C_1`. Relative to the p–`w_0` cut of `C_0`, they lie on the same arc. Relative to the `w_0`–q cut of `C_1`, `w_1,w_2` lie on one arc and `w_3` on the other. Thus two equations have one XOR label and the third has the opposite label. Exactly one must fail at best:

```
B=0,       mu=1,       c=2+B+mu=3.                         (3.1)
```

The same calculation holds with any of the four junctions as the `C_0,C_1` connector. At minimizing assignments the macrographs are cacti, in agreement with Section 2. The checker independently enumerates all 53 simple cycles and certifies the optimum three.

This explicitly disproves extending the clean-ring conclusion to arbitrary multiple intersections, even when no vertex belongs to three selected old cycles.

### 3.2 Pure branching: `B=1, mu=0, c=3`

Take distinct connectors `x_0,x_1,x_2`, a vertex v, and six private vertices `a_i,b_i`. Use

```
C_i=(v,a_i,x_(i-1),x_i,b_i),       i mod 3.
```

These three 5-cycles are edge-disjoint in a simple graph on ten vertices. At v the degree is six, so any partition has at least three cycles. The three connector multiplicities are two, and v is the sole additional shared vertex, of multiplicity three. Hence `B=1`. Its one NAE constraint can be satisfied, so `mu=0`. The old partition is globally minimum and again equality holds in (1.5).

These examples isolate the two different difficulties: more than two visits can be forced by degree, whereas a multiplicity-two ring can be blocked purely by incompatible cyclic order.

## 4. A global rank ledger for overlap packets

This section gives an actual structural count theorem. Its hypotheses are explicit; the missing universal bound on its parameter is not assumed.

### 4.1 Definitions and elementary payment certificates

Partition the old cycles into nonempty packets P. Let

```
H_P = union_(C in P) C,       S_P=V(H_P),       k_P=|P|.
```

Assume `H_P` is connected. Its boundary is

```
partial P = S_P intersect union_(R != P) S_R.
```

Call P **two-port-complete** if for every two distinct `x,y in partial P`, some **old cycle of P** contains both x and y. This is vacuous when the boundary has size at most one. It is not enough that some cycle of `H_P` contains x,y; that cycle may not occur in any minimum partition.

Suppose there is a common number gamma such that

```
2 k_P <= gamma (|S_P|-1)        for every P.                (4.1)
```

Useful direct certificates include:

* If all cycles in P pass through one common vertex v, then `2k_P=d_(H_P)(v)<=|S_P|-1`, so `gamma=1` suffices. Cycle lengths need not be comparable.
* If their average length is at least `alpha |S_P|`, then

  ```
  alpha |S_P| k_P <= |E(H_P)| <= |S_P|(|S_P|-1)/2,
  ```

  so `gamma=1/alpha` suffices. In particular this holds if each cycle has that length lower bound.
* For the **canonical equal-support packets**, group all cycles having exactly the same vertex set S. Every member is a Hamilton cycle of the packet graph. The packet is connected, two-port-complete, and `gamma=1` suffices. Its multiplicity can be as large as `(s-1)/2`; it is not treated as that many separate support nodes.

For a nonhomogeneous example, pair `2k` internally disjoint u–v paths into k old cycles. They form a common-pole packet. If its foreign attachments are confined to u,v, it is two-port-complete, although its old cycles generally have different vertex sets. It has cost k, certified by the degree `2k` at a pole. Such a packet can be attached to other packets at its poles in an incidence forest. In the length-two-path example `K_(2,2k)`, treating its old 4-cycles as separate canonical packets would give an overlap k-clique; grouping the common-pole packet removes that artificial factor. The checker includes this example with separate cycle attachments at both poles.

Define the packet multiple-overlap graph Q by

```
P R in E(Q)  iff  |S_P intersect S_R|>=2.                   (4.2)
```

For equal-support packets its nodes are the distinct old vertex sets. Strict containment counts as an overlap edge, as does an incomparable multiple intersection.

A clean ring means s old cycles with exactly the s prescribed distinct singleton intersections and no other pairwise intersections. Call D **clean-ring-stable** if it has no clean ring with `s>=3`. Every minimum partition is clean-ring-stable, but Section 6 shows the converse is very false in general.

### Theorem 4.1 — independent packets have a forest rank budget

Suppose D is clean-ring-stable and the packets are two-port-complete. If I is an independent set of Q, then its packet–vertex incidence graph is a forest. Including all original graph vertices,

```
sum_(P in I) (|S_P|-1) = n-kappa(I_incidence) <= r(G).       (4.3)
```

**Proof.** The supports of packets in I pairwise meet in at most one vertex. Suppose their incidence graph contained a cycle and choose a shortest one. It uses `s>=3` distinct packets and distinct connectors. Its selected supports have no extra intersections: an extra intersection of consecutive supports would give a 4-cycle, and an extra intersection or incidence chord between nonconsecutive selected supports would give a shorter cycle. Thus the selected packet supports form a clean ring.

At each selected packet, its two connectors are boundary vertices. Choose an old cycle in that packet containing both, using two-port completeness. The chosen old cycles form a clean ring as well: they contain the connectors and are subsets of supports with no other intersections. Their two-arc exchange contradicts clean-ring stability. Thus the packet incidence is a forest.

Its number of edges is `sum |S_P|`, and its number of nodes is `n+|I|`; the forest identity proves the equality. Since each connected packet lies inside one component of G, an incidence component cannot connect different components of G. Its number of components is at least `kappa(G)`, proving the inequality. ∎

This uses rings of arbitrary size. No local-exchange radius is hidden in the proof.

### Theorem 4.2 — fractional-colouring packet bound

Under the hypotheses above and (4.1),

```
|D| <= (gamma/2) chi_f(Q) r(G).                            (4.4)
```

**Proof.** For each independent I, (4.1) and (4.3) give

```
sum_(P in I) k_P <= (gamma/2) r(G).                        (4.5)
```

A fractional colouring assigns nonnegative numbers `lambda_I` to the finitely many independent sets so that `sum_(I containing P) lambda_I>=1` for each packet. Multiply (4.5) by `lambda_I` and sum. Since all `k_P` are nonnegative,

```
|D|=sum_P k_P
    <= sum_I lambda_I sum_(P in I) k_P
    <= (gamma/2) r(G) sum_I lambda_I.
```

Minimizing the last sum proves (4.4). Ordinary b-colourability gives the simpler bound `|D|<=gamma b r(G)/2`. ∎

**What is genuinely paid once.** Within one colour class the entire support-rank sum is at most `r(G)`, not one copy of r for each packet or overlap component. Colours can be reused in different components of Q. Thus if every component of Q is b-colourable, the bound uses b, not the number or total order of those components. For equal-support packets, all coincident Hamilton cycles are paid by their one support charge. In particular:

```
Q edgeless:   |D|<=r(G)/2;
Q bipartite:  |D|<=r(G);
chi_f(Q)<=b:  |D|<=b r(G)/2.                               (4.6)
```

These are unconditional conclusions for minimum partitions satisfying the stated structural conditions. A universal bound on **canonical** `chi_f(Q)` is actually false: in `K_(2,2k)` every simple cycle has length four, every partition has k cycles, and their distinct supports all share the two poles. The private degree-two vertices occur in disjoint pairs, so the canonical overlap graph is `K_k` in every partition, including every optimum. Its fractional chromatic number is k. The coarser common-pole packet has `gamma=1` and a one-node overlap graph. Thus nonidentical supports sometimes must be grouped even in a true optimum; bounded chromatic number of the canonical supports is not the missing lemma one may simply assume.

### 4.2 Exact characterization in the homogeneous linear-support class

Suppose every packet is an equal-support packet, and distinct supports meet in at most one vertex. Then the following are equivalent:

1. D is globally minimum;
2. D is clean-ring-stable;
3. the packet–vertex incidence graph is a forest.

Moreover, in this case

```
c_f(G)=c(G)=|D|<=r(G)/2.                                  (4.7)
```

**Proof.** Minimum implies clean-ring stability, and Theorem 4.1 gives forest incidence. Conversely, forest incidence makes every simple cycle of G lie wholly in one packet graph: a cycle using different packets would have to cross a separating attachment vertex and revisit it. This can also be proved by successively removing leaves of the packet-incidence forest, as in Section 0.

Inside packet P, all old cycles are spanning, so every vertex has degree `2k_P`. Hence its cycle count is at least `k_P`, attained by the old cycles. Since all graph cycles are packet-local, these lower bounds add and prove global optimality.

There is also an explicit exact fractional certificate, without any rounding theorem. Put `y_e=1/|S_P|` on every edge of packet P. Every simple graph cycle lies in one packet, so its y-weight is at most one. But

```
y(E(G))=sum_P |E(H_P)|/|S_P|=sum_P k_P=|D|.
```

Weak duality for an exact fractional cycle partition gives `c_f>=|D|`; the displayed integral partition gives the opposite inequality. Finally use (4.1) with `gamma=1` and the forest rank identity. ∎

This covers arbitrarily dense Hamilton packets attached at articulations, not just cactus graphs. It also supplies a concrete signed-dual repair—in fact a nonnegative certificate—on this class, rather than invoking the general fractional value bound and assuming it rounds.

## 5. Two-port completeness is not supplied by 2-connectivity or optimality

The following is a direct port consequence of the transition example in Section 8 of `ResearchInterfaces.md`; the short proof is included so that the packet hypothesis is auditable here.

Take `k>=2`, junctions `w_0,...,w_k` in cyclic order, and two private length-two branches on every link of that `(k+1)`-cycle. Call the graph `T_k`. It is simple, even, and 2-connected. There is a partition into two long cycles, each choosing one branch per link. Degree four at a junction proves

```
c(T_k)=2.                                                 (5.1)
```

Choose the two private vertices p,q on the same link `w_0w_1`. Every simple cycle in `T_k` is either the local four-cycle on one link or a long cycle selecting one branch on every link. This classification follows by following the used links on the base junction cycle; once a cycle leaves a link, it cannot turn back without repeating a junction.

The only simple cycle containing **both** p and q is the local four-cycle on their link. Removing it leaves a chain of k four-cycle blocks, all forced. Therefore

```
minimum cost with p and q on one old cycle = k+1.           (5.2)
```

A 2-connected packet can thus have a cycle through any two given vertices, yet forcing even one specified pair to co-occur in a partition can increase its minimum cost by an unbounded amount. It is invalid to manufacture the port condition by minimum-preserving local reoptimization without proof. The checker verifies (5.1)–(5.2) by exact cover for `2<=k<=6`.

## 6. A laminar logarithmic hierarchy and its global repair

This construction rules out several superficially global incidence criteria at once. It does **not** produce minimum partitions of superlinear size.

### 6.1 A complete tripartite Hamilton packet

For any odd s, label three classes by `A_i,B_i,C_i`, `i in Z_s`. For each `t in Z_s`, take the cycle

```
(A_i, B_(i+t), C_(i+2t))   concatenated for i=0,...,s-1.      (6.1)
```

It is one Hamilton cycle of length `3s`: each class is visited once in a permuted order. These s cycles partition `K_(s,s,s)`.

For completeness, an AB edge determines t from its index difference, as does a BC edge. A CA edge in (6.1) goes from `C_(i+2t)` to `A_(i+1)`, so its index difference is `1-2t`. Since s is odd, multiplication by two is invertible modulo s, giving each CA edge exactly once. There are no edges within a class. This proves exact coverage. The graph is `2s`-regular, so s cycles are also globally minimum. Uniform edge weight `1/(3s)` proves its fractional optimum is s as well.

### 6.2 Recursive construction on `K_(3^h)`

Let `n=3^h`, `h>=1`, with vertices the strings of length h over `{0,1,2}`. For every internal node of the full ternary prefix tree, its vertex block S has three equal children of odd order s. Put the s Hamilton cycles (6.1) on the edges between its three children. Recurse inside the children.

Every pair of vertices belongs to exactly one such cross-edge packet, determined by the first position where their strings differ. Hence all packets together partition `K_n`. At each of the h levels, the number of old cycles is `n/3`. Thus

```
q_h=h n/3.                                                (6.2)
```

Every old support is one prefix block. Supports are nested or disjoint, and every nonempty pairwise intersection has at least three vertices. Consequently there is **no clean ring of any size**. Every individual equal-support packet is a globally minimum Hamilton decomposition of its own edge graph, with zero fractional gap.

### 6.3 Incidence and overlap properties

The following properties hold simultaneously.

* The ordinary multiple-intersection graph on all old cycles has just **one** component: a root Hamilton cycle meets every old cycle in at least three vertices.
* The incidence graph is **chordal bipartite**, meaning it has no induced cycle of length at least six. To prove this, choose a minimum-size support node on a proposed induced long cycle. Its two neighboring support nodes along that cycle both contain its support, by laminarity. Each therefore contains both adjacent connector vertices, giving an incidence chord.
* Its binary cycle space is generated by 4-cycles: a cycle longer than four has a chord; split along the chord and use induction. Thus filling all incidence squares leaves no one-dimensional cycle-space obstruction.
* The vertex–cycle matrix has rank exactly `n/3` **over every field**. The bottom triple supports are disjoint nonzero columns, hence independent. Every other support column is the sum of the bottom triple columns it contains. Therefore

  ```
  q_h / rank(vertex–cycle matrix) = h.                       (6.3)
  ```

* For canonical equal-support packets, Q is the comparability graph of the prefix tree's internal nodes: two distinct supports are adjacent exactly when one contains the other. Colour by depth to obtain h colours, and take a root-to-bottom chain to obtain an h-clique. Hence

  ```
  chi_f(Q)=chi(Q)=h.                                       (6.4)
  ```

Thus no-clean-ring stability, one overlap component, local packet optimality, square-generated incidence cycle space, and laminar incidence **together** do not give a linear cycle count for the supplied partition.

### 6.4 Why the new collision certificate also needs packet amalgamation

There is a useful exact observation for **any** laminar cycle family, not just (6.2).

Take s cycles on a Berge ring. In a connected laminar intersection family, a largest support contains all other supports. Delete that cycle node from the ring: the remaining ring path still connects all other selected cycles. Hence a second selected support contains all the remaining `s-2` supports. Denote these two largest supports by `S_1,S_2`, with `S_2 subseteq S_1`. At vertices of `S_2`, both large cycles contribute, and outside `S_2` only the first can contribute. Therefore the branching term is exactly

```
B = sum_(i other than the two largest) |C_i| >= 3(s-2).       (6.5)
```

So the upper-bound certificate `2+B+mu` is already too large to certify a reduction, irrespective of the signs. Even the correction (2.2) cannot certify one: by (2.4), `eta(z)<= [B+f(z)]/2`, and consequently its right side is at least

```
2 + ceil(3(s-2)/2) > s.                                   (6.6)
```

This is a limitation of the **proved collision upper bound**, not a lower bound on the true cost of the two macrographs. Those macrographs may be cheaply factorable. Indeed, repeatedly charging all repeat visits at a densely coincident vertex set is precisely the accounting mistake that must be avoided.

The packet theorem makes this obstruction quantitative in a different way. Since (6.2) is clean-ring-stable, for **any** connected, two-port-complete packet grouping satisfying (4.1), Theorem 4.2 implies

```
gamma chi_f(Q) >= 2h n/[3(n-1)].                           (6.7)
```

Thus no regrouping of the unchanged old cycles into packets of that kind can have a uniformly bounded payment-times-colouring parameter. A genuine change of the cycle partition is required.

### 6.5 Explicit global optimum and rank warning

Here is a self-contained Hamilton decomposition of `K_(2d+1)`. Label the vertices by infinity and `Z_(2d)`. For `t=0,...,d-1`, use

```
(infinity, t, t-1, t+1, t-2, t+2, ...,
 t-(d-1), t+(d-1), t-d),                                  (6.8)
```

with finite labels modulo `2d`.

Each is spanning and simple. Its infinity-neighbors are t and `t-d`, which cover the finite vertices exactly once over the d cycles. The successive finite edges have alternating endpoint sums `2t-1` and `2t` modulo `2d`. The odd-sum class contains d unordered pairs, all present; the even-sum class contains `d-1` nonloop pairs, all present. As t varies, every sum class occurs once. Thus the cycles partition all edges.

Degree `2d` proves the optimum is d. Applying (6.8) to `n=3^h` gives

```
c(K_n)=c_f(K_n)=(n-1)/2,                                  (6.9)
```

where the fractional lower bound is also supplied by uniform edge weight `1/n`.

The minimum Hamilton partition itself has vertex–cycle matrix rank **one**, but `(n-1)/2` columns. Therefore no constant-times-vertex-incidence-rank bound holds even for true minimum partitions. The hierarchy (6.3) and the optimum rank-one partition fail rank shortcuts in different ways; neither should be confused with the nullity identity (0.1).

### 6.6 Signed dual repair after insufficient optimization can lose a logarithm

In the hierarchical partition give every edge of an old cycle length l price `1/l`. Every old cycle is tight and the total price is `q_h`. In the Hamilton partition (6.8), the average cycle price is

```
q_h / ((n-1)/2) = 2h n/[3(n-1)].                           (6.10)
```

For `h>=2`, some Hamilton cycle therefore has price at least (6.10), an excess of at least `2h n/[3(n-1)]-1` over the allowed value one. More generally, every feasible signed cycle dual has total value at most `(n-1)/2`, simply by summing its inequalities over (6.8). **Any** repair from the old-tight total must therefore lose at least

```
q_h-(n-1)/2 = Theta(n log n).
```

This rules out an additive O(n) signed-dual repair theorem whose hypothesis is merely no clean rings, laminar incidence, or separately optimal packets. It does **not** rule out such a repair from a genuinely global minimum partition. The distinction in the quantifiers is essential.

## 7. Identical incidence, unboundedly different global optimum

This section isolates cyclic-order information that even the full vertex–cycle matrix omits. It also proves a real global recombination rule for a nontrivial family.

### Theorem 7.1

For every `t>=2`, there are connected simple 4-regular graphs `G_t^-`, `G_t^+` on the same `5t` labelled vertices, with supplied cycle partitions `D_t^-`, `D_t^+`, such that:

* their vertex–cycle matrices agree entry by entry after identifying the old cycle labels;
* both partitions consist of t cycles on disjoint five-vertex blocks and one spanning cycle;
* `c(G_t^-)=t+1`, so `D_t^-` is globally minimum;
* `c(G_t^+)=2`, witnessed by two explicitly constructed Hamilton cycles.

In particular the supplied partitions have the same length multiset, original-degree weight multiset, and all support-intersection data, while the optimum costs have ratio `(t+1)/2`.

### 7.1 The globally minimum construction

Let `F_i` be a 5-cycle on a private block `S_i`. Its complement inside `K_5` is another 5-cycle. Delete one edge of that complement, obtaining a spanning path `P_i` with distinct endpoints. Concatenate the paths `P_1,...,P_t` cyclically by adding one edge between successive blocks. The resulting R is a Hamilton cycle, edge-disjoint from every `F_i`. Let

```
D_t^-={F_1,...,F_t,R}.
```

The graph is simple and 4-regular. Each block has nine internal edges and exactly two boundary edges.

In any cycle partition, a cycle using a boundary edge must use **all** boundary edges: its image is a nonempty even edge-subgraph of the cyclic quotient of the blocks. For `t=2` that quotient consists of two parallel edges; the conclusion is the same. There is consequently exactly one crossing cycle. It uses just one simple path in each five-vertex block, at most four internal edges. At least five internal edges remain in each block, and all of these are covered by cycles wholly within that block. At least one additional cycle is therefore required per block. This proves

```
c(G_t^-)>=t+1,
```

with equality supplied by `D_t^-`.

### 7.2 A two-slot insertion identity

On five new vertices `0,1,2,3,4`, let F be their 5-cycle in that order. The disjoint paths

```
P=(0,2,4),             Q=(1,3)
```

together with F have the following alternative partition into two spanning paths:

```
U=(0,1,3,2,4),         V=(1,2,0,4,3).                      (7.1)
```

Their endpoint pairs agree with P and Q, respectively. The edge identity is verified directly:

```
E(F) disjoint_union E(P) disjoint_union E(Q)
    = E(U) disjoint_union E(V).                            (7.2)
```

There are eight internal edges on either side.

Suppose a graph already has both

* an old partition consisting of one Hamilton cycle R and disjoint 5-cycles `F_i`; and
* a partition into two Hamilton cycles `H_1,H_2`.

Choose an edge `uv in R intersect H_1` and a different edge `wz in R intersect H_2`, oriented as encountered on R. In R, replace uv by `u,P,v` and wz by `w,Q,z`, and add F as one new old 5-cycle. In `H_1`, replace uv by `u,U,v`; in `H_2`, replace wz by `w,V,z`.

The new old R is Hamilton because P,Q are vertex-disjoint and together contain all five new vertices. Each new `H_i` is Hamilton because U,V each contain all five new vertices once. Identity (7.2), together with the four common external edges, proves that the two partitions have exactly the same edge set. All new vertices are private, so simplicity is preserved. External edges of the first insertion slot remain in `R intersect H_1`, and those of the second remain in `R intersect H_2`; hence the operation can be iterated arbitrarily many times.

### 7.3 Base case and the cheap construction

On vertices `0,...,4` start with

```
R =(0,1,2,3,4),            F_1=(0,2,4,1,3),
H_1=(0,1,3,2,4),          H_2=(0,2,1,4,3).
```

Each pair partitions `K_5`, and R has edges in both `H_1,H_2`. Apply the insertion `t-1` times on fresh five-vertex blocks. This gives `G_t^+`, an old partition with t disjoint 5-cycles and one spanning cycle, and two spanning replacement cycles. The degree is four, so the replacement is globally minimum:

```
c(G_t^+)=2.                                               (7.3)
```

Use exactly those five-vertex supports and old small-cycle labels in the construction of `G_t^-`. The old incidence matrices then agree literally. This completes the proof of Theorem 7.1. ∎

### 7.4 What the example does and does not exclude

For both supplied partitions, the vertex–cycle matrix has rank t over every field: the disjoint block columns are independent and the spanning column is their sum. Their distinct-support overlap graph is a star. Their incidence graph is a union of `K_(2,5)` blocks sharing the spanning-cycle node. **Every incidence cycle has length four**; there is no Berge ring using three or more old cycles.

Thus a very large global recombination can exist when the entire family of distinct-old-cycle ring exchanges is absent. A direct recombination into two cycles necessarily cuts the old spanning cycle at **at least `2t` distinct vertices**. Indeed, both new cycles are Hamilton because the graph is 4-regular. In each old five-vertex block, some vertex must mix one old F-edge with one old R-edge in a new cycle; otherwise the old 5-cycle remains a closed component of one replacement. There cannot be exactly one such vertex: the restriction of either replacement to the old 5-cycle would then have exactly one odd-degree vertex. Thus there are at least two mixing vertices per block. At each, the old R–R transition has been cut. The blocks are disjoint, so these cuts total at least `2t`.

This is a statement about a direct exchange from the supplied old partition, not a prohibition on sequences of count-preserving preparatory moves. The bad construction shows that the same abstract incidence pattern cannot guarantee the direct reduction for arbitrary cyclic orders.

This is not a counterexample to an incidence-based *necessary upper bound on minimum partitions*: `t+1` is still linear in `5t`. It proves the narrower and useful statement that incidence data alone cannot determine whether the supplied partition is minimum, or the cost of its best global recombination.

## 9. Exact remaining obstruction

The established implications are deliberately separated:

1. **Full optimality supplies hereditary optimality.** It forces all inequalities (1.7), (2.3), and the independent-packet forest condition. This step is valid for exchanges of unbounded size.
2. **Signs and branching quantify actual failures of two-trail simplicity.** They cannot simply be set to zero: Section 3 gives sharp global optima. A tight ring also forces cactus macrographs, a further proved restriction.
3. **A packet rank account is available.** If the old cycles admit the explicit port-complete grouping and bounded parameter `gamma chi_f(Q)`, Theorem 4.2 already gives O(n), charging coincident dense supports once. The homogeneous linear-support case even has an exact fractional certificate.
4. **What is not proved is any uniform bound on that parameter, or a replacement operation that bypasses it for every optimum.** The laminar hierarchy shows that every condition derived solely from clean-ring stability, square-filled incidence, and independent packet optimality can coexist with a logarithmic overcount. Keeping its old cycles fixed cannot give a bounded packet parameter of the stated kind. The repeat-visit bound is also specifically too expensive there.
5. **Support-only data are insufficient for a complete recombination rule.** A rule recognizing the direct reductions in Section 7 must allow multiple cuts of one old cycle and use actual cyclic order. That section gives a global two-slot rule for one family and an equal-incidence family where the reduction is impossible; it does not rule out every conceivable incidence-based necessary upper bound. Section 5 shows that demanding favorable ports from an otherwise optimum packet is not a cost-free repair, even for 2-connected series-parallel graphs.

Accordingly, a proof continuing this route needs a new **cost-controlled cross-packet recombination** that is justified by full global optimality, can change internal transitions, and does not pay once per nested support level or once per repeat visit. No such general rule is established here. In particular, neither a lexicographic length argument nor a signed-dual fitting argument has been shown to control these cross-packet costs.

The input `c_f(G)<=r(G)` remains useful but does not close this gap. Outside the packet-forest class, the local nonnegative certificates need not be globally cycle-feasible; (6.10) exhibits a logarithmic violation after insufficient optimization. No additive O(n) repair from an arbitrary **global optimum** has been proved or refuted by these examples.

**Bottom line:** there is a genuine signed-overlap extension of the clean-ring exchange, a proved packet-colouring rank bound with exact dense-packet special cases, and explicit global constructions pinpointing what they miss. The universal constant C and the unrestricted Erdős–Gallai theorem remain unresolved in this work.
