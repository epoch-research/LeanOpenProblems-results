# Exact finite-dimensional obstructions for planar few-distance sets

## Status and main conclusions

**The sharp bound `n <= C D sqrt(log(2D))` is neither proved nor disproved.**
The results here are unconditional obstructions to specific finite-dimensional
reductions, not a claimed impossibility theorem for all uses of the distance
colors or for arguments restricted to global maximizers.

Three results are proved below.

1. For **every** planar point set, its distance coherent closure `C(P)` satisfies
   
   \[
   \dim\mathcal C(P)\ge\frac{n^2}{4D+2}.
   \]
   It also contains a full real matrix-algebra corner of order at least
   `n/(2D+1)`. Consequently, for **every global maximizer** of `F(D)`, not
   merely for a particular grid, the coherent closure has dimension at least
   `c D log D` for large `D`. Completion to a coherent configuration cannot
   be assumed to preserve `O(D)` dimension, even on global maximizers.

2. On a square grid, the coherent closure is **exactly** the centralizer
   algebra of the eight square symmetries. More generally the same holds for
   symmetric Cartesian products satisfying an explicit nonzero fourth-moment
   condition, which the grid and all our perturbations satisfy. For even
   side length `L` and `n=L^2`, its dimension is
   
   \[
   \dim\mathcal C=\frac{n^2+2n}{8}.
   \]
   In particular, the near-sharp grids have coherent dimension
   `Theta(D^2 log D)`, not `O(D log D)` or even `O(D^2)`.

3. There are arbitrarily small **algebraic Cartesian perturbations of square
   grids** preserving simultaneously:
   * the entire polynomial Hilbert function, at every degree;
   * all point moments through degree `4K`;
   * the tuple `J, M, M^(circ 2), ..., M^(circ K)` up to a **single orthogonal
     conjugacy fixing the constant vector**, for any `K <= (L-16)/4`;
   * the same unmarked coherent algebra, as actual matrix subspaces under
     the natural indexing.
   
   Nevertheless their distance counts jump from
   `Theta(n/sqrt(log n))` to `n^2/32+3n/8`, and their ordered distance energies
   fall from `Theta(n^3 log n)` to `48n^2+O(n^(3/2))`.

Thus the obstruction is stronger than an EDM-spectrum-only example. Entire
Hilbert functions, growing collections of radial inertia data, and unmarked
coherent closure still lose the original distance-color fusion information.
This **does not** rule out retaining the individual `A_s`, exact higher-degree
annihilation, full Gaussian/positive-definite kernel information, or genuinely
new consequences of global cardinality extremality.

## 1. Definitions and normalization

Let `P={p_1,...,p_n}` consist of distinct points of the real plane. Write

\[
 M_{ij}=|p_i-p_j|^2,\qquad
 S(P)=\{s>0:s=M_{ij}\text{ for some }i,j\},\qquad D=|S(P)|.
\]

Let `A_0=I` and `A_s=[M_ij=s]` for `s>0`. Thus
`sum_(s in S union {0}) A_s=J`. Ordered multiplicities and energy are

\[
 r_s=\sum_{ij}(A_s)_{ij},\qquad
 E(P)=\sum_{s>0}r_s^2,\qquad\sum_{s>0}r_s=n(n-1).
\]

The **distance coherent closure** `C(P)` is the smallest real matrix subspace
containing `I,J,M` and closed under ordinary matrix multiplication, transpose,
and entrywise multiplication. It contains every `A_s` by exact entrywise
Lagrange interpolation. Equivalently it is the coherent algebra generated by
the distance coloring. No commutativity is assumed.

A finite-dimensional subalgebra of entrywise functions containing `J` has a
basis of disjoint `0-1` indicators of a partition of ordered pairs. Closure
under ordinary multiplication gives the usual coherent-configuration
intersection numbers. We use this terminology only for the **closure**, not
as an assumption on the original distance colors.

## 2. Coherent-closure growth: an obstruction valid for every maximizer

### Theorem 2.1 — Radial-block lower bound

Let `P` be any planar point set, `n>=2`. Let `m` be the maximum number of its
points on a circle centered at its centroid, counting the possible
radius-zero shell as a singleton. Then

\[
 m\le 2D+1,\qquad
 \boxed{\dim\mathcal C(P)\ge\frac{n^2}{2m}\ge\frac{n^2}{4D+2}.}       \tag{2.1}
\]

If there are `t` centroid-radius shells, `C(P)` contains a matrix corner
isomorphic to `M_t(R)`, with

\[
 t\ge n/m\ge n/(2D+1).                                             \tag{2.2}
\]

**Proof.** Center the points and put `H=I-J/n`. The centered Gram matrix

\[
 B=-\tfrac12 HMH
\]

belongs to `C(P)`, as does `B circ I=diag(|p_i|^2)`. Polynomial interpolation
in this diagonal matrix gives all shell projections `E_a`.

A positive-radius shell containing `m_a` points has at most two points at
any given positive distance from one of its own points. Indeed two distinct
circles in the plane meet in at most two points. Therefore
`m_a-1<=2D`. The radius-zero shell, if present, has one point.

Now consider all the nonzero matrices

\[
 E_a A_s E_b,\qquad s\in S(P)\cup\{0\}.                            \tag{2.3}
\]

They are in `C(P)`, have disjoint supports, and partition all `n^2` ordered
pairs. Each has at most `2m` nonzero entries. If the source shell has positive
radius, a source point is not the centroid; its distance circle meets the
target's centroid circle in at most two points, so there are at most two
entries per row. A radius-zero target has at most one entry per row. If the
source shell is the centroid itself, there is just one row and at most `m`
entries. The zero distance causes no exception. Thus there are at least
`n^2/(2m)` nonzero, linearly independent matrices in (2.3).

Finally let `e_a=1_(shell a)/sqrt(m_a)`. All matrix units

\[
 e_a e_b^T=\frac{E_a J E_b}{\sqrt{m_a m_b}}
\]

belong to `C(P)`. Their span is the full matrix algebra on
`W=span{e_a}`. With `Q_W=sum_a e_a e_a^T`, this says
`Q_W C(P) Q_W = End(W) ~= M_t(R)`. QED.

The circle argument is the only geometric input beyond the rank-two Gram
realization. It is a lower bound on the true closure, not an assumption of
regularity or of association-scheme structure for the `A_s`.

### Corollary 2.2 — Global extremality does not repair small coherent closure

Let

\[
 F(d)=\max\{|P|:D(P)\le d\}.
\]

The maximum exists because feasible cardinalities are nonempty bounded
integers; for example the classical bound is
`|P|<=binom(d+2,2)`. Square grids and the sum-of-two-squares count give, for
all sufficiently large integers `d`,

\[
 F(d)\ge c_0 d\sqrt{\log d}.                                     \tag{2.4}
\]

For completeness, a grid of side length
`L=floor(a sqrt(d) (log d)^(1/4))`, with a sufficiently small fixed `a>0`,
has `D<=C L^2/sqrt(log L)<=d` and `L^2>=c_0 d sqrt(log d)`.

Therefore **every** `P` with `|P|=F(d)` and `D(P)<=d` satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\dim\mathcal C(P)\ge c_1 d\log d,\qquad
        \mathcal C(P)\text{ contains an }M_t(\mathbb R)\text{ corner with }
        t\ge c_2\sqrt{\log d}.}                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

In particular `dim C(P)/(d+1)` is unbounded even if only global maximizers
are considered. No regularity, lattice hypothesis, or classification of the
maximizers is used. This refutes a constant-dimension-cost coherent-completion
shortcut on the precise extremal class allowed in the question.

It does **not** give a quadratic lower bound for the closure of every
maximizer, and does not refute every possible stronger max-only property.

## 3. Exact coherent closure of Cartesian products

The next result determines a genuine noncommutative closure, rather than
postulating an association scheme.

### Theorem 3.1 — Square-symmetry centralizer

Let `A=-A` be a set of `L>=3` distinct real numbers, with at least two
absolute values. Put `P=A x A`, `n=L^2`, and

\[
 \mu_j=\frac1L\sum_{a\in A}a^j.
\]

Assume `mu_4 != 3 mu_2^2`. Then

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C(P)=\operatorname{End}_{D_4}(\mathbb R^P),}       \tag{3.1}
\]

where `D_4` denotes the eight coordinate sign changes and swaps. The
right-hand side is the space of all matrices constant on its ordered-pair
orbits. In particular this is the full orbital coherent algebra, not just a
subalgebra of it.

**Proof.** Write `p_i=(x_i,y_i)`. Centering is automatic. The Gram matrix
`B=[x_i x_j+y_i y_j]` is in `C(P)`. Put

\[
 \rho_i=x_i^2+y_i^2,\qquad u_i=x_i^2-y_i^2,\qquad v_i=2x_i y_i.
\]

Diagonal extraction gives `diag(rho)` and hence `rho rho^T` in the algebra.
The exact rank-two Schur identity is

\[
 T:=2B^{\circ2}-\rho\rho^T=uu^T+vv^T.                             \tag{3.2}
\]

The Cartesian sign symmetries give

\[
 u^Tv=0,\qquad
 \alpha:=\|u\|^2=2n(\mu_4-\mu_2^2),\qquad
 \gamma:=\|v\|^2=4n\mu_2^2.
\]

There are at least two squared coordinate values, so
`c:=n(mu_4-mu_2^2)>0`. Also `alpha-gamma=2n(mu_4-3mu_2^2)!=0`. Ordinary
spectral separation in the rank-two matrix (3.2) yields

\[
 U:=\frac{T^2-\gamma T}{\alpha-\gamma}=uu^T\in\mathcal C(P).        \tag{3.3}
\]

A mixed ordinary/entrywise product now exposes an orientation-sensitive
invariant:

\[
 W:=\frac{(U\circ B)B}{c}\in\mathcal C(P),\qquad
 W_{ij}=u_i(x_i x_j-y_i y_j).                                     \tag{3.4}
\]

To check (3.4), use
`sum_k u_k x_k^2=c`, `sum_k u_k y_k^2=-c`, and
`sum_k u_k x_k y_k=0`.

The algebra thus contains matrices whose joint entry values are

\[
 (\rho_i,\rho_j,u_i^2,u_j^2,B_{ij},W_{ij}).                        \tag{3.5}
\]

The row/column versions of `rho` and `u^2` come from multiplying their
diagonal matrices by `J`. These six invariants separate all ordered-pair
`D_4` orbits. Here is an explicit proof, including stabilizer cases. Bring
the first point to the fundamental chamber `p=(a,b)`, `a>=b>=0`; its values
`rho_i,u_i^2` uniquely determine `a,b`.

* If `a>b>0`, then `u_i=a^2-b^2>0`, and for `q=(z,w)` the two numbers
  `B_ij=az+bw` and `W_ij/u_i=az-bw` determine `z,w` individually.
* If `a>0=b`, then `B_ij` determines `z`, and `rho_j` determines `|w|`.
  Reflection in the horizontal axis fixes `p` and exchanges the two choices.
* If `a=b>0`, then `B_ij` and `rho_j` determine `z+w` and `z^2+w^2`, hence
  the unordered pair `{z,w}`. Coordinate swap fixes `p`.
* If `p=0`, then `rho_j,u_j^2` determine the unordered absolute coordinates
  of `q`, which determine its `D_4` orbit.

Take a real linear combination of the six matrices avoiding the finitely
many hyperplanes that would equate two different tuples (3.5). It has
different entry values on different ordered-pair orbits. Entrywise Lagrange
interpolation therefore puts every orbital indicator in `C(P)`.
Conversely, `I,J,M` and all the closure operations are invariant under the
square symmetries, so `C(P)` is contained in their centralizer. QED.

### Exact dimensions and square grids

Burnside's lemma on ordered pairs gives

\[
 \dim\mathcal C(A\times A)=
 \begin{cases}
  (n^2+2n)/8,&L\text{ even},\\
  (n^2+4n+3)/8,&L\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{3.6}
\]

For even `L`, only the identity and the two diagonal reflections have fixed
points, with fixed-point counts `n,L,L`. For odd `L`, the two axis
reflections also fix `L` points and the three nonidentity rotations each
fix the origin. Square fixed-point counts and divide by eight. The case
`L=2`, excluded from the extraction division, is the ordinary four-vertex
square and also obeys (3.6).

There are

\[
 t=\binom{\lceil L/2\rceil+1}{2}
\]

point orbits, so the algebra contains a full `M_t(R)` corner. For even
`L`, `t=L^2/8+L/4`.

For the centered unit-spaced grid,

\[
 \mu_2=\frac{L^2-1}{12},\quad
 \mu_4=\frac{(L^2-1)(3L^2-7)}{240},\quad
 \mu_4-3\mu_2^2=-\frac{L^4-1}{120}\ne0.                           \tag{3.7}
\]

Thus (3.1)--(3.6) apply to every square grid of side at least three. Since
`D=Theta(n/sqrt(log n))`,

\[
 \dim\mathcal C=\Theta(n^2)=\Theta(D^2\log D).                    \tag{3.8}
\]

This is an exact obstruction on the standard hard-regime examples to an
upper bound such as `dim C <= C D log D`, which combined with (2.1) would
have supplied the desired estimate. In fact even `dim C=O(D^2)` is false
universally. **The grids are not asserted to be global maximizers.** The
maximizer-specific statement is exactly (2.5), not (3.8).

## 4. Simultaneous radial-jet and full-Hilbert-function obstruction

### Theorem 4.1 — Algebraic moment-preserving perturbations

Fix integers `K>=1` and `m>=2K+8`, and put `L=2m`, `n=L^2`. Let

\[
 a_j=j-\tfrac12\quad(1\le j\le m),\qquad
 A_0=\{\pm a_1,\ldots,\pm a_m\},\qquad P=A_0\times A_0.
\]

There exist `0<b_1<...<b_m`, all algebraic and arbitrarily close to the
`a_j`, such that for `A=\{\pm b_1,...,\pm b_m\}` and `Q=A x A`:

1. All unweighted point moments of total degree at most `4K` agree between
   `P` and `Q`.
2. There is one real orthogonal matrix `O`, with `O1=1`, such that
   
   \[
   O M_P^{\circ k}O^T=M_Q^{\circ k}\quad(0\le k\le K),             \tag{4.1}
   \]
   where the zeroth power denotes `J`. Thus every linear combination,
   every self-adjoint ordinary matrix word, and every finite Gaussian
   Taylor truncation using these jets has the same spectrum and inertia.
3. At every degree `ell>=0`, the polynomial Hilbert function is
   
   \[
   h_\ell(P)=h_\ell(Q)=\#\{(a,b):0\le a,b<L,\ a+b\le\ell\}.       \tag{4.2}
   \]
4. Under the natural indexing by signed coordinate indices,
   
   \[
   \mathcal C(P)=\mathcal C(Q)=\operatorname{End}_{D_4}(\mathbb R^n).
                                                                        \tag{4.3}
   \]
5. The perturbed set has exactly
   
   \[
   \boxed{D(Q)=\frac{n^2}{32}+\frac{3n}{8},\qquad
   E(Q)=48n^2-120n^{3/2}+8n+120\sqrt n.}                          \tag{4.4}
   \]

Both sets are genuinely planar, with centered Gram rank two and EDM rank
four. The original grid satisfies

\[
 D(P)=\Theta(n/\sqrt{\log n}),\qquad E(P)=\Theta(n^3\log n).       \tag{4.5}
\]

#### 4.1 The exact genericity mechanism

Set `q=2K` and impose the moment equations

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^m b_i^{2j}=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i^{2j},\qquad 1\le j\le q. \tag{4.6}
\]

Their Jacobian has rank `q` near the original distinct positive `a_i`.
More strongly, choose **any** `q` coordinate indices. On those columns the
Jacobian has entries `2j b_i^(2j-1)`; factoring rows and columns leaves a
Vandermonde determinant in the distinct `b_i^2`. Every such minor is
nonzero.

It follows that if a nonzero polynomial `R(b)` depends on at most eight
coordinate indices, then it cannot vanish identically on a neighborhood
of the original point in the manifold (4.6). There are at least `q`
unused indices. Solve the moment equations for these indices by the real
analytic implicit function theorem. The indices on which `R` depends can
vary over an open set, where a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish identically.
This proves local nonvanishing, not just an unsupported genericity claim.

The one-dimensional squared gaps are the quadratic forms

\[
 (2b_i)^2,\qquad (b_i+b_j)^2,\qquad (b_i-b_j)^2\quad(i<j),          \tag{4.7}
\]

together with zero. Sums of two such forms are distinct **as formal
polynomials**, except for exchanging the two summands. To verify this,
represent a gap by `v v^T`, where

\[
 v\in\{0,2e_i,e_i+e_j,e_i-e_j:i<j\}.
\]

Off-diagonal entries of `v v^T+w w^T` identify all pair types unless two
opposite-sign types on the same index pair cancel. A sole off-diagonal
entry of magnitude one identifies a single pair type; its diagonal
residual identifies either the zero vector or a doubled coordinate.
Magnitude two identifies the same pair type repeated. Two different
nonzero off-diagonal positions identify their two pair types. With no
off-diagonal entries, either both types are zero/doubled coordinates
(diagonal coefficients divisible by four), or the pair is
`e_i+e_j,e_i-e_j` (diagonal coefficients two at `i,j`). These cases are
distinct and their decompositions are unique.

The difference between two formally different squared planar distances is
therefore a nonzero quadratic polynomial involving at most eight indices.
By the preceding local-surjectivity argument, none vanishes identically on
the moment manifold. A connected sufficiently small analytic neighborhood
minus these finitely many proper zero sets is dense and open. Choose `b`
there. This proves all the required exact distance noncollisions
simultaneously while preserving (4.6).

The coordinates can be chosen algebraic. Put `t_i=b_i^2` and
`R_0(t)=prod_i(t-a_i^2)`. Newton identities show that (4.6) is equivalent
to keeping the first `q` nonleading coefficients of the monic root
polynomial fixed. Locally all the allowed polynomials are

\[
 R_0(t)+H(t),\qquad \deg H\le m-q-1.                             \tag{4.8}
\]

Their roots remain simple, positive, and ordered for sufficiently small
coefficients. Root-to-coefficient coordinates are a local analytic
diffeomorphism. The desired noncollision choices form a nonempty open
subset of this coefficient chart, which contains rational coefficient
choices. Its roots `t_i`, and their positive square roots `b_i`, are
algebraic.

#### 4.2 Exact multiplicities

There are `g=m^2` nonzero squared gaps in (4.7). Their one-dimensional
ordered multiplicities `c_a` are:

* `2` for the `m` doubled-coordinate gaps;
* `4` for the `m(m-1)` pair sum/difference gaps;
* `c_0=L` for zero.

In `A x A`, squared distances are unordered pairs of these squared gaps,
with repetition, other than `{0,0}`. Formal uniqueness and the avoidance
argument prove

\[
 D=g+\binom{g+1}{2}=g(g+3)/2.
\]

The multiplicities of `{0,a}`, `{a,a}`, and `{a,b}`, `0<a<b`, are
respectively `2L c_a`, `c_a^2`, and `2c_a c_b`. Write

\[
 U=\sum_{a>0}c_a^2=4L^2-6L,\qquad
 V=\sum_{a>0}c_a^4=64L^2-120L.
\]

Then

\[
 E=4L^2U+2U^2-V=48L^4-120L^3+8L^2+120L,
\]

which gives (4.4). These count actual ordered distance classes; there are
no weights substituted for the multiplicities.

#### 4.3 Simultaneous orthogonal conjugacy, not just separate spectra

Odd coordinate moments vanish. Equations (4.6) preserve all even
one-dimensional moments through degree `2q=4K`. Since the configurations
are Cartesian products, every planar point moment of total degree at most
`4K` agrees.

Let `V_P,V_Q` be the evaluation matrices of all monomials in two variables
of total degree at most `2K`, using the same column order. Their feature
Gram matrices agree exactly:

\[
 V_P^T V_P=V_Q^T V_Q.                                             \tag{4.9}
\]

Thus the map `V_P c -> V_Q c` is a well-defined isometry of column spaces
and extends to an orthogonal `O` on `R^n`. The constant column gives
`O1=1`. For every `k<=K`, the universal polynomial expansion of
`|x-y|^(2k)` has degree at most `2K` on each side, so a fixed symmetric
coefficient matrix `C_k` satisfies

\[
 M_P^{\circ k}=V_P C_k V_P^T,\qquad
 M_Q^{\circ k}=V_Q C_k V_Q^T.
\]

This proves (4.1). In particular arbitrary mixed ordinary products of the
first `K` radial jets have matching joint traces and spectral invariants.

If desired, `O` can be chosen to commute with the square symmetries. The
column-space isometry intertwines their permutation representations. For
arbitrarily close configurations their column projections `Pi_P,Pi_Q` are
arbitrarily close. Extend the isometry `T` by the invertible operator
`T Pi_P+(I-Pi_Q)(I-Pi_P)` and take its orthogonal polar factor. All these
operators commute with the group, and the polar factor still agrees with
`T` on the original column space. Thus one may take
`O in End_(D_4)(R^n)`. This is an ordinary-algebra conjugacy; it is **not**
an entrywise-algebra automorphism.

For `K>=1`, equality of `mu_2,mu_4` also gives the familiar exact EDM
spectrum. With

\[
 S=2n\mu_2,\qquad T_4=2n(\mu_4+\mu_2^2),
\]

it is

\[
 \{-S,-S,S+\sqrt{nT_4},S-\sqrt{nT_4},0^{(n-4)}\}.                 \tag{4.10}
\]

Indeed the coordinate columns are orthogonal to `1` and to the squared
radius vector, and their Gram matrix is `(S/2)I_2`. The squared radius is
nonconstant, so `nT_4>S^2`, proving rank four.

#### 4.4 Entire Hilbert function and common coherent closure

For any `L` distinct numbers in `A`, let `g_A(t)=prod_(a in A)(t-a)`.
The vanishing ideal of `A x A` is

\[
 I(A\times A)=(g_A(x),g_A(y)).                                   \tag{4.11}
\]

Division reduces a polynomial to degree less than `L` separately in `x,y`.
If the remainder vanishes on the Cartesian product, successive univariate
interpolation shows it is zero. The two monic generators have leading
monomials `x^L,y^L`; reduction never increases total degree. Hence the
standard monomials are exactly `x^a y^b`, `0<=a,b<L`, and their degree
filtration proves (4.2). This is equality of all ranks, not only of finitely
many low-order moment ranks.

Finally (4.6) preserves `mu_2,mu_4`. The nonzero grid cumulant (3.7)
therefore remains nonzero. Theorem 3.1 identifies both coherent closures
with the same `D_4` orbital algebra under the common coordinate indexing,
proving (4.3).

### Precise scope of the obstruction

The construction preserves a number of radial jets proportional to
`sqrt(n)`, not to `D(P)`. It cannot preserve all jets through the exact
annihilator degree while introducing new distances. Indeed if
`f(s)=prod_(a in S(P))(1-s/a)` and `deg f=D(P)<=K`, then
`f[M_P]=I`; (4.1) would force `f[M_Q]=I` and hence
`S(Q) subset S(P)`.

The original distance colors are different **fusions of the same ordered-
pair orbitals**. Neither the individual `A_s`, their ranks, nor their
positions in the Schur algebra are preserved. Orthogonal conjugacy does not
commute with entrywise multiplication. Thus (4.1)--(4.3) do not say that the
full colored Euclidean data are equivalent. In particular, `D` is not
matched; these examples do not refute an inequality allowed to use `D`
separately.

Indeed color-dependent ranks strongly distinguish the two configurations.
The grid's shortest-distance matrix is `T_L tensor I + I tensor T_L`, where
`T_L` is path adjacency. Its eigenvalues are
`2 cos(pi a/(L+1))+2 cos(pi b/(L+1))`, `1<=a,b<=L`; exactly the `L` pairs
`a+b=L+1` give zero. Thus its rank is `L^2-L`. In the generic perturbation,
every positive color has at most `8L` ordered entries when `L>=4`, by the
multiplicity list above, and hence rank at most `8L`. **Color-dependent rank
arguments are not ruled out by this construction.**

Also the perturbed set has quadratic distance count and is not asserted
to be a global maximizer. Theorem 4.1 is an information-loss obstruction
for the stated invariant packages; the unconditional extremal conclusion
is Corollary 2.2. No max-only structural principle is refuted by silently
applying a nonextremal perturbation.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

## 6. Checked literature boundaries

The corpus was used to check the following precise statements; no claim of
exhaustive literature search or publication priority is made for the
lemmas derived in this note.

* **Petrov--Pohoata, arXiv:1912.08181**, Theorem `BBS`: a planar `s`-distance
  set has at most `binom(s+2,2)` points. Their inertia/CLP theorem explains
  the existing Hilbert-function quadratic bound. Cardinality extremality
  is not equality in that bound.
* **Nozaki, arXiv:0912.2387**, Theorem `main`: the Euclidean generalized
  Larman--Rogers--Seidel integrality conclusion requires
  `|X|>=2N`, where
  `N=binom(d+s-1,s-1)+binom(d+s-2,s-2)`. In dimension two this is
  `N=s^2`, so the hypothesis is `n>=2s^2`. For every `s>=2`,
  
  \[
   \binom{s+2}{2}<2s^2.
  \]
  
  Thus this particular standard integrality threshold is **vacuous for
  every planar s-distance set**, including every global maximizer. It
  cannot be invoked to supply arithmetic rigidity here. This does not
  exclude a genuinely improved planar, color-dependent rank argument.
* **Bannai--Bannai, arXiv:0905.2143**, main theorems: coherent-configuration
  conclusions require Euclidean design and support/design-strength
  hypotheses (or specified tight-design hypotheses). They are not theorems
  that arbitrary cardinality-maximal `s`-distance sets form schemes.
* **Cilleruelo--Sharir--Sheffer, arXiv:1306.0242**, Section 1: the square grid
  has `D=Theta(n/sqrt(log n))` and ordered equal-distance energy
  `Theta(n^3 log n)`. Their sets `E_i` count both orientations, matching the
  normalization here.
