# Actual Gaussian collisions: normalization obstructions and one finite support lemma

## Status

**Neither the height-free sharp bound nor the unrestricted actual contraction is proved or disproved here.** In particular, no solution of

\[
 D(P)\gg |P|/\sqrt{\log |P|}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 K(P)^2\le K(Q)^2+C,\quad 2\le |Q|\le |P|/2,
\]

is asserted for arbitrary finite Gaussian sets, much less arbitrary planar sets.

There are two new, fully actual, elementary obstructions:

1. **Pigeonhole-modulus matching can fail even with every sub-cardinality ideal available.** There are Gaussian-primitive sets of size \(n=L^4\) for which a matching from all Gaussian ideals of index less than \(n\) to their actual collision-distance labels has size at most \(5\sqrt n\). If rows are norm integers, even allowing all orientations of each ideal, the bound is \(3\sqrt n\). In a simpler variant, every ideal of index in \([n/2,n)\) has many actual collisions but exactly the same three collision labels. The full common-ideal normalization of every such fiber gives the same four-point set.
2. **Optimizing the inverse-\(r_2\) kernel over all ideals does not repair the scaling obstruction.** For every \(n\ge3\) there is a Gaussian-primitive actual set \(P\subset\mathbb Z\) with
   \[
   \boxed{\sup_{\beta\ne0}
   \sum_{\substack{x,y\in P,\ x\ne y\\\beta\mid x-y}}
       \frac1{r_2(N((x-y)/\beta))}=\frac12.} \tag{A}
   \]
   The sum includes **all actual fibers and all ordered collision multiplicities**. Moreover, every subset with at least three points still has a small reciprocal-representation kernel after division by its own full Gaussian gcd. This is not just an unremoved common scaling factor.

Both constructions have bounded \(K\); indeed the second has **all unordered distances distinct**. They obstruct the specified certificate/kernel mechanisms, not the sharp distance inequality or all possible adaptive support-sensitive arguments.

Section 5 states **exactly one unproved finite assertion**: an adaptive prime-fiber inequality for the actual Hartley support, with occupied-point entropy as a cost. Its truth would give the height-free bound directly. Its proof is missing. No positive-order norm entropy, ambient norm capacity, or fixed-modulus limiting theorem is substituted for that assertion.

No Lean files were edited. In particular, `Spec.lean` is unchanged.

## 1. Actual collisions and full common-ideal normalization

Let \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[i]\), \(N(z)=z\bar z\), and, for \(|P|=n\ge2\), let

\[
 \mathcal S(P)=\{N(x-y):x,y\in P,\ x\ne y\},\qquad
 D(P)=|\mathcal S(P)|,\qquad K(P)=n/D(P).
\]

All distances below are positive actual squared distances. Write

\[
 a_P(z)=|\{x\in P:x+z\in P\}|,\qquad
 r_d(P)=\sum_{N(z)=d}a_P(z).
\]

### 1.1 Normalizing the points, not an ambient representation count

Choose \(p_0\in P\), and let \(g_P\ne0\) generate the ideal generated by all \(p-p_0\). Then

\[
 P^{\mathrm{prim}}=(P-p_0)/g_P\subset\mathcal O
\]

is Gaussian-primitive. Multiplication by \(N(g_P)\) bijects its actual norm support with that of \(P\). Thus cardinality, \(D\), \(K\), and all radial pair multiplicities are preserved, with their labels rescaled. This can be done again inside every actual fiber.

In particular, a primitive set of at least two points is not contained in one coset of **any** nonunit Gaussian ideal. Full normalization therefore removes unary ideal refinements. It does not bound the original or normalized diameter in terms of cardinality.

The stronger identity \(\gcd\mathcal S(P)=N(g_P)\) was already proved in `lattice_energy_obstruction.md`; no stronger normalization claim is needed here.

### 1.2 The sharp finite occupancy lower bound

For \(\beta\ne0\), put \(m=N(\beta)\) and

\[
 P_c=P\cap(c+(\beta)),\qquad n_c=|P_c|.
\]

The quotient has exactly \(m\) elements. The ordered collision mass is exactly

\[
 C_\beta(P)=\sum_c n_c(n_c-1)
 =\sum_{\substack{z\ne0\\\beta\mid z}}a_P(z). \tag{1}
\]

If \(n=am+s\), \(0\le s<m\), then

\[
 \boxed{C_\beta(P)\ge ma(a-1)+2as.} \tag{2}
\]

To prove this, move one point from a class of size at least two more than another class into the smaller class. This decreases the sum of squares. At the minimum, \(s\) classes have size \(a+1\) and the others have size \(a\), giving (2). In particular,

\[
 \boxed{n/2\le m<n\ \Longrightarrow\ C_\beta(P)\ge2(n-m).} \tag{3}
\]

This improves the real-valued Cauchy bound \(n^2/m-n\), but the extra multiplicities need not give extra norm labels.

For example, write \(H=\operatorname{diam}(P)^2\). Applying the actual-endpoint argument of `finite_gaussian_collision_bounds.md` after full normalization gives exactly

\[
 D(P)\,S\!\left(\frac{2H}{nN(g_P)}\right)
 \ge S(n-1)-S(\lceil n/2\rceil-1), \tag{4}
\]

where \(S(x)\) counts positive Gaussian norm integers at most \(x\). Indeed each represented \(m\in[n/2,n)\) divides an actual normalized difference; the quotient norm is at most \(2H/(nN(g_P))\). A fixed actual label can be used by at most that many different represented quotient norms. The rough-norm injection likewise remains valid with this normalized quotient bound.

Equation (4) still has a height parameter. Neither (2) nor primitive normalization removes it.

## 2. A primitive obstruction to actual collision matching

### 2.1 The certificate being tested

Make a finite bipartite graph whose left vertices are the nonunit Gaussian ideals \(I\) with \(N(I)<n\), modulo associates. Its right vertices are \(\mathcal S(P)\). Put an edge \(I\sim d\) if some two **actual** points in a common \(I\)-fiber have squared distance \(d\).

Let \(\nu_{\rm id}(P)\) be its maximum matching size. Always

\[
 D(P)\ge\nu_{\rm id}(P). \tag{5}
\]

This allows every possible actual collision for an ideal, not an arbitrarily chosen one. There is an even more economical graph with represented integers \(2\le m<n\) as rows: join \(m\) to \(d\) if **some** ideal of norm \(m\) has that collision. Denote its matching number by \(\nu_{\rm norm}(P)\). This gives a row more choices than fixing one Gaussian generator for \(m\).

A lower bound of order \(n/\sqrt{\log n}\) for either matching number would suffice for the desired distance bound. The following theorem rules out that unconditional certificate. The same upper bounds apply to fractional matchings with unit capacity at every row and every norm label.

### Theorem 1 — repeated actual fibers at all large sub-cardinality indices

Let \(L\ge2\), \(r\ge2\), and put

\[
 A_L=\{0,\ldots,L-1\}+i\{0,\ldots,L-1\},\quad
 k=L^2,\quad n=rk,\quad T=n!,
\]

\[
 P_{L,r}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{r-1}(A_L+jT),\qquad h=2(L-1)^2.
 \tag{6}
\]

Then:

* \(P_{L,r}\) is Gaussian-primitive and has \(n\) points.
* For **every** ideal \((\beta)\) with \(h<N(\beta)<n\), its nonempty actual fibers are exactly
  \[
  \{u,u+T,\ldots,u+(r-1)T\},\qquad u\in A_L. \tag{7}
  \]
  There are \(k\) such fibers, each of size \(r\).
* Its ordered collision mass for every such ideal is
  \[
  C_\beta(P_{L,r})=kr(r-1)=n(r-1), \tag{8}
  \]
  and the entire collision-norm support is the same set
  \[
  \mathcal M_r=\{T^2,4T^2,\ldots,(r-1)^2T^2\}. \tag{9}
  \]
* Each fiber in (7), after division by its **full** common Gaussian ideal, is, up to a unit and translation, \(\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}\). Its normalized support is \(\{1,4,\ldots,(r-1)^2\}\).

#### Proof

The grid contains both differences \(1\) and \(i\), so its union of translates is primitive. The translates are disjoint since \(T>L\).

If \(m=N(\beta)<n\), then \(\beta\mid m\mid T\). Consequently

\[
 \beta\mid (u+jT)-(v+\ell T)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta\mid u-v. \tag{10}
\]

A nonzero \(u-v\in A_L-A_L\) has norm at most \(h\). If \(m>h\), divisibility in (10) forces \(u=v\). This proves the exact fibers (7), not just their occupancy counts. Equations (8) and (9) follow immediately. The common ideal of a fiber is generated by \(T\), since its consecutive differences are \(T\). QED.

In fact the global ordered multiplicities at the labels in (9) are exactly

\[
 r_{j^2T^2}(P_{L,r})=2(r-j)k,\qquad 1\le j<r. \tag{11}
\]

For verification, every difference is \(\ell T+x+iy\), with \(|x|,|y|\le L-1\). Since \(T>4L^2\), equality of its norm with \(j^2T^2\) first forces \(|\ell|=j\), and then forces \(x=y=0\). Thus the full multiplicity table at these labels is repeated for every ideal in the specified range.

### 2.2 Two consequences

**The original near-cardinality range can see only three labels.** Take \(r=4\). Then \(n/2=2L^2>h\), so every ideal of every represented index \(n/2\le m<n\) has exactly the fibers (7) with four points. Each row has ordered mass \(3n\), but all rows have exactly the same three neighbors. Even the optimal choice of actual collision witnesses, with all orientations and all their multiplicities available, has matching size at most three. Normalizing each fiber by its full gcd does not distinguish the rows.

**Adding every smaller pigeonhole modulus still does not suffice for matching.** Let

\[
 \mathcal A(x)=\#\{I\subset\mathcal O:0<N(I)\le x\}
             =\frac14\sum_{1\le d\le x}r_2(d).
\]

Only rows of index at most \(h\) can have neighbors outside \(\mathcal M_r\). Therefore

\[
 \nu_{\rm id}(P_{L,r})\le\mathcal A(h)+r-1,
 \qquad
 \nu_{\rm norm}(P_{L,r})\le S(h)+r-1. \tag{12}
\]

The harmless unit-ideal overcount makes these bounds slightly generous. Every ideal has one generator \(a+bi\) with \(a\ge1,b\ge0\); hence, for \(h\ge1\),

\[
 \mathcal A(h)\le\lfloor\sqrt h\rfloor(\lfloor\sqrt h\rfloor+1)
 \le h+\sqrt h\le2h,
 \qquad S(h)\le h. \tag{13}
\]

Set \(r=L^2\), so \(n=L^4\). Equations (12)–(13) give

\[
 \boxed{\nu_{\rm id}(P_{L,L^2})<5\sqrt n,
 \qquad\nu_{\rm norm}(P_{L,L^2})<3\sqrt n.} \tag{14}
\]

Both are \(o(n/\sqrt{\log n})\). This includes all ideals below the cardinality, not only one fixed interval of indices. For a fractional matching, the small rows carry at most their number in total flow, and every other row sends its flow to the \(r-1\) common columns; the same proof applies.

### 2.3 Why this is not a distance counterexample

There are actually at least \((r-1)L^2\) distinct **pinned** distances from \(0\) to the other grid translates. The labels

\[
 (jT+x)^2+y^2,
 \quad 1\le j<r,\quad 0\le x,y<L, \tag{15}
\]

are all distinct. Different \(j\)'s have disjoint ranges. For fixed \(j\), an equality first forces \(x=x'\), since \(2jT\) exceeds the possible difference of the quadratic remainder; then nonnegative \(y,y'\) force \(y=y'\). Thus

\[
 D(P_{L,r})\ge(r-1)L^2,
 \qquad K(P_{L,r})^2\le\left(\frac r{r-1}\right)^2\le4. \tag{16}
\]

The missing labels are supplied by geometric cross-fiber comparisons, or by inspecting much more of the actual support in the small-index fibers. They are not forced by assigning a different label to each pigeonhole modulus. In particular, this theorem does **not** preclude using all of the support of a selected small-index fiber, or taking geometric windows. It rules out the specified matching/constant-congestion transfer from forced modulus collisions.

## 3. Common-ideal optimization does not repair inverse representation weights

Let

\[
 r_2(d)=|\{z\in\mathcal O:N(z)=d\}|.
\]

For any nonzero \(\beta\), define the fully actual ideal-collision kernel

\[
 J_\beta(P)=
 \sum_{\substack{x,y\in P,\ x\ne y\\\beta\mid x-y}}
 \frac1{r_2(N((x-y)/\beta))}. \tag{17}
\]

This sums over **all** nonempty fibers, including all ordered pair multiplicities. If all of \(P\) lies in one coset of \((\beta)\), it is the kernel of that normalized set. But \(\beta\) need not divide every difference in (17).

For an actual distance \(d\) contributing to (17), \(d/N(\beta)\) is a positive norm integer, and at most \(r_2(d/N(\beta))\) different displacement vectors divisible by \(\beta\) have norm \(d\). Each displacement has translation multiplicity at most \(n\). Hence the valid capacity consequence is

\[
 \boxed{J_\beta(P)\le nD(P).} \tag{18}
\]

A uniform lower bound \(\sup_\beta J_\beta(P)\gg n^2/\sqrt{\log n}\) would therefore suffice for the sharp distance bound. It is false by much more than the loss from one unremoved common scaling factor.

### Theorem 2 — primitive CRT obstruction, uniform over all normalized subsets

For every \(n\ge3\) and integer \(a\ge1\), there is a Gaussian-primitive set

\[
 P=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\subset\mathbb Z
\]

with the following properties.

1. Its unordered actual distances are all different:
   \[
   D(P)=\binom n2. \tag{19}
   \]
2. For every subset \(Q\subset P\) of size \(k\ge3\), let \(g_Q\) be the full Gaussian gcd of its differences. Then
   \[
   \boxed{
   W(Q):=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in Q\\x\ne y}}
       \frac1{r_2(N((x-y)/g_Q))}
       \le\frac{k(k-1)}{4(2a+1)}.} \tag{20}
   \]
   A unit choice in \(g_Q\) does not change the norm.
3. For all Gaussian \(\beta\ne0\),
   \[
   \boxed{J_\beta(P)\le
     \max\left\{\frac12,\frac{n(n-1)}{4(2a+1)}\right\}.} \tag{21}
   \]
   In particular, taking \(a\ge n^2\) gives exactly
   \[
   \boxed{\sup_{\beta\ne0}J_\beta(P)=\frac12.} \tag{22}
   \]

#### Proof: exact pair-specific prime divisibility

Assign a distinct rational prime \(p_e>n\), \(p_e\equiv1\pmod4\), to every unordered pair \(e=\{i,j\}\). Only finitely many primes are needed. Their existence requires no quantitative prime-distribution theorem: the infinitude of primes \(1\bmod4\) has the elementary proof using prime divisors of \((2p_1\cdots p_s)^2+1\).

For each modulus \(p_e^a\), prescribe

\[
 u_i\equiv u_j\equiv0\pmod{p_e^a},
 \qquad
 u_t\equiv t\pmod{p_e^a}\quad(t\notin\{i,j\}), \tag{23}
\]

using vertex indices \(1,\ldots,n\). The residues assigned to the other vertices are distinct and nonzero modulo \(p_e\). CRT supplies simultaneous actual integers \(u_1,\ldots,u_n\).

They are distinct: to distinguish vertices \(i,j\), use a prime assigned to \(\{i,t\}\) with \(t\ne i,j\). At that prime, \(u_i\) is zero and \(u_j\) is nonzero.

Translate by \(u_1\), divide by the positive integer

\[
 g=\gcd(u_2-u_1,\ldots,u_n-u_1),
\]

and call the resulting integers \(x_i\). For every \(e\), a third vertex has a different residue from its two endpoints modulo \(p_e\), so \(p_e\nmid g\). Consequently the normalized set is primitive and still satisfies

\[
 p_{\{i,j\}}^a\mid x_i-x_j,
 \qquad
 p_{\{i,j\}}\nmid x_u-x_v
 \quad\text{for every other unordered pair }\{u,v\}. \tag{24}
\]

A rational gcd of real-integer differences also generates their Gaussian ideal: this follows either from integer Bezout or from equality of the generated ideals. Thus this is full Gaussian primitivity, not just a coordinate convention.

Property (24) already proves (19). The squared distance of pair \(e\) is divisible by \(p_e\), while that of every other pair is not.

#### Proof of the subset assertion

Fix \(Q\) with at least three points and a pair \(e\) in it. A third point of \(Q\) is not congruent to this pair modulo \(p_e\). Therefore \(p_e\nmid g_Q\), where \(g_Q\) can be chosen to be the positive rational gcd of the real differences. The normalized real difference for pair \(e\) remains divisible by \(p_e^a\), so its squared norm is divisible by \(p_e^{2a}\).

Gaussian unique factorization gives, for any represented \(d>0\),

\[
 r_2(d)=4\prod_{p\equiv1\ (4)}(v_p(d)+1). \tag{25}
\]

Thus every ordered pair in \(Q\), after its full common-ideal normalization, has reciprocal weight at most \(1/[4(2a+1)]\). This proves (20).

For a two-point subset the full normalized set is \(\{0,1\}\) up to a unit, and its kernel is instead exactly \(2/r_2(1)=1/2\). The restriction \(k\ge3\) in (20) is essential.

#### Proof of optimization over every ideal

Write \(p_e=\pi_e\bar\pi_e\). A real integer is divisible by \(\pi_e\) or by \(\bar\pi_e\) precisely when it is divisible by \(p_e\). Thus (24) implies that either of these Gaussian primes divides a difference of \(P\) only at the designated unordered pair \(e\).

There are two exhaustive possibilities for \(\beta\).

* If \(\beta\) has some assigned Gaussian prime factor \(\pi_e\) or \(\bar\pi_e\), only the two ordered versions of pair \(e\) can collide modulo \(\beta\). Each reciprocal weight is at most \(1/4\). Hence \(J_\beta\le1/2\).
* If \(\beta\) has none of the assigned Gaussian prime factors, every colliding difference keeps both factors above its own assigned \(p_e\) to exponent at least \(a\), even after division by \(\beta\). Its quotient norm has \(p_e\)-valuation at least \(2a\), and the sum is at most \(n(n-1)/[4(2a+1)]\).

This proves (21) for **all** Gaussian ideals, with no index or height restriction. Finally, take \(\beta=x_i-x_j\). It contains the assigned prime for pair \(\{i,j\}\), so exactly this pair collides, and its two quotient differences are \(\pm1\). Therefore \(J_\beta=1/2\). For \(a\ge n^2\), the other bound in (21) is less than \(1/2\), proving (22). QED.

### 3.1 What adaptivity can and cannot change in this example

Every actual ideal fiber selected after earlier similarities is an affine image of an actual subset of the original \(P\). Fully normalizing that subset gives the same set, up to a unit, as dividing its original differences by \(g_Q\). Consequently (20) holds at **every** adaptively selected node of size at least three, even if all subsets rather than just ideal fibers are allowed.

For example, let \(a=n^2\) and let \(n\) grow. Uniformly for \(3\le k=|Q|\le n\),

\[
 \frac{W(Q)}{k^2/\sqrt{\log k}}
 \le\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}{4(2n^2+1)}\longrightarrow0. \tag{26}
\]

There is no larger-than-pair normalized node at which a uniform inverse-\(r_2\) lower bound is restored. Optimizing (17) over all ideals, while retaining all actual fiber multiplicities, does not help either.

This still does not obstruct a support-sensitive method with a large-\(D\) alternative. Here \(K(P)=2/(n-1)\), and every pair already gives zero-loss actual contraction. The issue is exactly that many *unused ambient orientations* can suppress every reciprocal weight, while none of the actual distance labels coincide. Theorem 2 does not say that the kernel method fails after adding an appropriate low-\(D\) hypothesis and a separate argument for the other case.

## 4. What is not supplied by the proved obstructions

The first construction shows that even actual fibers with large collision multiplicity need not give a bounded-congestion injection from norm moduli into norm labels. The second shows that a common-ideal optimization does not universally repair a representation-weight lower bound. Neither theorem controls what happens in an arbitrary set with unbounded \(K\).

There remains a legitimate finite object which these arguments do not replace: **the actual norm support separately inside every fiber**. For a chosen prime \(\pi\), it consists of the integers

\[
 r_{c,d}=|\{(x,y)\in P_c^2:x\ne y,\ N(x-y)=d\}|,
 \qquad D(P_c)=\#\{d:r_{c,d}>0\}. \tag{27}
\]

The global collision mass only gives

\[
 \sum_{c,d}r_{c,d}=\sum_c n_c(n_c-1).
\]

It does not determine the fiber support counts in (27). Normalization relabels each fiber's support bijectively but does not turn an ambient representation count into its actual support. The following is one precise way to ask for the missing coupling.

## 5. Exactly one missing finite lemma

For bookkeeping at singleton leaves only, extend the actual squared-ratio potential by

\[
 \Phi(A)=
 \begin{cases}
 |A|^2/D(A)^2,&|A|\ge2,\\
 1,&|A|=1.
 \end{cases} \tag{28}
\]

This singleton convention is not a definition of a positive distance for a singleton.

For a Gaussian-primitive set \(P\) of size \(n\ge2\) and a Gaussian prime \(\pi\), let \(P_c\) be its nonempty actual residue fibers, \(n_c=|P_c|\), and \(\theta_c=n_c/n\). Define the two finite quantities

\[
 H_\pi(P)=-\sum_c\theta_c\log\theta_c,
 \qquad
 B_\pi(P)=\sum_c\theta_c\Phi(P_c). \tag{29}
\]

Equivalently, if \(s_1\) is the number of singleton fibers,

\[
 B_\pi(P)=\frac1n\left(s_1+
   \sum_{c:n_c\ge2}\frac{n_c^3}{\bigl(\#\{d:r_{c,d}>0\}\bigr)^2}\right). \tag{30}
\]

Thus these are quantities of an explicit finite actual endpoint/fiber table. No norm probability law or inverse representation weight occurs in the denominator.

### Missing lemma APH — adaptive prime-fiber Hartley drift (**UNPROVED**)

There exists an absolute constant \(C>0\) such that every Gaussian-primitive finite \(P\subset\mathcal O\), \(n=|P|\ge2\), has a Gaussian prime \(\pi\) with

\[
 2\le N(\pi)\le n
\]

for which

\[
 \boxed{\Phi(P)\le B_\pi(P)+C H_\pi(P).} \tag{APH}
\]

This is the **only proposed unproved finite lemma in this note**. It allows the prime to depend on the actual set. It does not assert a fixed-prime inequality. Its candidate family is finite and nonempty: \(1+i\) is available, and primitivity ensures that every candidate partition has at least two occupied fibers.

No proof of (APH) is supplied. It is a sufficient, support-sensitive assertion, not claimed to be equivalent to the original distinct-distance conjecture.

### 5.1 Why this single lemma would eliminate height

Normalize the root by its full common Gaussian ideal. At each nonsingleton node, apply (APH), retain the actual fibers as children, and fully normalize each child before continuing. Cardinality and \(\Phi\) are unchanged by normalization. Every split is proper, so this is a finite tree with singleton leaves.

If a node \(v\) contains \(n_v\) original points, its weight is \(n_v/n\). Independently of any conjecture, the occupied-point entropy budget telescopes exactly:

\[
 \sum_{v\text{ internal}}\frac{n_v}{n}H_v=\log n. \tag{31}
\]

Indeed \(n_vH_v=n_v\log n_v-\sum_{w\text{ child of }v}n_w\log n_w\); all internal terms cancel, and the singleton leaf terms are zero.

Multiply each instance of (APH) by its node weight and sum. The child potentials telescope, while the leaves contribute exactly one by (28). Thus (APH) would give

\[
 \Phi(P)\le1+C\log n,
 \qquad
 D(P)\ge\frac{n}{\sqrt{1+C\log n}}. \tag{32}
\]

This is only the consequence identifying why the finite lemma suffices; no conditional Lean theorem was written. The argument does not establish the stronger constant-loss half-cardinality contraction. In particular, a general stopping frontier can have a large entropy cost, so (31) is not an automatic bounded-cost halving theorem.

### 5.2 A proved reduction: bounded \(K\) is not the missing case

For **any** actual partition of a finite set \(P\) into nonempty subsets \(P_c\), one has

\[
 \Phi(P)-\sum_c\theta_c\Phi(P_c)
 \le2\Phi(P)\,H((\theta_c)_c). \tag{33}
\]

Indeed, \(D(P_c)\le D(P)\) for nonsingletons, while the singleton convention gives \(1\ge1/D(P)^2\). Therefore

\[
 \sum_c\theta_c\Phi(P_c)\ge\Phi(P)\sum_c\theta_c^3.
\]

Also

\[
 1-\sum_c\theta_c^3
 =\sum_c\theta_c(1-\theta_c^2)
 \le2\sum_c\theta_c(1-\theta_c)
 \le2H((\theta_c)_c),
\]

using \(-\log t\ge1-t\). This proves (33).

Consequently, on a class with \(K(P)^2\le B\), (APH) holds for every proper candidate partition with \(C=2B\). Both proved counterexample families above are in this already harmless range. The genuinely missing assertion is a uniform support charge when \(K(P)\) is unbounded, not a universal lower bound on an ambient inverse-representation kernel.

### 5.3 The precise support accuracy required

For a nonsingleton fiber put \(\lambda_c=D(P_c)/D(P)\). With \(s_1\) as in (30), (APH) is exactly

\[
 1-\sum_{c:n_c\ge2}\frac{\theta_c^3}{\lambda_c^2}
   -\frac{s_1}{n\Phi(P)}
 \le\frac{C H_\pi(P)}{\Phi(P)}. \tag{34}
\]

For large \(\Phi(P)\), this asks for a correspondingly accurate match between actual support loss and the occupied-point split. Merely controlling collision mass, or replacing \(\lambda_c\) by an ambient norm density, does not establish (34).

This is not a positive-order entropy surrogate for \(D\). The potential in (28)–(34) uses the **actual order-zero support**, and the only entropy is that of the occupied point fibers. The positive-order norm-entropy obstruction in `adaptive_ideal_entropy_obstruction.md` therefore cannot be ignored or used as a proof here. The non-autocorrelation profiles in `lattice_energy_obstruction.md` do not establish (APH) either.

Likewise, the fixed-mask result in `periodic_norm_two_scale.md` does not furnish (APH) at arbitrary finite heights. Its threshold depends on the modulus, and its proved centered-window contraction is not this prime-fiber support inequality. No modulus-uniform inference is made.

  \[
  \prod_{v\text{ internal}}
  \frac{n_v^{n_v}}{\prod_{w\text{ child of }v}n_w^{n_w}}

For example, the \(n=256\) translated-grid test has \(D=429\), all 197 proper ideal rows below \(n\), and ideal/norm matching numbers 26 and 23. These values only check the finite construction; the asymptotic obstruction is proved by (12)–(14), not inferred from a numerical trend.

All new obstruction proofs are elementary Gaussian divisibility, CRT, exact endpoint counting, and a separated-coordinate calculation. No unproved prime-distribution or orientation-equidistribution input is used.

**Proven:** (1)–(4), Theorems 1 and 2, the matching bounds, the all-subset normalized-kernel obstruction, the entropy identity (31), and the actual-partition inequality (33).

**Unproved:** the single finite assertion (APH). Equation (32) is its conditional consequence, not an established bound.

**Still unresolved:** the height-free sharp Gaussian distinct-distance bound, the unrestricted actual half-cardinality contraction, and the genuine sharp planar problem. The proved examples are not counterexamples to any of these. At no point is \(D(P)\le S(\operatorname{diam}(P)^2)\) reversed.
