# Balanced cube-neighbourhood packing: a pair-witness theorem and the general gap

## Status

**The arbitrary balanced-relation packing claim is not resolved here.** No
absolute constants are proved for general symmetric `B`, and no counterexample
to that claim is constructed. In particular, nothing here proves the original
Ramsey assertion.

The new positive result is a constant-density theorem for **pair-generated**
bad relations. It allows an arbitrary, nonlinear forbidden-pair graph, does
not assume host transitivity or translations, and needs no spare vertices:

> If a tuple is bad exactly when some pair of its coordinates belongs to a
> fixed symmetric relation `R`, and its one-coordinate bad probabilities are
> all `p <= 1/4`, then `H_d` has an avoiding injection whenever `N >= m`.

The proof is a pivotal-coordinate argument followed by an ordinary graph
switch. A quantitative approximate-balance version is also established.

An explicit, exactly balanced construction shows why this argument cannot
simply be applied to the pair-cylinder core of an arbitrary `B`: that core
can have maximum degree of order `p N/d`, not `p N/d^2`. This construction
**does have avoiding injections**, and is only a diagnostic for the proposed
critical-link decomposition.

Finally, an analytic counterexample audits a tempting block-switching
criterion in the corpus. No numerical search is used anywhere in this note.
`Submission/Spec.lean` is unchanged. These are paper proofs, not a Lean
formalization.

## 1. Pair-generated relations and a pivotal-coordinate bound

Let `R` be a symmetric relation on a finite set `Omega` of size `N`. Loops
are allowed at this stage. For `s >= 0`, let

\[
 B_s^R=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_s):\exists i<j,\ (x_i,x_j)\in R\},
 \qquad p_s=\Pr(B_s^R).
\]

All probabilities use independent uniform coordinates, including repeated
values. Put `B=B_d^R`, `p=p_d`, and

\[
 p_a=\Pr(B_d^R\mid X_1=a),\qquad
 b=\max_a p_a,\qquad \eta=b-p\ge0.
\]

Exact one-coordinate balance is the case `eta=0`, `b=p`.

### Lemma 1 (pivotal deletion)

\[
                  d(p_d-p_{d-1})\le 2p_d.                 \tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** On a bad `d`-tuple choose one witnessing pair of coordinate
positions. Deleting any coordinate outside that pair leaves the tuple bad.
Thus at most two coordinate deletions can turn a bad tuple into a good one.
By exchangeability, the expected number of such deletions is
`d(p_d-p_{d-1})`. This proves (1).

More generally, if a bad-tuple property is preserved by adjoining
coordinates and is generated by witnesses on at most `k` coordinate
positions, the same argument gives `d(p_d-p_{d-1}) <= k p_d`. This
statement does not require balance. The pair-specific input below is
stronger than this general pivotal bound.

## 2. Balance forces a small forbidden-pair maximum degree

For `a in Omega`, write

\[
 \delta_a={|R(a)|\over N},\qquad n=d-1,\qquad t=n\delta_a.
\]

Let `W_1,...,W_n` be independent uniform elements of `Omega`, and set

\[
 C=\{(W_1,\ldots,W_n)\notin B_n^R\},\qquad
 J=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{R(a)}(W_i),\qquad A=\{J\ge1\}.
\]

The probability increment contributed by the fixed coordinate `a` is

\[
 u_a:=\Pr(C\cap A)=p_a-p_{d-1}
       \le\eta+{2p\over d},                               \tag{2}
\]

using (1). This is where the balance of the **whole pair-generated event**
enters.

There are two useful lower bounds. First, `A` implies that the tuple with
first coordinate `a` is bad, so `Pr(A) <= b`. Since `J` is binomial,

\[
 E J=t,\qquad E J^2=t+(1-1/n)t^2\le t+t^2.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives `Pr(A) >= t/(1+t)` when `t>0`; the case `t=0` is
immediate. Consequently

\[
                         t\le {b\over1-b}.               \tag{3}
\]

Second, for every value `z`,

\[
 \Pr(B_n^R\mid W_i=z)\le b.                               \tag{4}
\]

Indeed, adjoining another independent coordinate can only create additional
forbidden pairs, and the resulting `d`-tuple conditional probability is at
most `b`. Hence

\[
 E[J\mathbf1_C]
  =\sum_i E[\mathbf1_{R(a)}(W_i)\Pr(C\mid W_i)]
  \ge (1-b)t.
\]

The integer inequality `1_{J>=1} >= J-binomial(J,2)`, multiplied by
`1_C`, now yields

\[
 \begin{split}
 u_a
 &\ge E[J\mathbf1_C]-E\binom J2\\
 &\ge (1-b)t-\tfrac12t^2\\
 &\ge \kappa(b)t,                                        \tag{5}\\
 \kappa(b)&:=1-b-{b\over2(1-b)}
             ={(1-2b)(2-b)\over2(1-b)}.
 \end{split}
\]

For `b<1/2`, `kappa(b)>0`. Combining (2) and (5) proves:

### Lemma 2 (quantitative maximum degree)

For a pair-generated relation, if `b<1/2`, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \max_a\delta_a\le
 {\eta+2p/d\over(d-1)\kappa(b)}.}                         \tag{6}
\]

In particular, under exact balance and `p<=1/4`,
`kappa(p)>=3/4-1/6=7/12`, so

\[
 \boxed{
 \max_a |R(a)|\le {24p\over7d(d-1)}N.}                   \tag{7}
\]

This is the essential improvement: merely using
`Pr(B | X_1=a) >= Pr(A)` gives only `O(pN/d)`. Pivotal deletion plus
balance supplies the extra factor `1/d`.

## 3. A complete constant-density packing theorem for pair witnesses

Let `F_d` be the graph on the even vertices of `Q_d` that joins vertices at
Hamming distance two. Every vertex has degree

\[
                            D=\binom d2.                 \tag{8}
\]

Two distinct even cube vertices belong to a common neighbourhood edge
precisely when their Hamming distance is two. Therefore an injection
`f:V(F_d)->Omega` avoiding every `R`-pair on an edge of `F_d` avoids
`B_d^R` on every neighbourhood edge of `H_d`.

We use the following graph packing criterion, with its switch checked
explicitly rather than invoking a hypergraph block switch.

### Lemma 3 (graph switch)

Two simple graphs `F,R_0` on `N` vertices pack if

\[
                         2\Delta(F)\Delta(R_0)<N.        \tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** Choose a bijection `f` minimizing the number of conflicting
edges. Suppose `uv` is a conflict. Define

\[
 \begin{split}
 A&=f^{-1}\left(\bigcup_{w\in N_F(u)}N_{R_0}(f(w))\right),\\
 S&=f^{-1}(N_{R_0}(f(u))),\qquad
 T=\bigcup_{w\in S}N_F(w).
 \end{split}
\]

Both `|A|` and `|T|` are at most `Delta(F)Delta(R_0)`. Moreover `u` lies
in both sets because `uv` is a conflict. Thus

\[
 |A\cup T\cup\{v\}|\le2\Delta(F)\Delta(R_0)<N.
\]

Choose `z` outside this union and swap the images of `u,z`.
Every edge `uw` with `w!=z` becomes nonconflicting because `z notin A`.
Every edge `zw` with `w!=u` becomes nonconflicting because `z notin T`.
The edge `uz`, if present, retains its unordered pair of images and cannot
be a newly created conflict. All other edges are unchanged. Since `z!=v`,
the original conflict `uv` is removed. This contradicts minimality. QED.

### Theorem 4 (pair-witness case of the packing claim)

Let `d>=2`, `m=2^(d-1)`, `N>=m`, and `B=B_d^R`. If `B` is exactly
one-coordinate balanced with `p<=1/4`, then there is an injection of the
vertices of `H_d` into `Omega` avoiding `B` on every edge.

**Proof.** Remove any loops from `R` to obtain a simple graph `R_0`, and
pad `F_d` with isolated vertices to order `N`. Removing loops is legitimate
because the desired map is injective. From (7)--(8),

\[
 {2D\Delta(R_0)\over N}
 \le {24p\over7}\le {6\over7}<1.
\]

Lemma 3 supplies a packing of `F_d,R_0`; restrict it to the `m` even cube
vertices. Every neighbourhood edge of `H_d` is pairwise at distance two,
so no pair in its image belongs to `R`. This is exactly avoidance of
`B_d^R`. QED.

The power-of-two condition on `d` is not needed for this theorem. For
`d=2`, duplicate neighbourhoods impose the same constraint and cause no
problem. For the pair-generated case `d=1`, the bad relation is empty.

The approximate-balance version follows directly from (6): it is enough
that `N>=m`, `b<1/2`, and

\[
                        d\eta+2p<\kappa(b).              \tag{10}
\]

Thus constant-density packing is stable under one-coordinate balance errors
of order `1/d`, not under arbitrary constant errors.

## 4. The exact gap for a critical-pair decomposition of arbitrary B

For an arbitrary symmetric `B subset Omega^d`, `d>=3`, define its full
pair-cylinder core by

\[
 (a,b)\in R_B
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (a,b,z_3,\ldots,z_d)\in B
 \text{ for every completion }(z_3,\ldots,z_d).
\]

Let `B^R=B_d^{R_B}` and `p_R=Pr(B^R)`. Then `B^R subset B`, but exact
balance of `B` gives only

\[
 p_R\le p,\qquad \max_a\Pr(B^R\mid X_1=a)\le p;
\]

it does **not** give exact balance of `B^R`. Applying the proof of Lemma 2
with this upper bound gives

\[
 \max_a {|R_B(a)|\over N}
 \le {p-p_R+2p_R/d\over(d-1)\kappa(p)},                   \tag{11}
\]

and therefore only

\[
 {2D\Delta(R_B)\over N}
 \le {d(p-p_R)+2p_R\over\kappa(p)}.                       \tag{12}
\]

The residual mass `p-p_R` is multiplied by `d`. Even when the core can be
packed, avoiding it need not avoid the residual relation. In particular,
conditioning an injection to avoid the core cannot be assumed to preserve
one-coordinate balance or the residual bad probability.

The next construction shows that the maximum-degree issue in (11) is real,
not just a weak estimate. It concerns a precisely defined critical-link
reduction, not a counterexample to the original packing assertion.

### 4.1 An exactly balanced B with a highly unbalanced pair core

Fix a power of two `d>=4` and an integer `q>=16`. Choose an integer `n`
divisible by `qd`, with

\[
 n\ge100qd^2,\qquad n\ge2m,
\]

and set `k=n/(qd)`. Let a type set `A` of order `n` consist of one special
type `0`, a set `S` of `k` types, and the remaining types `T`.

On type tuples, let `h` be the indicator of the event that the tuple
contains both `0` and at least one member of `S`. Put

\[
 p=1-\left(1-{1\over qd}\right)^{d-1}.
\]

Elementary two-term inclusion--exclusion gives

\[
                         {1\over2q}\le p\le {1\over q}. \tag{13}
\]

For `a!=0`, set

\[
 h_a=E[h(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\mid X_1=a],\qquad
 r=\left({n-1\over n}\right)^{d-1},\qquad
 w_a={p-h_a\over r},\qquad
 \bar w={1\over n-1}\sum_{a\ne0}w_a.
\]

Here `0<=h_a<d/n`. Explicitly, for `a in S`,

\[
 h_a=1-(1-1/n)^{d-1},
\]

whereas for `a in T`,

\[
 h_a=1-(1-1/n)^{d-1}-(1-k/n)^{d-1}
                  +(1-(k+1)/n)^{d-1}.
\]

Define a symmetric rational-valued function `W:A^d->[0,1]` by

\[
 W(a_1,\ldots,a_d)=
 \begin{cases}
 h(a_1,\ldots,a_d),&\text{some }a_i=0,\\
 \sum_i w_{a_i}-(d-1)\bar w,&\text{all }a_i\ne0.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{14}
\]

For an all-nonzero tuple, the second expression lies between

\[
 {p-d^2/n\over r}\quad\hbox{and}\quad {p+d^2/n\over r}.
\]

Indeed every `w_a` lies in `[(p-d/n)/r,p/r]`, as does `bar w`.
Our choices imply `d^2/n<=p/50`, `r>=1/2`, and `p<=1/16`. Thus the
second expression is strictly between zero and one.

The one-coordinate mean of `W` is exactly `p` for every type. At type
`0` this is the definition of `p`. At type `a!=0`, the contribution from
`h` is `h_a`; the all-nonzero event has conditional probability `r`,
and the conditional mean of the second line of (14) is `w_a`. Therefore

\[
                       E[W\mid X_1=a]=h_a+r w_a=p.       \tag{15}
\]

To turn this into an actual zero-one relation, choose an integer `L` such
that all `L W(a_1,...,a_d)` are integers, and use

\[
                         \Omega=A\times\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z.
\]

A tuple `((a_i,t_i))_{i=1}^d` belongs to `B` precisely when the standard
representative of `sum_i t_i mod L` belongs to

\[
                   \{0,\ldots,LW(a_1,\ldots,a_d)-1\}.
\]

For each fixed type tuple, the bad probability is exactly `W`. Conditional
on any single full coordinate `(a_i,t_i)`, the sum of the remaining tags is
uniform, so (15) proves **exact one-coordinate balance** of this zero-one
relation. It is invariant under all coordinate permutations.

Its full pair-cylinder core is exactly the complete bipartite relation
between

\[
                  \{0\}\times\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z
       \quad\hbox{and}\quad S\times\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z.
\]

Such a pair forces `h=1` under every completion. Conversely, a pair of
nonzero types has an all-nonzero completion with `W<1`; a free tag can be
chosen to make that completion good. A pair involving `0` but no type in
`S` has a completion with no type in `S`, where `W=0`.

Consequently, with `N=nL`,

\[
                 {\Delta(R_B)\over N}={k\over n}
                            ={1\over qd}\ge {p\over d}. \tag{16}
\]

This is a factor of at least `d` larger than `p/d^2`. The ratio `N/m` can
be made arbitrarily large, and `p` arbitrarily small. This only disproves
an attempted maximum-degree deduction for the core, **not** the packing
claim.

For clarity, these examples actually have avoiding injections. There are
at least `m` types in `T`. On tuples all of whose types are in `T`, (14) is
a single constant `u_T<1`. Choose `d` tags whose sum is outside its bad
interval. Assign these tags to the `d` strong syndrome classes of `H_d`,
and assign distinct types from `T` to its `m` vertices. This is an injection,
and each neighbourhood has the same good tag sum.

### 4.2 What a completion would still have to prove

A general proof along this route needs an operation that deals with
unbalanced dense critical links **and simultaneously controls the residual
bad relation on the resulting actual injections**. Equations (11)--(16)
show why applying the pair-generated theorem to the core is not such an
operation. Deleting high-degree core vertices or conditioning on core
avoidance is not known here to preserve a useful residual estimate.

No all-orders assertion, no covariance-to-avoidance inference, and no
unproved global pressure estimate is used to bridge this gap.

## 5. Corpus audit: a proposed block-switching criterion cannot be used

The corpus contains Konarski--Żak--Woźniak, *A note on packing of uniform
hypergraphs*, arXiv:1802.05051v1, file
`/corpus/src/1802.05051/1802.05051.tex`.

Its displayed theorem labelled `beta` states that `k`-graphs `H_1,H_2`
on `n` vertices pack if, for some `0<beta<k`,

\[
 \Delta_\beta(H_1)\Delta_{k-\beta}(H_2)
 +\Delta_{k-\beta}(H_1)\Delta_\beta(H_2)
 <\binom n\beta-\binom k\beta+2.                         \tag{17}
\]

The proposed proof switches a whole `beta`-set but counts only edges
containing that entire set. Edges meeting the switched set in a nonempty
proper subset can also become conflicts. The following argument disproves
(17) **as stated in that corpus version**; it is not merely an objection
to the proof.

Take `k=4`, `beta=2`, and `n=4^t`. Let `H_1` be the affine lines in
`F_4^t`. Every line has four vertices and every pair determines one line,
so

\[
            \Delta_2(H_1)=1,\qquad e(H_1)={n(n-1)\over12}.
\]

Let `H_2` be a random `4`-graph with independent edge probability `1/4`.
Write `M=binomial(n-2,2)`. Chernoff's inequality and a union bound imply

\[
 \Pr(\Delta_2(H_2)>M/3)\le\binom n2 e^{-M/84}=o(1).
\]

But the probability that `H_1,H_2` pack is at most

\[
 n!\,(3/4)^{n(n-1)/12}
 \le\exp\left(n\log n-{\log(4/3)\over12}n(n-1)\right)
 =o(1).
\]

Thus for sufficiently large `n=4^t`, there is an `H_2` for which the two
hypergraphs do not pack, although the left side of (17) is at most

\[
 {2\over3}\binom{n-2}{2}<\binom n2-4,
\]

which is exactly its asserted threshold. The difference on the last line
is `(n^2+7n-36)/6>0` for `n>=4`.

This counterexample has a different target, with quadratically many edges;
it does **not** refute the cube-neighbourhood claim. It does rule out using
(17) as a general black-box justification for a half-edge switch. The
ordinary graph switch in Section 3 explicitly accounts for every changed
edge and is unaffected by this issue.

## Final conclusion

- **Proved:** for pair-generated `B`, exact one-coordinate balance and
  `p<=1/4` suffice at `N>=m`; arbitrary nonlinear `R` is allowed.
- **Proved:** the approximate-balance criterion (10) and the pivotal bound
  for bounded-size witnesses.
- **Proved diagnostic:** arbitrary balanced `B` can have a full pair-cylinder
  core of degree at least `pN/d`; its balance cannot simply be inherited.
- **Audited:** the tempting general block-switching criterion (17) in the
  cited corpus preprint is false as stated.
- **Unresolved:** the original constant-density packing assertion for
  arbitrary symmetric, exactly one-coordinate balanced `B`.
