# Erdős 371: an actual-arithmetic predictable-hit obstruction

## Outcome and scope

**The proposed unsigned estimate is neither proved nor disproved here.**
In particular, this note does not claim that Erdős 371 is false.

There is, however, a quantitative obstruction for the **actual prime
factors of consecutive integers**, not a probabilistic countermodel.
In the fixed power band

\[
                 X^{3/4}<p\le X^{9/10},                 \tag{0}
\]

there are at least

\[
       (\log(27/25)-o(1))X
\]

indices whose largest-prime hit is already completely determined by the
preceding prime-factor history. This remains true if the two preceding
factor lists are supplied **only up to interchange of the endpoints**.
Even the still coarser history consisting just of the unlabelled hits
leaves at most `(log X)^4` possible indices on these events.

Consequently:

1. The conditional-L2 majorant of the requested feedback, obtained by
   conditioning on that reflection quotient, is at least
   `(log(27/25)-o(1)) X^{3/4}`. It diverges, rather than tending to zero.
2. The sum of the mutual informations between a hit and the preceding
   **unlabelled hit history** in (0) is at least
   `(3/4 log(27/25)-o(1)) log X`, and is `O(log X)`.
3. Even conditioning only on the two current truncated winners gives a
   hit-prediction energy bounded away from zero in another fixed power
   band. The explicit lower limit obtained below is `0.03313817...`.

These statements disprove specific strengthened intermediate estimates
for martingale, entropy, or independent-resampling approaches. They do
**not** disprove the requested estimate: in that estimate the signed
spatial average is taken **before** squaring. The distinction is essential.
An argument exploiting cancellation in those spatial averages, rather
than bounding them by these conditional energies, is not excluded.

No Lean file was read or changed. In particular `Spec.lean` was not used.
The only asymptotic analytic inputs below are the ordinary prime Mertens
estimates and Chebyshev's upper bound for the number of primes. There is
no prime-pair asymptotic, no prime-step graph expansion, and no assumed
joint independence.

## 1. Feedback and the genuinely nested history

Let `N` be uniform on `{1,...,X}`; expectations in this note refer to this
finite probability space. For a prime `r` put

\[
 L_r(N)=1_{r\mid N+1}-1_{r\mid N},\qquad H_r=|L_r|.
\]

For an odd prime `p`, let `a_{p^-}` be the sign in the question. It is
always `+1` or `-1`: prime 2 hits exactly one endpoint, and two consecutive
integers cannot have the same truncated largest prime greater than 1.
The nested winner is precisely the label of the last prime that hit:

\[
 a_r=(1-H_r)a_{r^-}+L_r.                                  \tag{1}
\]

Thus the filtration generated by the histories `(a_r,H_r)`, `r<p`, is
exactly the ternary labelled-hit filtration

\[
 \mathcal F_p=\sigma(L_r:r<p),
\]

since `L_r=H_r a_r`. This is not a filtration of all prime residues: it
retains only which endpoint, if either, each earlier prime divides.

We also use two coarser histories. Define the squarefree kernels

\[
 d_{0,p}=\prod_{r<p,\ r\mid N}r,\quad
 d_{1,p}=\prod_{r<p,\ r\mid N+1}r,\quad D_p=d_{0,p}d_{1,p},
\]

and put

\[
 \mathcal U_p=\sigma(H_r:r<p)=\sigma(D_p),\qquad
 \mathcal G_p=\sigma(\{d_{0,p},d_{1,p}\}).                  \tag{2}
\]

The braces in `G_p` mean an unordered pair, with multiplicity. Therefore
`G_p` is the labelled history modulo simultaneous interchange of the two
endpoints. It is reflection-even as factor data. All three are increasing
histories in the prime cutoff, and `U_p` is coarser than `G_p`, which is
coarser than `F_p`.

Write

\[
 q_p=\mathbb E H_p
     =\frac{\lfloor X/p\rfloor+\lfloor(X+1)/p\rfloor}{X}
     =\frac2p+O(1/X),
 \qquad Z_p=a_{p^-}(H_p-2/p).
\]

For a prime set `I`, the requested unweighted energy is

\[
 \mathcal E_I(X)=\sum_{p\in I}\frac{|u_X(p)|^2}{p}
               =\sum_{p\in I}p\,|\mathbb E Z_p|^2.       \tag{3}
\]

Conditioning and then using Jensen gives the legitimate but potentially
very wasteful majorant

\[
 \mathcal E_I(X)\le
 \mathcal K_{\mathcal G,I}(X):=
 \sum_{p\in I}p\,\mathbb E
       |\mathbb E(Z_p\mid\mathcal G_p)|^2.                \tag{4}
\]

Theorem 1 below proves that the right side of (4) diverges for actual
integers in (0).

For comparison, let

\[
 m_p^{\mathcal A}=\mathbb E(H_p\mid\mathcal A_p),\qquad
 \mathcal V_{\mathcal A,I}(X)=
       \sum_{p\in I}p\,\mathbb E(m_p^{\mathcal A}-2/p)^2. \tag{5}
\]

Since `a_{p^-}` is `F_p`-measurable and has square 1,

\[
 \mathcal K_{\mathcal F,I}=\mathcal V_{\mathcal F,I}.
\]

The exact martingale-difference part contributes **nothing** to the
spatially averaged feedback:

\[
 \mathbb E[a_{p^-}(H_p-m_p^{\mathcal F})]=0,\qquad
 u_X(p)=p\,\mathbb E[a_{p^-}(m_p^{\mathcal F}-2/p)].        \tag{6}
\]

The problem in this decomposition is the predictable drift, not the
martingale innovation. The following results quantify why its ordinary
conditional energy is the wrong bound.

## 2. A positive-density supply of already-determined hits

The argument works for fixed constants

\[
       \tfrac12<b<c<1,\qquad b<c^2.
\]

Set

\[
 I=(X^b,X^c]\cap\{\text{primes}\},\qquad
 \kappa=\log(c^2/b)>0,\qquad \delta=(1-c)/4.
\]

### Lemma 1 (large-prime band mass)

For every fixed `1/2<t<1`,

\[
 \#\{m\le X:P(m)>X^t\}
          =X\log(1/t)+O_t(X/\log X).                      \tag{7}
\]

Indeed an integer at most X has at most one prime factor greater than
`X^t`. Count its multiples and use

\[
 \sum_{X^t<p\le X}\frac1p=\log(1/t)+O_t(1/\log X),\qquad
 \pi(X)\ll X/\log X.
\]

Let `p_*(n)=max(P(n),P(n+1))`. A union bound, used in the favorable
direction, now gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 \#\{n\le X:X^b<p_*(n)\le X^c\}
 &\ge \#\{n\le X:P(n)>X^b\}\\
 &\quad-\#\{n\le X:P(n)>X^c\}
       -\#\{n\le X:P(n+1)>X^c\}\\
 &\ge \kappa X-O_{b,c}(X/\log X).                        \tag{8}
 \end{split}
\]

Changing the argument from `n` to `n+1` costs only one endpoint here.
No lower bound for a two-prime correlation is being assumed.

### Lemma 2 (squarefull loss is small on almost all indices)

Let

\[
 \Delta(n)=\log\frac{n(n+1)}{\operatorname{rad}(n(n+1))}.
\]

Then uniformly in `X>=1`,

\[
                    \mathbb E\Delta(N)<9.               \tag{9}
\]

To prove it, put

\[
 C_0=\sum_{r\ {
m prime}}\sum_{j\ge2}\frac{\log r}{r^j}
    =\sum_r\frac{\log r}{r(r-1)}<3.
\]

For the last bound, replace primes by all integers and use the decreasing
function `log t/t^2` on `[2,infinity)`:

\[
 C_0\le 2\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{\log m}{m^2}
 \le 2\left(\frac{\log2}{4}
                    +\int_2^\infty\frac{\log t}{t^2}\,dt\right)
 =1+\tfrac32\log2<3.
\]

The identity

\[
 \log(m/\operatorname{rad}m)
      =\sum_{r}\sum_{j\ge2}(\log r)1_{r^j\mid m}
\]

and elementary counts of multiples give
`E Delta <= (2+1/X) C_0 < 9`, proving (9). Hence at most
`9X/(delta log X)` indices have `Delta>delta log X`.

We will also use the elementary moment bound

\[
 \mathbb E\,2^{\omega(N(N+1))}\ll(\log X)^3.              \tag{9a}
\]

Indeed `4^{omega(m)}<=tau_4(m)` prime-power by prime-power, and

\[
 \sum_{m\le Y}\tau_4(m)
 =\sum_{abc\le Y}\left\lfloor\frac{Y}{abc}\right\rfloor
 \le Y\left(\sum_{a\le Y}\frac1a\right)^3
 \le Y(1+\log Y)^3.
\]

Since consecutive integers are coprime, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \mathbb E\,2^{\omega(N(N+1))}
 =\mathbb E[2^{\omega(N)}2^{\omega(N+1)}]
 \le\big(\mathbb E4^{\omega(N)}\,
          \mathbb E4^{\omega(N+1)}\big)^{1/2}\ll(\log X)^3.
\]

Consequently all but `O(X/log X)` indices satisfy
`2^{omega(n(n+1))}<=(log X)^4`. This will give a polylogarithmic, rather
than just subpower, bound on the number of admissible factor assignments
on the good hits.

### Lemma 3 (good hits)

There is a set `B_X` of indices, each assigned the unique prime `p_*(n)`
in I, such that

\[
 |B_X|\ge\kappa X-O_{b,c}(X/\log X)-X^{1-\delta},          \tag{10}
\]

and for every `n in B_X`, with `p=p_*(n)`,

\[
 H_p(n)=1,\qquad D_p(n)\ge X^{1+\delta}>2X+1,
 \qquad 2^{\omega(D_p(n))}\le(\log X)^4.                  \tag{11}
\]

for all sufficiently large X.

Remove from (8) the indices `n<X^{1-delta}`, those with
`Delta(n)>delta log X`, and those with
`2^{omega(n(n+1))}>(log X)^4`. At every remaining index,

\[
 \operatorname{rad}(n(n+1))\ge X^{2-3\delta}.
\]

Since p is the largest prime of the two endpoints, all the other primes
are less than p, and exactly

\[
 D_p=\operatorname{rad}(n(n+1))/p
       \ge X^{2-3\delta-c}=X^{1+\delta}.
\]

Also p divides exactly one endpoint, and `D_p` divides
`rad(n(n+1))`, so the imposed bound on the number of distinct factors
implies `2^{omega(D_p)}<=(log X)^4`. This proves (10)--(11).
The loss term in (10) includes the explicit loss `9X/(delta log X)`
and the `O(X/log X)` loss from (9a).
For `b=3/4,c=9/10`, one has

\[
 \kappa=\log(27/25)=0.0769610411\ldots,\qquad\delta=1/40.
\]

## 3. Rigidity of the actual histories

### Lemma 4 (three support bounds)

1. On `D_p>X`, every atom of `F_p` is a singleton.
2. On `D_p>2X+1`, every atom of `G_p` is a singleton.
3. On `D_p>X`, every atom of `U_p` has cardinality at most
   \[
   2^{\omega(D_p)}\le\exp(4\log X/\log\log X)             \tag{12}
   \]
   for all sufficiently large X. On the good hits (11), the stronger
   bound `(log X)^4` holds.

**Proof.** The two squarefree integers `d_0,d_1` are coprime. With their
labels fixed, any two members of the same atom obey

\[
 n\equiv0\pmod {d_0},\qquad n\equiv-1\pmod {d_1}.
\]

They are congruent modulo `D=d_0 d_1`. If `D>X`, there is at most one
such member of `[1,X]`. This proves the first assertion.

If the labels are interchanged, the other index would obey

\[
                       n'\equiv-n-1\pmod D.
\]

Thus `D` would divide `n+n'+1`, a positive integer at most `2X+1`.
That is impossible if `D>2X+1`. This proves the second assertion: even
the unordered split between the endpoints already determines the index.

For the third assertion, `D` alone imposes `D|n(n+1)`. A squarefree D
has exactly `2^{omega(D)}` roots of this congruence modulo D, and each
root has at most one representative in `[1,X]` when `D>X`. Restrictions
from the absent primes can only reduce this number. Finally
`D<=X(X+1)`. If `k=omega(D)`, then `D>=(k+1)!`; the elementary bound
`log(k!)>=k log k-k` gives
`k<=4 log X/log log X` for large X. Multiplying by `log 2<1` proves
(12). The bound is uniform over all the atoms. ∎

This is a positive-prefix support phenomenon, not an iid or CRT-limit
calculation. The CRT reflected representative lies outside the prefix.
There are linearly many good hits in Lemma 3, rather than just a finite
or sparse family of constructed examples.

## 4. A divergent conditional feedback majorant

### Theorem 1

For I and kappa as above,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal K_{\mathcal G,I}(X)
       \ge(\kappa-o(1))X^b,\qquad
 \mathcal V_{\mathcal F,I}(X)
       \ge(\kappa-o(1))X^b.
 \quad}                                                   \tag{13}
\]

In particular the conditional majorant (4) for (0) is at least
`(log(27/25)-o(1)) X^{3/4}`.

**Proof.** Partition `B_X` into the disjoint subsets `B_{X,p}` according
to its largest prime. On every `n in B_{X,p}`, Lemma 4 shows that both
`G_p` and `F_p` have singleton atoms. Consequently

\[
 \mathbb E(Z_p\mid\mathcal G_p)(n)
   =a_{p^-}(n)(1-2/p),\qquad m_p^{\mathcal F}(n)=1.
\]

Since `a_{p^-}^2=1`, each such index contributes
`p(1-2/p)^2/X` to either energy. Summing and using `p>X^b` gives

\[
 \mathcal K_{\mathcal G,I},\ \mathcal V_{\mathcal F,I}
 \ge X^b(1-2X^{-b})^2\frac{|B_X|}{X}.
\]

Now apply (10). ∎

Thus even the **reflection-even quotient of the nested factor history**
is too informative for this conditional-L2 substitution. It is not
valid to argue that forgetting the absolute endpoint labels automatically
leaves a small conditional feedback energy.

The nonvanishing is not just an L2 tail with no independent-resampling
consequence. For example

\[
 \sum_{p\in I}\operatorname{TV}
   \big(\mathcal L(\mathcal F_p,H_p),\,
        \mathcal L(\mathcal F_p)\otimes\operatorname{Bern}(2/p)\big)
 \ge\kappa-o(1).                                         \tag{14}
\]

On the singleton atoms in `B_{X,p}`, the actual conditional hit law is
`Bern(1)`, whereas the independently resampled law is `Bern(2/p)`.
Their variation distance is `1-2/p`. Summing proves (14). Resampling a
prime hit independently while retaining its actual past therefore fails
by a nonvanishing aggregate amount in this power band.

## 5. The obstruction persists in the completely unlabelled hit process

### Theorem 2

For I and kappa as above,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sum_{p\in I} I(H_p;\mathcal U_p)
    \ge b\kappa\log X-4\kappa\log\log X-O_{b,c}(1),
 \quad}                                                   \tag{15}
\]

where logarithms and information use natural units. Also

\[
 \sum_{p\in I} I(H_p;\mathcal U_p)
                    \le2(c-b)\log X+O_{b,c}(1).           \tag{16}
\]

In particular this sum is `Theta(log X)` for actual unlabelled hits in
(0), with lower coefficient

\[
       \tfrac34\log(27/25)=0.05772078085\ldots.
\]

Moreover the hit-prediction energy using just this unlabelled past
satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal V_{\mathcal U,I}(X)
       \ge(\kappa-o(1))\frac{X^b}{(\log X)^4}.
 \quad}                                                   \tag{17}
\]

**Proof of (15).** Put `K_X=(log X)^4` and define the `U_p`-measurable
event

\[
 A_p=\{D_p>X,\ 2^{\omega(D_p)}\le K_X\}.
\]

It contains all the good hits in (11). On an atom of `U_p` in `A_p`, put
`v=Pr(H_p=1 | U_p)`. If `v>0`, Lemma 4 and the uniform distribution on
`[1,X]` give `v>=1/K_X`. The binary relative entropy satisfies

\[
 d(v\|q)\ge v\log(v/q)-v,                                \tag{18}
\]

because `(1-v)log(1-v)>=-v` and `-log(1-q)>=0`.
Here `q=q_p<=3/p`, so on these atoms

\[
 d(v\|q_p)\ge
       v(\log p-\log K_X-\log3-1).
\]

The coefficient is positive uniformly for `p>X^b` when X is large.
On all other atoms the divergence is nonnegative. Therefore

\[
 I(H_p;\mathcal U_p)
 \ge(\log p-\log K_X-\log3-1)
       \Pr(H_p=1,A_p).                                   \tag{19}
\]

By (10)--(11), the sum of the probabilities in (19) over I is at least
`kappa-O_{b,c}(1/log X)-X^{-delta}`: it counts all the disjoint good
largest-prime hits, and possibly other hits as well. Use `log p>=b log X`
and `log K_X=4 log log X`. The error from the lost `O(X/log X)` indices
is `O_{b,c}(1)`, and `X^{-delta} log X` is bounded and tends to zero.
This proves (15).

**Proof of (16).** Mutual information is at most the entropy of `H_p`.
Uniformly for `p<=X^c`, `q_p=2/p+O(1/X)` and

\[
 h(q_p)=\frac2p(\log p-\log2+1)
            +O(1/p^2)+O((\log X)/X).
\]

Sum this over I. The accumulated last error is `o(1)` by Chebyshev's
bound, and the ordinary estimates

\[
 \sum_{p\in I}\frac{\log p}{p}=(c-b)\log X+O_{b,c}(1),
 \qquad\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p=\log(c/b)+O_{b,c}(1/\log X)
\]

give (16).

**Proof of (17).** On the same atoms with `v>0`,

\[
 (v-2/p)^2\ge v^2(1-2K_X/p)^2
       \ge\frac{v}{K_X}(1-2K_X/X^b)^2.
\]

For large X the factors in parentheses are positive. Sum over those
atoms, multiply by p, and then sum over I. Equations (10)--(12) give

\[
 \mathcal V_{\mathcal U,I}\ge
 \frac{X^b}{K_X}(1-2K_X/X^b)^2
       \left(\kappa-O_{b,c}(1/\log X)-X^{-\delta}\right),
\]

which proves (17). ∎

Thus a claim that the actual prime hits have a vanishing aggregate
predictability or a sub-logarithmic total information cost in every
fixed power band is false **even without any endpoint labels**.
The large information here need not be directional information about the
winner. An entropy argument that isolates a smaller, genuinely signed
quantity could avoid this obstruction; (15) is not a lower bound for
`sum I(a_{p^-};H_p)`.

## 6. A coarser current-winner prediction obstruction

There is also a nonvanishing calibration at the level of the current
truncated winners, not only their entire histories.
Let `W_p` be either the ordered pair
`(P_{p^-}(N),P_{p^-}(N+1))` or its unordered version, and define
`m_p^W=E(H_p|W_p)`. Take

\[
 X^{9/10}<p\le X^{19/20},\qquad h=89/100,
 \qquad\gamma=2\log(2h)-1>0.
\]

Let B be the event that **both** full largest primes lie in
`(sqrt(X+1),X^h]`. The one-point count used in (7), followed by the union
bound, gives

\[
                \Pr(B)\ge\gamma-O(1/\log X).              \tag{20}
\]

For the indicated p, B is the same as the event that both truncated
winners lie in that interval. Indeed a truncated prime greater than
`sqrt(X+1)` cannot coexist in the same endpoint with a prime at least p.
Every `W_p` atom meeting B therefore has `H_p=0` throughout it. Thus
`m_p^W=0` on B, and

\[
 \begin{split}
 \liminf_{X\to\infty}
 \sum_{X^{9/10}<p\le X^{19/20}}p\,
       \mathbb E(m_p^W-2/p)^2
 &\ge4\gamma\log(19/18)\\
 &=0.03313817376\ldots>0.                                \tag{21}
 \end{split}
\]

For the ordered pair, `a_{p^-}` is already measurable and has square 1,
so (21) also applies to the corresponding conditional-feedback majorant.
For the unordered pair, (21) is a statement about hit prediction only.
It is not asserted to be a lower bound for a conditional signed feedback.

This makes precise a residual issue with using only the current winner
state: unlabelled hits still have size-exclusion calibration. Of course
a signed average over this symmetric excluded region could cancel. The
estimate (21) deliberately does not discard that distinction.

## 7. Direct audit of the high-prime feedback

Here is the direct arithmetic expression that was tested before passing
to the obstructions. It is recorded to specify what remains unestimated,
not offered as another sufficient criterion.

Let `p>sqrt(X+1)` be odd and `M=floor(X/p)`. Around `kp`, the old largest
prime is exactly `P(k)`. The two hits pair to give

\[
 \begin{split}
 T_p:=\sum_{n\le X}a_{p^-}(n)H_p(n)
  &=2\sum_{2\le k\le M}
       \big(F_{k,p}(kp-1)-F_{k,p}(kp+1)\big)\\
  &\quad+1_{p\mid X+1}a_{p^-}(X),                         \tag{22}\\
 F_{k,p}(m)&=1_{P_{p^-}(m)<P(k)}.
 \end{split}
\]

The `k=1` pair is zero: `p-1` and `p+1` both have the earlier prime 2.
For `k>=2`, equality of either neighboring truncated maximum with `P(k)`
is impossible by coprimality. This justifies the factor 2 in (22).

There is the exact expansion, for `m=kp+/-1`,

\[
 F_{k,p}(m)=1_{P(m)<P(k)}+
 \sum_{r<k,\ r\mid m,\ P(r)<P(k)}
                          1_{m/r\text{ prime}>p}.         \tag{23}
\]

There is at most one prime factor greater than p. Its cofactor is less
than k; the equality case would force `k(q-p)=+/-1` and is impossible.
Formula (23) exhibits the two sign orientations that must actually be
estimated. Neither a one-prime marginal nor the above support rigidity
estimates their signed difference when averaged over k and then squared
over power-sized p. No bound proving the requested `o(1)` was obtained
for this term.

### Finite normalization check

The exact recursion is

\[
 S_p=S_{p^-}-T_p+1_{p\mid X+1}
    =(1-2/p)S_{p^-}-R_p+1_{p\mid X+1}.
\]

There is a harmless finite distinction in the weight normalization:

\[
 \sum_{2<p\le X+1}\frac{2w_X(p)}p=1-w_X(2),\qquad
 \sum_{2\le p\le X+1}\frac{2w_X(p)}p=1.                  \tag{24}
\]

Thus the asserted Cauchy--Schwarz bound with `sqrt(J/2)` remains correct
(the exact odd-prime factor is even a little smaller). This distinction
does not resolve or invalidate the proposed sufficient hypothesis.

### Precise remaining issue

The following three strengthened assertions are now rigorously ruled out
for actual consecutive integers:

* vanishing conditional feedback energy after conditioning on the
  reflection quotient of the nested factor history, by (13);
* sub-logarithmic aggregate information in the preceding unlabelled hits,
  by (15);
* vanishing hit-prediction energy even from just the current two winners,
  by (21).

The requested assertion (3) is **not** among them. To prove it one must
retain cancellation in `E[a_{p^-}(m_p^F-2/p)]`, or estimate the actual
oriented cofactor sums (22)--(23), rather than replacing these averages
by any of the conditional absolute-square bounds just disproved.
A refined signed entropy or arithmetic large-sieve argument is still
possible in principle. No such estimate, no counterexample to (3), and
no proof or disproof of Erdős 371 has been obtained in this attempt.
