The results below must not be used as an unconditional proof of that limit.

## A power-saving lower bound from Cartesian incidences

Let `A` be a finite subset of `F_p^*`, and put `m = |A|` and

    T(A) = #{(a,b,c) in A^3 : ac = ab + b^2}.

The following deduction uses the published Stevens–de Zeeuw Cartesian-product
incidence theorem. It was independently checked twice in this investigation.
It is stronger than the square-root support bound stated in the checked 2024
factorial paper. No claim of bibliographic priority is made.

For `a in A`, put

    r(a) = #{b in A : b + b^2/a in A}.

Then `T = sum r(a)` and Cauchy–Schwarz gives `T^2 <= m sum r(a)^2`.
The diagonal `b = b'` in this last sum contributes exactly `T`. For `b != b'`,
put `rho = b'/b`. Eliminating the common first coordinate gives

    c' = rho^2 c + b' - b'^2/b.

The set `L` of distinct lines generated by all such ordered pairs satisfies

    m(m-1)/2 <= |L| <= m(m-1).

Indeed the slope fixes at most two possible nonzero ratios `rho`; since
`rho != 1`, the intercept `rho*b*(1-rho)` then determines `b` and `b'`.
There is no hidden multiplicity from the eliminated coordinate:

    a = b^2/(c-b),

and `c-b != 0` in every genuine solution. Some incidences may reconstruct an
`a` outside `A`, but dropping that condition only increases the count. Thus

    sum r(a)^2 <= T + 2 I(A x A, L).

### The non-elementary incidence input

Stevens–de Zeeuw, *An Improved Point-Line Incidence Bound Over Arbitrary Fields*,
arXiv:1609.06284, Cartesian theorem (local source
`/corpus/src/1609.06284/1609.06284.tex`, lines 153–161), states

    I(U x V, L) << |U|^(3/4) |V|^(1/2) |L|^(3/4) + |L|

when `|U| <= |V|`, `|U| |V|^2 <= |L|^3`, and, in characteristic `p`,
`|U| |L| << p^2`. There is no extra prohibition on parallel lines or pencils.
For `U=V=A`, `m>=3`, the first two conditions hold since `|L|>=m`.
The characteristic condition follows from `m^3 << p^2`. Therefore

    I(A x A, L) << m^(11/4),
    T^2 << mT + m^(15/4) << m^(15/4),
    T << m^(15/8).

The last step also uses `T <= m^2`. Bounded `m` is handled by an absolute
constant. This estimate for arbitrary `A` needs the stated size restriction;
it is false without one, as `A=F_p^*` shows.

### Factorial consequence

For `0 <= n <= p-3`, the three values

    a=n!, b=(n+1)!, c=(n+2)!

belong to the original factorial image, including `0!=1!`. They satisfy
`ac=ab+b^2`. Their ratio `b/a=n+1` distinguishes the `p-2` triples, so

    p-2 <= T(A_p).

If `m <= c p^(2/3)` for a suitable fixed positive `c`, the incidence bound
applies and gives `m >> p^(8/15)`. Otherwise the same conclusion is already
implied by the stronger lower bound `m > c p^(2/3)`. Hence

    |A_p| >> p^(8/15).

An independent published black-box route is the affine-energy incidence
inequality in Petridis–Roche-Newton–Rudnev–Warren, *An Energy Bound in the
Affine Group*, arXiv:1911.03401, Theorem 1.1. For
`G={x |-> b^2 x+b : b in A}`, its affine energy is at most `3m^2`.
That theorem gives the global estimate

    T(A) << m^(15/8) + m^(3/2) + m^(5/2)/sqrt(p),

which yields the same factorial exponent. The elementary reduction does not
assume either published theorem in Lean.

## Remaining mathematical gap

A sufficient route, now fully reduced in Lean, is

    S_r(p) / p -> 1/r!  for every fixed r >= 2,
    S_r(p) = sum_v choose(#{1 <= n < p : n! = v mod p}, r).

No uniformity as `r` grows is required for the Bonferroni limit step. However,
even the second-order asymptotic remains unproved. Its collisions involve
index gaps of order `p`; fixed-degree polynomial or fixed-gap prime-splitting
estimates do not control them. The power-saving support lower bound cannot
be reversed into an upper bound on collision energy.

Other investigated approaches do not supply the missing estimate:

* Exact time evolution and Wilson reflection generate a virtually cyclic
  orbit graph, not a uniformly expanding graph. Matched affine line
  compositions telescope, so their higher energies do not automatically
  flatten.
* Every fixed-length local tuple-cover condition can hold with `o(p)` value
  support. Even `1-o(1)` transition consistency permits artificial small-support
  examples. The exact global gluing must be retained.
* Gross–Koblitz identities concern normalized p-adic Gauss units. Complex
  Gauss-sum phase equidistribution is not a theorem about those reductions.
* Real metric Poisson theorems for lacunary sequences cannot be transferred
  automatically to the prime grid; the required modular terms are precisely
  factorial collision counts.
* Adjacent transpositions change one prefix product, but a Stein/switching
  argument still needs an unproved long-range, occupancy-weighted drift or
  decorrelation estimate.

None of these limitations is a proof that the conjecture cannot be solved.
They explain why the verified partial results do not yet fill either theorem.

## Direct-defect continuation: exact Lean reductions

Write `D_p = p-1-|A_p|`, and for `n >= 2` define

    U_n = product_{1 <= j < n} (n!/j! - 1).

`FactorialDefect.lean` proves that natural division is exact, that
`p | U_n` is equivalent to an earlier occurrence of `n! mod p` when `p` is
prime and `n < p`, and that

    |A_p| + #{2 <= n < p : p | U_n} = p-1.

It also proves that the exact target support limit is equivalent to
`D_p/p -> 1/exp(1)` on the same prime filter. Neither limit is asserted.

`DefectLimitCriterion.lean` defines

    V(N) = sum_{p prime, N < p <= 2N} (D_p - p/exp(1))^2

and proves `V(N)/N^2 -> 0` implies the exact target limit. This really is an
all-prime condition: any prime with a fixed proportional error contributes
at least a fixed positive multiple of `N^2` to a suitable window. The proof
uses the window scale `(p+1)/2`, covers `p=2`, and never averages `V` by the
number of primes. **The required estimate for `V` remains unproved.**

Both files were independently read, compiled with strict kernel/tactic
options, and axiom-audited. They do not import the specification. An expanded

## An unconditional prime-density advance (mathematical, not Lean-formalized)

The following deduction from published effective Chebotarev inputs was
independently checked twice. No bibliographic priority is claimed.

There are absolute positive constants `c,C` such that, for sufficiently
large `X`, the number of primes `X < p <= 2X` with

    D_p < c log(log X) / (log(log(log X)))^2

is at most

    C (log(log(log X)))^2 / log(log X) * (pi(2X)-pi(X)).

In particular, for every fixed `B`, the primes with `D_p <= B` have relative
prime density zero. This includes density zero of socialist primes. This is
not a linear defect bound, does not exclude every sufficiently large
exceptional prime, and does not imply `D_p/p -> 1/exp(1)`.

### Saturation and coprime-degree covariance

Let `F_h(T) = product_{i=1}^h (T+i) - 1` and let `rho_h(p)` count its roots.
Each `F_h` is irreducible over the rationals: if `F_h=AB` for nonconstant
monic integer polynomials, then `A+B` vanishes at `-1,...,-h`, which is
impossible by degree and monicity. Pair resultants are therefore nonzero.

For fixed `H`, primes not dividing those resultants give disjoint root sets
for `h <= H`. Each root is a valid starting index for `x!=(x+h)!`. Removing
the possible starting index zero, or excluding the factors `h!-1`, gives a
collision forest and hence

    D_p >= sum_{q in Q, q <= H} rho_q(p).

This forest/first-return argument is essential: arbitrary collision pairs
cannot simply be counted as independent support defects. The logarithm of
the product of the required resultants, polynomial discriminants, and
`q!-1` factors is `O(H^4 log H)`. At the cutoff below this product is less
than `X`, so no prime in `(X,2X]` is bad.

For distinct prime degrees `q,r`, the root fields `K_q,K_r` have compositum
degree exactly `qr`: it is divisible by both degrees and at most their
product. Thus their tensor product is a field, even though their Galois
closures need not be disjoint. Prime ideal theory, or Burnside and
Chebotarev in the common closure, gives mean `rho_q=1` and mixed mean
`rho_q rho_r=1`. For fixed degree cutoffs, the bound `rho_q^2 <= q rho_q`
and weights `1/q` already prove density zero of bounded defect.

### A bounded-variance family of degrees

Take prime degrees `q>3` with `q=3 or 11 mod 14`. Write `q=2m+1`. Modulo 2,

    F_q(T) = T^m (T+1)^(m+1) + 1.

It is squarefree and has no root in `F_2`. It has a cubic factor:
`T^3+T+1` in the first progression and `T^3+T^2+1` in the second. This follows
by evaluating at an element of order 7 in `F_8`, using respectively
`alpha+1=alpha^3` or `alpha+1=alpha^5`.

Frobenius at 2 is therefore fixed-point-free but not a `q`-cycle. Burnside's
prime-degree theorem implies the Galois group is doubly transitive: the
alternative is a subgroup of `AGL(1,q)`, whose fixed-point-free elements are
all `q`-cycles. Consequently the group mean of `rho_q^2` is 2. The cubic
factor need not give a permutation supported on just three letters; no
such stronger assertion is used.

### Effective cutoff and exceptional-zero signs

Use

    H = floor((1/4) log(log X) / log(log(log X))).

The splitting field of a pair has degree `n <= (H!)^2` and
`log Disc <= O(n H^3 log H)`, by adjoining the roots successively and using
the discriminant tower formula. Hence `n <= (log X)^(1/2+o(1))`.

Thorner--Zaman's signed effective Chebotarev formula has absolute constants
and applies when

    log X >> (log Disc)^2/n + n(log n)^2,

which holds here. Its error is exponentially small enough to sum over all
pairs. The possible exceptional term corresponds to a nontrivial quadratic
character of the full Galois group over the rationals. It has zero inner
product with the transitive permutation characters of odd degrees `q` and
`qr`. Its coefficient against `rho_q^2` is nonnegative, so its negative sign
can only improve the required upper second-moment bound. This argument
uses the signed formula at both dyadic endpoints, not an absolute estimate
for an uncontrolled exceptional zero.

For `M` selected prime degrees, `M ~ H/(3 log H)`, the prime average of
`(sum rho_q - M)^2` is at most `M+o(1)`. Chebyshev gives an exceptional
proportion at most `(4+o(1))/M` below defect `M/2`, proving the displayed
bound. For example any fixed `c<1/24` works after increasing the threshold.

## Twists and Pascal correlations: positive estimates and the remaining gap

For `f_c(n)=c^n n!`, let `S_2(c)` count unordered equal-value pairs. Uniform
short differencing gives, for every nonprincipal multiplicative character,

    |sum_{n=1}^{p-1-d} chi(P_d(n))| << p^(7/8) (log p)^(1/4),

independently of the macroscopic gap `d`. Averaging `c^d P_d(n)=1` over all
nonzero `c`, and using `sum_{d<p-1} gcd(d,p-1) <= (p-1) tau(p-1)`, proves

    average_c S_2(c) = (p-2)/2 + O(p^(7/8+o(1))).

This does not identify `c=1` as a typical twist. Support size is not invariant
under twisting. A centered fourth moment below `p^4` would yield uniform
second-moment control, but no such full estimate has been proved, and even
uniform `S_2(c) ~ p/2` would not determine the support constant.

Pascal's recurrence gives useful cancellation for locally balanced
character words. For `C_s(n)=binom(s,n)`, a product-one word with cumulative
characters nonprincipal at `D` positions has whole-triangle sum
`O(D^2 p log^2 p)`. Thus bounded-width neutral clusters can have arbitrary
macroscopic separations. In the width-one four-point case the level sets
reduce to the Mobius ratio `u(v+d)/(v(u+d))`; the exceptional level 1 is
exactly the Wilson row, and other levels have square-root-error counts.

However centering a twist moment removes frequencies `chi^d=1`; it does not
force bounded-width neutral clusters. Primitive frequency configurations
`(rho,rho,rho,rho^(-3))` survive fourth-cumulant subtraction. Four distinct
macroscopic unit gaps also leave a full three-character-parameter family
of equal-slope constraints. Those are not controlled by the local Pascal
bounds. The equal-row fourth-moment block can be bounded by
`O(p^(11/3+o(1)))`, but the mixed-row/mixed-character block is still unproved.

All twist/Pascal statements in this section are mathematical investigations,
not Lean proofs of the required limit.

## Global monodromy continuation: an explicit obstruction, not a settlement

`GlobalMonodromyObstruction.md` records a different global route that was
investigated and its verified limitation. The factorial interpolant `F_p`
has coefficient `[X^(p-2)] F_p = 1`, so its degree is unconditionally either
`p-1` or `p-2`. Fixed-degree polynomial value-set estimates do not apply with
a degree-independent error at this scale.

More strongly, choose `a != 0` with `-a` a quadratic nonresidue. The polynomial
`g(X)=X+a X^(p-2)` has full geometric and arithmetic monodromy `S_(p-2)` for
every prime `p>=5`, yet `|g(F_p^*)|/p -> 1/2`. Its fibers are exactly the
orbits of `x -> a/x`. It satisfies the Poisson second-factorial-moment main
term `p/2`, and every nonprincipal multiplicative-character sum is at most
`2 sqrt(p)` in absolute value, by a two-Jacobi-sum calculation.

This is not a factorial counterexample. It rigorously shows that even the
combination of full symmetric monodromy, square-root character cancellation,
and the expected second-moment main term cannot yield the conjectured support
constant without additional factorial-specific information. The detailed
proof includes the tame monodromy argument and exact character identities.
No new conditional criterion or Lean lemma was added; `Spec.lean` is unchanged.
Neither the all-prime limit nor its negation has been proved.


## Exact twist-reflection continuation

`FactorialTwistReflection.lean` is now an independently strict-compiled,
axiom-audited auxiliary module. Write `f_c(n)=c^n n!`, with `c != 0` and
`0 <= n < p`. Wilson and Fermat give

    f_{-c}(n) = -1/f_c(p-1-n).

Consequently the *entire* full-domain fiber histogram, including the empty
fibers, is invariant under `c -> -c`, after relabeling outputs by `a -> -1/a`.
If `N_c(a)` denotes the positive-index fiber and `M_r(c)` its binomial moment,
then the full moment is exactly

    fullM_{r+1}(c) = M_{r+1}(c) + choose(N_c(1), r).

In particular,

    S2(c) + N_c(1) = S2(-c) + N_{-c}(1),

and the two positive-support cardinalities differ by at most one. The module
proves these facts for every prime, including 2, and contains reusable finite-map
involution and insertion lemmas. The independently rerun strict build succeeded;
21 main/helper axiom audits are recorded in
`/tmp/FactorialTwistReflectionAudit.lean` and `.log`, all with only `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

The identities isolate the entire odd twist-frequency part as an endpoint
correction. They do not estimate the even-frequency part or prove the support
limit. `Spec.lean` remains unchanged with both original proof placeholders.

### Additional finite-field calculations, not Lean-formalized

Further research passes produced the following exact reductions. They remain
mathematical auxiliary calculations, not inputs asserted as axioms in Lean.
None provides a settlement.

* Complete gap sums satisfy
  `C_chi(d)=sum_{n in Fp} chi(P_d(n))=A_chi(d)+chi(d!)`.
  Reflection makes `C_chi(d)=0` when both the gap and character are odd.
  For an odd gap `d=2h+1`, squaring the centered variable gives the polynomial
  `Q_h(z)=z product_{j=1}^h(z-j^2)^2`. The collision equation becomes
  `x^d Q_h(z)=1`, with `x=c^2`; its degree is still `d`. Thus the sign quotient
  does not reduce macroscopic odd unit gaps to bounded-degree root problems.
* For collisions with `n+k=q+s`, `q=p-1`, the fixed-band equation reduces to
  `z^t t!(t+|s|)!=b`, with `z=-c^2` and an explicitly determined nonzero `b`.
  Consecutive-gap counting gives a uniform `O(p^(2/3))` bound per band.
  Summing over twists costs at most `q tau(q)` per band. Hence the union of
  `|s|<=B` bands has centered fourth moment
  `O((B+1)^4 p^3 tau(q))`. This removes a growing but still narrow even sector;
  it does not control macroscopic mixed bands.
* In `R=Fp[X]/(X^p-X)`, let `M` multiply by `X` and `Tg(x)=g(x+1)`.
  The exact recurrence operator `L=T-M-I` satisfies
  `det(ZI-L)=Z^p-Z-1` and `L^p-L=I`. Put `U=MT^-1`; then
  `[T,U]=I`, `U^p=0`, and the factorial vector is
  `(I-U)^-1 e_0=sum_{j=0}^{p-1} U^j e_0`, with `U^j e_0=j! e_j`.
  The spectral polynomial is insensitive to ordering the nonzero multipliers:
  ordering them in inverse pairs gives the same spectrum but half-size image.
  Thus this small Artin--Schreier spectrum does not determine coordinate fibers.
* The precise interpolating-polynomial recurrence includes the forcing term
  `F(X+1)-(X+1)F(X)=1-(X+1)^(p-1)+s(X^p-X)`, where `s=sum n!`.
  Ordinary differentiation does not descend as a derivation of `R`:
  on degree-<p representatives its commutator with `M` is `I-J`, where
  `J=sum_{r=0}^{p-1} T^r` is rank one, rather than simply `I`.
* For `U_n(z)=sum_{i=0}^n z^i/i!`, with `z != 0`, every fiber has size
  `O(p^(2/3))`, uniformly in `z` and the target. Indeed two equal values at
  distance `h` give a nonzero degree-`h-1` polynomial in the initial index,
  with leading coefficient `z`. In particular normalized derangements
  `D_n/n!=U_n(-1)` agree with any proposed modular constant on only
  `O(p^(2/3))` indices. A real approximation `D_n~n!/e` cannot be reduced to
  a nearly constant modular factor.
* The finite polynomial `Q_p(X)=sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(X)_j` obeys
  `Q_p(X+1)-(X+1)Q_p(X)=1-(X^p-X)`. For `alpha^p-alpha=1`, the explicit
  `beta=Q_p(alpha)` is nonzero and satisfies `beta^p=(alpha+1)beta`.
  It is a normal-basis generator, and
  `n!=Tr(-beta^(p^n)/(alpha beta))`. This trace functional is exactly
  constant-term evaluation on the rising-factorial basis, not an independent
  equidistribution input. The corresponding infinite falling-factorial series
  does not converge on this unramified Artin--Schreier residue class.

### Sharper smoothing calculation and an independent audit

A direct zero-extension argument (not yet Lean-formalized) improves the finite
constant. With H equally weighted consecutive translates of each fiber,

    3H E_c <= (p+H-1)(3p+H^2-1),  H>=1.

Here the diagonal shift contributes `p`; positive shift `d` contributes
`R_d(c)<=d`. The exact identity
`sum_{0<=j,k<H}|j-k|=H(H^2-1)/3` supplies the coefficient. This holds even for
H>p, with empty long-gap correlations. It is essential to zero-extend fiber
indicators, not to count equal padding values.

Using the nonnegative profile

### Joint shifts: a verified algebraic calculation, but no improved exponent

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, for distinct
`1<=h,u<p`, the two rising-factorial polynomials generate the full rational
function field:

    K(P_h(X),P_u(X)) = K(X).

Indeed put `g=gcd(h,u)`, `L=hu/g`. The difference
`P_h^(u/g)-P_u^(h/g)` has degree `L-1`, with nonzero next coefficient
`hu(h-u)/(2g)`. The extension degree divides h, u, and L-1, so is one.
This proof does not require L<p. For a common curve of
`P_h(X)=P_k(Y)` and `P_u(X)=P_v(Y)`, with distinct lengths on each side,
the embedded rational function fields coincide. Thus Y is a Mobius function
of X; absence of finite poles makes it affine. Leading and next coefficients
force h=k, u=v, and Y=X. Falling products and reciprocal sign cases can be
handled by the same coefficient/pole argument.

For the *off-diagonal* collision set C and nonwrapping H-by-H windows, let
`V_H(z)=#{t in [0,H)^2:z+t in C}`. Expanding threefold intersections and using
the preceding common-component check plus Bezout gives

    sum_z choose(V_H(z),3) << H^8 + H^5 p^(2/3).

Axis configurations use the ordinary uniform fiber bound O(p^(2/3)); all
other triples cost O(H^2) points. This calculation was independently audited,
including mixed-sign shifts and injectivity of actual integer bases into Fp.
If the diagonal is included, an additional `O(pH^4)` term is necessary;
without it the claimed bound fails already at H=3. Optimizing the correctly
stated off-diagonal bound at H~p^(1/4) gives only E_c<<p^(3/2). Thus it does
not improve the existing exponent: the O(H^2) budget per generic triple is
the bottleneck.

## Coefficient-averaging continuation: exact truncation, still no settlement

No Lean declaration was changed during this continuation. The following
mathematical audit concerns a differenced auxiliary sequence, not a proof of
the factorial-support limit.

For an odd prime p, put q=p-1, R(x)=x/(1-x), and
f_c(n)=c^n(n+1) for 0<=n<=p-2. A collision at gap d has the unique possible
starting value b=n+1=d R(c^d), provided c^d != 1. It is counted precisely when
1<=b<=p-1-d, using least nonnegative representatives. Write U_c for this
collision count.

### The exact wedge expansion

Let s(0)=0 and s(x)=x/p-1/2 for 1<=x<p, and let e_p(t)=exp(2*pi*i*t/p).
With normalized Fourier coefficients,

    v_B = p^(-1) sum_x s(x)e_p(-Bx)
        = i/(2p) cot(pi*B/p)        (B != 0),
    v_0 = 0.

On the admissible curve d,b,d+b != 0, the triangular indicator is exactly

    W(d,b) = 1-d/p + sum_{B!=0} kappa_d(B)e_p(Bb),
    kappa_d(B) = v_B (e_p(Bd)-1).

The underlying extension 1/2+s(d+b)-s(d)-s(b) has Fourier support on the
three lines A=0, B=0 and A=B. The literal triangle also has boundary
corrections; they vanish on the admissible curve. Keeping these boundary
terms straight avoids both a spurious independent frequency and an incorrect
constant term. In particular, the constant is 1-d/p, not (p-1-d)/p.

For balanced representatives of B,

    |kappa_d(B)| <= min(1/(pi*|B|), min(d,p-d)/p).

The weight at B=1 remains bounded away from zero when d/p is in [1/4,3/4].
Thus the coupled factor e_p(Bd)-1 does not eliminate low frequencies at
macroscopic gaps.

### What independent coefficient averaging actually gives

For r<=4, fixed gap tuple d=(d_1,...,d_r), and D the nonzero c avoiding all
poles, set

    S_d(a) = sum_{c in D} e_p(sum_j a_j R(c^(d_j))).

Orthogonality and injectivity of R give

    sum_{a_j in F_p} |S_d(a)|^2 <= p*gcd(d_j,q)*|D|,
    sum_{a in F_p^r} |S_d(a)|^2
        = p^r*gcd(d_1,...,d_r,q)*|D|.

The second equality is exact because D is invariant under the common power
kernel. For each fixed d_j, a_j=+/-B_j*d_j is a coefficient bijection.
On dyadic frequency blocks |B_j|~H_j, Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bounds the
absolute weighted sum by a constant (depending on r) times

    p * min(1,
            min_j sqrt(gcd(d_j,q)/H_j),
            sqrt(gcd(d_1,...,d_r,q)*p^(r-1)/product_j H_j)).

These are alternative bounds, not savings that can be multiplied.
Summing by the largest dyadic frequency yields the rigorous tail bound

    sum_{max |B_j|>K} product_j |kappa_(d_j)(B_j)|
                            * |S_d(+/-B_1*d_1,...,+/-B_r*d_r)|
      <<_r p * (1+log K)^(r-1)/sqrt K
               * sum_j sqrt(gcd(d_j,q)).

This removes the crude Fourier logarithmic loss and truncates high
frequencies. It does not yield o(p) for a fixed gap tuple with bounded gcds:
the retained low frequencies still carry nonvanishing weights. Summing over
gaps changes the phase functions and their pole sets, so it is NOT a new
independent coefficient average. For unrestricted r-gap summation, the tail
is O_r(p^(r+1)*tau(q)*(1+log K)^(r-1)/sqrt K). Any fourth-moment normalization
must still be applied explicitly; averaging over c divides by q, not p^2.

Independent parent derivations checked the carry identity, Fourier
normalization, coefficient fiber counts, and Cauchy--Schwarz/tail summation.

### The logarithmic-derivative coupling does not by itself distinguish the sums

For b_i=+/-B_i and theta=X*d/dX,

    F(X) = sum_i b_i*d_i*R(X^(d_i))
         = -theta(Q)/Q,
    Q(X) = product_i (1-X^(d_i))^(b_i).

However, arbitrary coefficients a_i in F_p have the same rational
logarithmic integrability: choose integers 0<=n_i<p with n_i=a_i/d_i in F_p
and use Q_a=product_i(1-X^(d_i))^(n_i). In particular, for every coefficient
vector (not just the small coupled ones),

    theta^(p-1)(F) = F-F^p,
    (theta+F)^p = theta+F.

These are differential identities, not additive-character cancellation.
The finite logarithm L_p(T)=sum_{k=1}^{p-1}T^k/k obeys

    theta_T L_p(T) = (T-T^p)/(1-T),
    (theta_T^2 L_p)(t) = R(t)     for t in F_p^* minus {1}.

Thus F(c) is the second Euler derivative of
sum_i (b_i/d_i)L_p(X^(d_i)) at c. The same representation holds for arbitrary
a_i with coefficients a_i/d_i^2. One cannot first reduce exponents modulo
p-1 and then differentiate: theta does not descend modulo X^(p-1)-1.

Small integer b_i do retain a bounded number of monomials in the numerator
and denominator of Q, and hence bounded-order Euler differential equations.
No additive-character bound exploiting that extra property was obtained.
A surviving simple pole also prevents direct removal by an Artin--Schreier
coboundary H^p-H. These observations and identities were independently
checked algebraically; they are not Lean-formalized and are not used as
assumptions in any Lean file.

The unresolved issue is still cancellation in the actual weighted
low-frequency, mixed-gap sums. Even resolving this auxiliary issue would
require further arguments to obtain factorial occupancy moments and the
support limit. Neither theorem in `Spec.lean` has a completed proof.

## Additive-twist continuation: origin-pencil transfer, not randomization

For odd p, use f(n)=n! at indices 0<=n<p, and set

    N_c(a) = #{n : f(n)+c*n=a},
    S_r(c) = sum_a choose(N_c(a),r),
    m(b) = N_0(b).

The support at c=0 is exactly the support in Spec, since 0!=1!.
For every function on F_p (not specifically factorials), each pair of distinct
indices determines exactly one c with equal twisted values. Consequently

    sum_c S_2(c) = choose(p,2).

This universal averaged second moment does not identify the c=0 moment.
The factorial recurrence gives a more specific, exact identity:

    N_c(0) = m(-c) - 1_{c=1}.

For n>=1, divide f(n)+c*n=0 by n to get f(n-1)=-c. The missing predecessor
at p-1 has factorial -1. In particular, writing D_0=p-|supp(f)|,

    #{c : N_c(0)=0} = D_0 + 1_{m(-1)=1},
    sum_c choose(N_c(0),r)
        = S_r(0) - choose(m(-1)-1,r-1).

Thus the zero-intercept pencil reproduces the original histogram, up to
one point; it is not an independent average. For the Redei polynomial
R(A,C)=product_n(A-f(n)-Cn), the same fact is

    (C-1) R(0,C) = -R(-C,0).

### What recurrence does to the other lines

On graph points away from the endpoint, T(x,y)=(x+1,(x+1)y). A line
y=a-cx becomes, in the new x-coordinate X,

    y=-cX^2+(a+c)X.

Horizontal lines therefore become the origin pencil, but nonhorizontal
lines become genuine parabolas. If indices i,j on the original line are
shifted together, their new line parameters are

    c'=c(i+j+1)-a,    a'=c(i+1)(j+1).

For a fixed original nonhorizontal line, distinct unordered pairs give
distinct new lines (the parameters recover i+j and ij). This injectivity
is only within that one line; different original lines can have overlapping
images and cannot be summed as disjoint new incidences.

Given two lines L_{c,a} and L_{d,b}, a graph point at n on the first whose
successor is on the second satisfies

    -cX^2+(a+c+d)X-b=0,    X=n+1.

There are at most two such points for c!=0. For c=0 there is at most one,
except for the exact transfer (d,b)=(-a,0). Consequently any subset of at
least three distinct nonendpoint graph points is collinear before and
after a simultaneous one-step shift if and only if its factorial values
were equal. This apparent two-time incidence count is another encoding of
the unknown original occupancy moment, not a product of independent counts.

Wilson reflection of a line yields, separately on the two index parities,

    (a+c+cX)y = +/-1.

For c!=0, these are reciprocal curves, each meeting a line in at most two
points. For c=0 they are horizontal lines. Reflection therefore does not
identify the full additive-twist histograms at distinct slopes either.
After h nonwrapping shifts a line becomes

    y=(X)_h (a-c(X-h)),

which has degree h+1 (or degree h for nonzero horizontal lines). Repeated
transport does not stay inside line pencils.

### Verified mathematical incidence estimates, not target estimates

A line containing factorial graph points at n and n+h gives a root of

    (a-cX) product_{j=1}^h(X+j) - a+c(X+h).

This polynomial is nonzero of degree h+1 when c!=0, and degree h when
c=0,a!=0. Consecutive-gap counting gives, for every line,

    N_c(a) <= 1 + floor((p-1)/(K+1)) + K(K+3)/2,

hence maximum occupancy M=O(p^(2/3)). The line a=c=0 has no points.

The published general Stevens--de Zeeuw theorem also implies, for any p
points in F_p^2 of maximum line occupancy M,

    sum_lines choose(|P intersect line|,3) << p^(17/7)+p*M^2.

Here is the checked exponent calculation. The number t_k of k-rich lines
is O(p^2/k^2) by pair counting. For k>=2*sqrt(p), pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz
on line incidences gives t_k<=2p/k. Between K=C*p^(3/7) and 2*sqrt(p), the
pair bound puts t_k<p^(8/7). If t_k>p^(7/8), the general incidence theorem
gives t_k<<p^(11/4)k^(-15/4); otherwise t_k<=p^(7/8). The theorem's extra
characteristic hypothesis p^(-2)t_k^13<<p^15 holds in this range. Dyadic
summation of k^3*t_k gives

    O(p^2*K + p^(11/4)*K^(-3/4) + p^(19/8) + p*M^2),

which is the displayed bound. Source: arXiv:1609.06284,
`/corpus/src/1609.06284/1609.06284.tex`, general theorem, lines 100--107.
For the factorial graph, vertical lines contribute no triples and therefore

    sum_c S_3(c) << p^(17/7).

This is still above the conjectural averaged scale p^2/6, and it gives no
extraction of the slope c=0. It is not Lean-formalized, and no bibliographic
priority claim is made for this deduction.

One further exact identity is sum_n n*f(n)=-1. For p>=5 this yields

    sum_n (f(n)+c*n)^2 = sum_n f(n)^2 - 2*c  in F_p.

A permutation twist must have c=(sum_n f(n)^2)/2, so at most one additive
twist can be a permutation. This counts nonpermutation directions, not the
size of their supports. No conjecture about left factorials is assumed.

## Smith/p-ordering continuation: exact rank encoding, not a rank estimate

No Lean file was changed. This pass investigated direct collision-class
counting rather than another twist average.

Use the distinct integer nodes S_N={1!,...,N!} and their square Vandermonde
matrix W_N=((i!)^j), 1<=i<=N, 0<=j<N. At N=p its rank over F_p is |A_p|+1,
with p! supplying the additional value zero; its nullity is exactly D_p.
The matrix on 0!,..., (p-1)! instead has an identically zero integer
determinant because 0!=1!, so that determinant cannot encode the defect.

For any finite set S of N distinct integer nodes, fix p and a p-ordering
(a_0,...,a_(N-1)), and write

    Q_k(X)=product_{h<k}(X-a_h),
    q_k=Q_k(a_k),    e_k=v_p(q_k).

Changing the monomial basis to the monic Newton basis gives an evaluation
matrix L*diag(q_k), where L_(i,k)=Q_k(a_i)/q_k. The minimum defining a
p-ordering makes every entry of L integral in Z_(p); L is lower unit
triangular. The nondecreasing e_k are therefore the local Smith exponents.
Globally the Smith invariants are Bhargava's set-dependent generalized
factorials, not the ordinary factorials. Exactly |S mod p| of these exponents
are zero: the greedy algorithm must visit every occupied residue class
before making a second visit to any class.

There is an exact residue-tree recursion. For S_a={x in S:x=a mod p} and
T_a={(x-a)/p:x in S_a},

    (e_k(S)) = sort(multiset union over nonempty S_a of
                        {j+e_j(T_a):0<=j<|S_a|}).

Different branches have unit differences; within one branch each earlier
choice contributes one additional factor p. Each nonempty branch contributes
exactly one zero. Thus the recursion requires the unknown residue partition
at its first step; it does not compute that partition from simpler data.
Arbitrary finite occupancy partitions can satisfy even the simpler condition
that all same-residue differences have valuation exactly one, so the generic
Smith/ultrametric restrictions cannot by themselves force the proposed
occupancy constant. This observation is not a factorial counterexample.

The global determinantal divisor has the exact formula

    Delta_r(S)=gcd_{T subset S, |T|=r} product_{x<y in T}(y-x).

Every minor is divisible by the Vandermonde product of its selected rows,
and the first r columns realize that product. Hence p does not divide
Delta_r(S) exactly when S contains r pairwise distinct residues. This again
encodes, rather than estimates, the desired rank.

The factorial determinant factors as

    det W_N = product_{i=1}^{N-1}(i!)^(N-i) * product_{n=2}^N U_n.

At N=p the first product and U_p are p-units. Its p-valuation is consequently
sum_{2<=n<p} v_p(U_n), whereas D_p=sum_{2<=n<p}1_{p|U_n}. Pair multiplicities
and higher p-adic coincidences both create overcounting. At p=11, the local
Smith exponents are

    (0,0,0,0,0,0,1,1,1,2,2),

so nullity, collision-pair count, and determinant valuation are respectively
5, 6, and 7. The difference 9!-1!=11^2*2999 exhibits the higher valuation.

### No prime-independent Newton ordering

A stronger obstruction to a fixed Newton basis is elementary. For N>=4,
S_N admits no simultaneous ordering for all primes even through its third
chosen node. The gcd of all pair differences is 1, so the first two nodes
of any simultaneous ordering must differ by 1; the only choice is {1,2}.
The next candidate products are (n!-1)(n!-2), n>=3. They are all even, and
the first two are 20 and 506, of gcd 2. Simultaneous prime-wise minimality
would require a product of absolute value 2, but all candidates are at
least 20. This rules out that basis construction, not all possible global
Smith methods.

For example, W_4 has integer Smith invariants (1,1,2,91080), whereas ordinary
Newton pivots are (1,1,20,9108). Dividing Newton columns by p-divisible pivots
is not an invertible operation over F_p; the resulting integer-valued
polynomials can distinguish nodes having the same residue and thus change
the evaluation lattice. Such normalization cannot be used to infer full
rank of the original matrix.

## Independent mixed-sum attempt: a sparse fourth-order resonance

A further independent attempt did not settle the target. It found a genuine
counterexample to an overbroad *auxiliary* pointwise estimate. The exact

For primes p>19 with p=17 mod24, set

    h=(p-1)/4, d=(p-5)/12, d_j=d+j*h (0<=j<4),
    b=(1,-1,-1,1), R(x)=x/(1-x),

    S_p = (p-1)/4 - 1 + O(sqrt(p)).

simple poles. Each coset can be parameterized four-to-one by z=alpha*t^4.

error. For a proper subset, none of the merged pole coefficients vanishes
on every pole, on any of the four cosets, so the same argument applies.

    w_p=(cot(pi/p)/p)^4 product_j sin(pi*d_j/p)

primes in the progression. These are asymptotic statements about the

## Higher even twist moments: the specified positive closure still loses

This continuation tested whether larger even moment order could overcome
the fourth-moment balanced-lattice obstruction. It did not give a target
proof. The following is a proved limitation of one explicitly specified
upper-bound procedure, NOT a lower bound on actual factorial fluctuations.

Put q=p-1 and X(c)=S_2(c)-average_c S_2(c), for multiplicative twists c^n*n!
on 1<=n<=q. For even m>=2 let M_m=sum_c X(c)^m. Ordered endpoint pairs give

    M_m=alpha_m sum_k sum_{x in Lambda_k} F_k(x),
    alpha_m=2^(-m)*q^(1-m),
    Lambda_k={(n,t) in Z^(2m):sum_i k_i(t_i-n_i)=0 mod q},
    F_k(n,t)=product_i 1_{k_i(t_i-n_i)!=0 mod q}
                            theta^(k_i)(t_i!/n_i!).

Here theta generates the multiplicative characters, and F_k is zero outside
[1,q]^(2m). All centered-frequency exclusions remain in F_k. Its support
size is D_k, and |F_k|=1 on that support.

Choose any finite nonempty shift set W_k subset Lambda_k, with N_k=|W_k|,
and average F_k(x+h) using its actual k-dependent normalization 1/N_k.
There is no boundary error with the zero extension. Let

    B_k=union_{h in W_k}(supp(F_k)-h), R_k=|B_k|,
    C_k(h,h')=sum_{x in Lambda_k} F_k(x+h)*conjugate(F_k(x+h')).

Omit D_k=0. Weighted Cauchy followed by taking absolute values of all
unequal-shift correlations gives the valid bound M_m<=P_m, where

    P_m^2=alpha_m^2 * (sum_k lambda_k*R_k)
           * sum_k lambda_k^(-1)
               [D_k/N_k + N_k^(-2) sum_{h!=h'} |C_k(h,h')|]

for any positive weights lambda_k. Short factorial ratios occur in C_k,
but both shifted centering masks and the lattice restriction must remain.

### Fixed even orders do not help this bound

Exactly C_k(h,h)=D_k. Also R_k>=N_k: fix one support point y and use the
distinct points y-h. Cauchy therefore shows, for every choice of shifts
and positive weights,

    P_m >= alpha_m sum_k sqrt(D_k).

This remains true for nonnegative probability weights on the shifts, since
R_k*sum_h w_k(h)^2>=1. It is the self-pair diagonal of the Cauchy square,
not the presence of non-gap-changing shifts, that causes this loss.

If every k_i is a unit modulo q, all exclusions become t_i-n_i!=0. Each
residue gap has exactly q ordered endpoint representatives. Consequently

    D_k=q^(m-1)*[(q-1)^m+q-1]          (m even).

Thus P_m >= (2^(-m)+o(1))*phi(q)^m*sqrt(q), already a scale
p^(m+1/2-o(1)) at any fixed m. It cannot prove M_m=o(p^m), even if all
unequal-shift correlation costs could optimistically be set to zero.

An independent small-gcd count sharpens the fixed-order statement. For
G>=1 put K_G={k:gcd(k,q)<=G}. Then |K_G|>=q-q*tau(q)/G. For tuples in
K_G^m the unrestricted gap equation has at least q^(m-1) solutions, and
each excluded coordinate contributes at most G^2*q^(m-2) solutions. Hence

    D_k >= q^(2m-1)*(1-m*G^2/q).

Choosing G of order q^(1/4), for fixed m, gives the sharper lower bound
P_m >= (2^(-m)+o(1))*q^(m+1/2) for this upper-bound expression.

### Growing the moment order does not avoid the same closure loss

There is a further exact bound, uniform in every even m. Define B(0)=0 and

    B(e)=#{(k,d) in (Z/q)^2:k*d=e}    (e!=0),
    T=sum_e B(e)=q^2-sum_{k=0}^{q-1}gcd(k,q).

This B is symmetric: B(e)=B(-e). Counting all character tuples and endpoint
gaps gives

    sum_k D_k=q^m * B^{*m}(0),

where convolution is on the cyclic group Z/q. For m=2r, symmetry and
Cauchy give

    B^{*m}(0)=sum_e (B^{*r}(e))^2 >= T^m/q.

Since D_k<=q^(2m), sqrt(D_k)>=D_k/q^m. Substitution into the diagonal
bound proves

    P_m >= (T/(2q))^m,
    P_m^(1/m)/p >= [q^2-sum_k gcd(k,q)]/(2q*p).

The elementary inequality sum_k gcd(k,q)<=q*tau(q), with tau(q)=o(q),
makes the right side tend to 1/2. Therefore even choosing m to grow with p
cannot turn THIS positive closure into an o(p) bound for max_c |X(c)|.
The desired actual moment might be much smaller: signed cancellations
were discarded before this bound, and no conclusion about M_m being large
has been drawn. A different procedure preserving such cancellations is
not ruled out.
