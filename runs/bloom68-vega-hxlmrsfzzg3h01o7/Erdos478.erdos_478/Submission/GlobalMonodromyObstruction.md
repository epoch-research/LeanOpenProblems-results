# Global monodromy route: a verified obstruction, not a settlement

Neither Erdős 478 nor its negation is proved here. `Spec.lean` is unchanged.
No Lean declarations or axioms were added. The statements below are mathematical
arguments about a possible global approach, not new hypotheses asserted for
factorial residues.

## 1. Why this global route was considered

For a degree-d Morse polynomial over a finite field of characteristic greater
than d, the geometric monodromy is the symmetric group S_d. With d fixed,
function-field Chebotarev gives
value-set density

    c_d = sum_{j=1}^d (-1)^(j-1)/j!

up to an error tending to zero as the field size grows. The constants in the
error depend on d. Since c_d tends to 1-1/e, applying such a result to the
factorial interpolating polynomial looks potentially relevant to the exact
constant in Erdős 478.

The issue is not just an omitted technical uniformity argument. At the degree
scale forced by factorial interpolation, the analogous uniform conclusion is
false, even with full symmetric monodromy and substantially stronger character
bounds than are known for factorials.

## 2. The factorial interpolant necessarily has degree comparable to p

Let p be an odd prime. Let F_p in F_p[X] be the unique polynomial of degree
at most p-1 with F_p(n)=n! for 0 <= n < p. Adding 0! does not change the
factorial value set, since 0!=1!.

Lagrange interpolation gives the exact formula

    F_p(X) = sum_{n=0}^{p-1} n! (1 - (X-n)^(p-1)).

All coefficients and factorials on the right are interpreted in F_p. It follows
that

    [X^(p-1)] F_p = -sum_{n=0}^{p-1} n!,
    [X^(p-2)] F_p = -sum_{n=0}^{p-1} n n! = 1.

For the last equality use the integer telescoping identity

    sum_{n=0}^{p-1} n n! = sum_{n=0}^{p-1} ((n+1)! - n!) = p! - 1.

Consequently deg F_p is either p-1 or p-2, unconditionally. In particular,
this conclusion does not assume Kurepa's left-factorial conjecture. A
fixed-degree value-set theorem cannot be applied with a degree-independent
error without additional justification.

## 3. An explicit countermodel to that uniformity

For every prime p >= 5, choose a in F_p^* with -a a quadratic nonresidue, and
put

    g_{p,a}(X) = X + a X^(p-2).

This is not a factorial sequence. It is used only to test the proposed
monodromy/character-sum method. On F_p^* it equals x + a/x, and its values
there are nonzero by the choice of a.

### Exact fibers and support

For nonzero x,y,

    g(x)-g(y) = (x-y)(1-a/(xy)).

Thus a fiber is exactly an orbit of the involution x -> a/x. Write

    b = #{x in F_p^* : x^2=a} in {0,2}.

There are b singleton fibers and (p-1-b)/2 doubleton fibers. Therefore

    |g(F_p^*)| = (p-1+b)/2,
    |g(F_p^*)|/p -> 1/2.

If M_v counts nonzero-domain preimages, then also

    sum_v choose(M_v,2) = (p-1-b)/2,
    sum_v choose(M_v,r) = 0  for every r >= 3.

So the conjectured Poisson second-factorial-moment main term p/2 can hold,
while the support constant differs from 1-1/e. Including the domain point
zero adds just one new value (zero), and does not change any limiting density.

### Full symmetric geometric and arithmetic monodromy

The degree is d=p-2 < p. We have

    g'(X)  = 1 - 2a X^(p-3),
    g''(X) = 6a X^(p-4).

There are precisely p-3 distinct critical points c in the algebraic closure;
they are nonzero and satisfy c^(p-3)=1/(2a). At each such point,

    g(c) = 3c/2.

Since p >= 5, the critical values are all distinct. Thus g is a Morse
polynomial and its geometric monodromy is S_d.

For completeness, the standard last implication can be justified as follows.
The cover x -> g(x) is connected of degree d, so its geometric monodromy G
acts transitively on d roots. Each finite branch point has exactly one simple
critical point above it, giving inertia generated by a transposition.
Infinity is totally ramified of index d. All ramification is tame, since
p>d and G is a subgroup of S_d.

Let H be the normal subgroup of G generated by the finite inertia groups.
The G/H-cover of the projective line can be ramified only at infinity and
is tame. If its degree is m and it has r points above infinity, the
Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives

    2g_Y-2 = -2m + (m-r) = -m-r.

Here r >= 1, so m > 1 would contradict g_Y >= 0. Hence H=G. A transitive
permutation group generated by transpositions is S_d: the graph whose edges
are those transpositions must be connected, and edge transpositions of a
connected graph generate the full symmetric group. This proves the claim.
The arithmetic group contains the geometric S_d and embeds in S_d, so it too
is S_d; the splitting field has constant field F_p.

This gives a family of regular, full-symmetric-monodromy polynomials of degree
less than p whose value-set density tends to 1/2, not c_d -> 1-1/e.

### Uniform square-root multiplicative-character bounds

Let chi be any nonprincipal multiplicative character of F_p^*, extended by
zero at zero. Then

    |sum_{x in F_p^*} chi(g(x))| <= 2 sqrt(p).

Here is a direct Jacobi-sum proof, so this assertion does not rely on assuming
any unproved uniform trace-function estimate.

If chi(-1)=-1, pairing x with -x makes the sum zero. Otherwise choose a
character eta with eta^2=chi^(-1), which is possible because chi(-1)=1.
Let lambda be the quadratic character. Substituting u=x^2 yields

    S = sum_{u in F_p} (eta(u)+(lambda eta)(u)) chi(u+a)
      = chi(a) [eta(-a) J(eta,chi)
                 + (lambda eta)(-a) J(lambda eta,chi)].

Both first arguments, the second argument chi, and the two products of the
arguments are nonprincipal. Indeed eta^2=chi^(-1) is nonprincipal, so
eta is neither 1 nor lambda; and eta chi=eta^(-1), while
lambda eta chi=lambda eta^(-1). Each displayed Jacobi sum consequently has
absolute value sqrt(p), proving the bound.

## 4. Exact scope and remaining gap

The countermodel is not a counterexample to Erdős 478: g does not satisfy
the factorial recurrence. It proves that even the following combination
of properties does not determine the required constant at growing degree:

* full symmetric geometric and arithmetic monodromy;
* degree below p but comparable to p;
* O(sqrt(p)) cancellation for every nonprincipal multiplicative character;
* the second-factorial-moment main term p/2.

Thus even a proof of full symmetric monodromy for F_p would not finish the
factorial problem. One still needs a factorial-specific estimate controlling
the base-field fibers on a linear scale, beyond generic monodromy and character
cancellation. No such estimate is proved here. Nor is an unbounded prime
family with a fixed positive-proportion deviation for factorials constructed.

The original all-prime limit and its negation remain unresolved by this work.
No conditional limit criterion was added or formalized.

The fixed-degree value-set formula and its degree dependence are recorded in
`/corpus/src/math_0412135/math0412135.tex`, lines 206-225. That source also
states the degree n<p, distinct-critical-values hypothesis and the symmetric

the monodromy proof for the particular countermodel directly.

The random-polynomial result in
`/corpus/src/2203.06729/` does not bypass the gap: its Poisson limit requires
the number of free coefficients to tend to infinity. The family F_p(X)-t
has only one free coefficient. Its more quantitative theorems also do not
cover prescribing all but that coefficient at degree comparable to p.

Supplemental computation, not used in place of the proofs:

  checked for all 238 odd primes below 1500. Full interpolation was additionally
  checked for every odd prime below 100.

  every prime from 5 through 47. For the factorial interpolant the same finite
  test found a non-Morse case at p=7; no all-prime Morse assertion is made.
* Numerical checks of 13,730 nonprincipal character sums, for primes from 5
  through 397, agreed with the proved 2 sqrt(p) bound and exact odd-character

* An independent Sage calculation in exact cyclotomic fields checked odd-
