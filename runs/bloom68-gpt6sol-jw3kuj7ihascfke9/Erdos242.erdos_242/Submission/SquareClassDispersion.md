# A proposed *global* square-class dispersion lemma for full Type I

**Status:** The dispersion lemma in the box below is **unproved**. It is a
precise, quantitative intermediate problem, not an assertion of the
Erdős–Straus conjecture or a new Lean axiom. The identities and implications
surrounding it are elementary, apart from the explicitly identified standard
character-sum estimate used for the quantitative comparison. Nothing in this
note modifies or imports `Submission/Spec.lean`.

## Exact residue boxes, including composite numerators

Let `n=24k+1`, `k≥1`. For `0≤j<6k` put

    x_j=6k+1+j,       a_j=4j+3=4x_j−n,
    t_j=3j+2 (mod a_j),     4t_j+1=3a_j.

Then `n/4<x_j<n/2`, `a_j` is odd, `a_j≡3 (mod 4)`, and a **full** Type-I
certificate is a positive `c|x_j²` with `c≡t_j (mod a_j)`. The allowed
exponents of every prime `r^e||x_j` are `0,…,2e`: replacing `c|x_j²` by
`c|x_j`, or by an arbitrary residue in the subgroup generated by the
prime factors, changes the question.

Write `G_j=(ℤ/a_jℤ)ˣ`. Dirichlet characters `ψ∈Ĝ_j` are extended by zero on
nonunits; denote their principal character by `1_j` and their nonprincipal
Jacobi character by `χ_j(v)=(v/a_j)`. The latter is nonprincipal **even if
`a_j` is composite**, because `χ_j(−1)=−1`. Define

    F_j(ψ) = Σ_{c|x_j²} ψ(c)
           = ∏_{r^e||x_j} (1+ψ(r)+⋯+ψ(r)^(2e)),
    D_j = F_j(1_j)
        = ∏_{r^e||x_j, r∤a_j} (2e+1),
    S_j = F_j(χ_j)
        = ∏_{r^e||x_j, r∤a_j, χ_j(r)=1} (2e+1).

The omitted factors of `S_j` are **one**, including when `χ_j(r)=−1`
(the sum of `2e+1` alternating signs is one). For `r|a_j`, both character
sums have factor one; a successful `c` cannot contain such a prime. Hence
`0<S_j≤D_j` for every `j` without any coprimality assumption on `n`.
The target is a unit with `χ_j(t_j)=−1`, because `4t_j≡−1 (mod a_j)`.
Orthogonality of **all** characters of the *unit group*, not just Jacobi,
gives the exact identity

    H(n) := Σ_{j<6k} #{c>0 : c|x_j², a_j|4c+1} = M(n)+T(n),
    M(n) := Σ_{j<6k} (D_j−S_j)/φ(a_j),
    T(n) := Σ_{j<6k} [1/φ(a_j)]
              Σ_{ψ∈Ĝ_j, ψ≠1_j,χ_j} conjugate(ψ(t_j)) F_j(ψ).       (★)

`T(n)` is real (indeed rational) because `H(n)` and `M(n)` are. The formula
is exact for *composite as well as prime* `n`; no independent distribution
of divisors or averaging over numerators is assumed. For a hard prime
`p=n`, `gcd(a_j,x_j)=1`, so `D_j=τ(x_j²)` and
`S_j=∏_{r^e||x_j,(r/p)=1}(2e+1)` by the Lean-verified Jacobi transfer
`PointwiseJacobi.jacobi_divisor_transfer`.

**Proposed intermediate lemma (uniform signed-box dispersion).** For every
**nonsquare** `n=24k+1`, `k≥1`, the *full, cross-denominator* character sum
in (★) satisfies

                          |T(n)| ≤ (99/100) M(n).              (D)

The constant is fixed, and (D) must hold **pointwise** for each `n`, not
only for almost all `n`. The sum includes *every* `0≤j<6k`, *every* prime
factor and its full `0,…,2e` exponent range, and *every* nonprincipal,
non-Jacobi character of `(ℤ/a_jℤ)ˣ`. This is a proposed theorem to prove,
**not a proved estimate** and not a permissible axiom for a Lean solution.

## Why its main term is *unconditionally positive* for every nonsquare

The Jacobi character `χ_n=(·/n)` is nonprincipal when odd `n` is not a
square: select a prime `q` appearing to an odd exponent in `n`, choose a
quadratic nonresidue modulo `q`, and use CRT to choose a unit `r (mod n)`
which is that nonresidue modulo `q` and `1` modulo every other prime-power
factor of `n`. Thus `χ_n(r)=−1`. For `n≡1 (mod 8)`, the supplementary laws
give `χ_n(−1)=χ_n(2)=1`. Reflect `r` to `m=min(r,n−r)<n/2`, retaining its
negative symbol. Double `m` until `n/4<2^h m<n/2`; these inequalities
hold because `n` is odd and the previous double was at most `n/4`.
Multiplicativity gives `χ_n(x)=−1` for the resulting eligible `x`.

For the corresponding `j`, `gcd(x,n)=1` (otherwise the symbol is zero),
so `gcd(x,a_j)=1` since `a_j+n=4x`. Now `x|x²`, and the established
`jacobi_divisor_transfer` proves `χ_j(x)=χ_n(x)=−1`. Some prime `r|x`
therefore has `χ_j(r)=−1`; its factor contributes `2e+1≥3` to `D_j`
but only one to `S_j`. Consequently `M(n)>0`. Conversely, for an odd
square `n=s²`, every divisor coprime to `a_j` has character `+1` at `n`
and hence at `a_j` by the same transfer. Therefore `D_j=S_j`, `M(n)=0`
and `H(n)=0` for all `j`, consistent with
`PointwiseJacobi.square_no_typeI_anywhere`. A square may still have a
Type-II solution, e.g. `25`.

If (D) were proved, (★) would immediately yield

             H(n) ≥ M(n)/100 > 0     for each nonsquare `n≡1 (mod 24)`.

Because `H(n)` is a **nonnegative integer counting the exact congruence**,
a particular index and positive divisor `c|x_j²` would then exist. In
particular every hard prime `p≡1 (mod 24)` is nonsquare; the existing
`Classification.good_of_typeI`, followed by `Reduction.good_of_hard_primes`,
would prove the strictly ordered `Spec.lean` theorem for *all* `n>2`.
This is a conditional mathematical deduction, **not** a formalization of an
unproved hypothesis.

## This is stronger than merely finding one solution

The bound (D) controls both signs of the nonquadratic error and gives a
*relative*, quantitative lower bound on the **number of full Type-I
pairs**, rather than saying that it is positive. This difference can be made
rigorous on hard primes: for `p=24k+1` and `L=log p≥64`, the standard
Pólya–Vinogradov interval bound
`|Σ_{v∈I}(v/p)|≤√p(1+L)` and a coprime-divisor double count imply

                          M(p) ≥ L³/(3·2¹⁹ log 2).           (†)

For reference, this is the **Type-I-only** adaptation of the explicit
joint-Type-I/II main-term estimate proved in
`Submission/JacobiCorrectedJointCount.md`: take dyadic `A` with
`p^(3/4)≤A≤p/16`, and the `H=A/4` first denominators with
`A≤4x−p<2A`. For each coprime `d,e≤⌊A^(1/16)⌋`, the interval bound
shows at least `H/(4de)` of those `x` have `de|x` and `(x/p)=−1`.
The coprime harmonic double sum is at least `L²/2¹²`, so their weighted
sum of `τ(x²)` is at least `HL²/2¹⁴`. If `(x/p)=−1`, then
`S_j≤D_j/3`; since `φ(a_j)≤a_j<2A`, the block contributes at least
`L²/(3·2¹⁶)` to **Type-I** `M(p)`. There are at least
`L/(8 log 2)` disjoint such blocks, proving (†). This uses only an
unconditional character-sum theorem for the *principal/Jacobi main
term*, not a claimed bound on the remaining characters.

Thus (D) would force `H(p)≥L³/(300·2¹⁹ log 2)`, an unbounded number of
**distinct cofactor/index pairs** as `p→∞`, and it would force Type I even
where an Erdős–Straus proof could instead use Type II. A bare existence
proof, or an identity involving only a negative Jacobi symbol, provides
neither this quantitative multiplicity nor any control on `T(n)`. This is
evidence of the proposed lemma's genuinely additional arithmetic content;
it is **not** a claim to have disproved the possibility of a different
all-prime proof.

## Why reciprocity or integral approximation does not prove (D)

At a fixed first denominator, set `a=4x−p`, `N=px`. Clearing denominators
on the two remaining unit fractions gives `ayz=N(y+z)`, or
`(ay−N)(az−N)=N²`. Positive integral points require a **positive divisor**
`u|N²` with `u≡−N (mod a)` (and its complementary divisor satisfying the
same condition); a Type-I certificate requires the still more specific
`c|x²` and `c≡t_j (mod a)`. For a hard prime, `gcd(a,N)=1`, and the
complementary residue is then automatic, but *existence* of the first
residue among the bounded prime-exponent boxes is not a Hasse-norm
statement. Quadratic Hilbert reciprocity recognizes the necessary sign
`χ_j(t_j)=−1`; it does not choose exponents in `0,…,2v_r(x)` so as to
hit `t_j`. If one adds **all** ray-class/Artin characters and the correct
integral restrictions on a fixed fiber, their orthogonality simply
recovers the exact finite residue condition; it does not show it is met
on at least one changing fiber. Rational or signed integral points and
local congruence solutions do not imply strictly positive ordered integral
points. A usable strong-approximation argument would still have to produce
an actual *integral, positive* point on some eligible fiber, hence an exact
bounded-exponent residue witness; it need not prove the stronger quantitative
estimate (D). No such result is invoked here.

**Remaining task:** Prove (D), replace it with another independently
provable theorem controlling the exact cross-`j` residue boxes, or find a
genuine counterexample to the stronger nonsquare Type-I assertion. Neither
the already proved square obstruction nor finite audits establish (D).
