## Formal kernel and highest-column condition

Use x for the original row index and t for its reciprocal factorial. Put

    L_j(H)(x) = x^j H(x-1)-H(x),
    K(A,H)(x,t) = A-(1-t) sum_(j>=1) L_j(H_j)(x) t^(j-1),

with finitely many rational polynomial H_j. This is the existing telescoping
class, whose weighted sum at t=1/x! is A*alpha-sum_j H_j(1).

If a formal kernel has t-degree m>=1, its t^m coefficient must belong to
the image of L_m. Higher nominal columns cannot evade this condition.
This is highest_coefficient_in_image.

The elementary kernel

    P_A(x,t) = A-(1-t)(x-1)

is valid, using H_1=1. For every m>=2, P_A^m is NOT a formal kernel of the
above class, for ANY retained rational coefficient, any finite number of
columns, and any rational polynomial auxiliary family.

Its highest coefficient is (x-1)^m. If L_m(H)=(x-1)^m, the degree formula
forces H to be constant. But a constant H gives c*(x^m-1), whose coefficient
of x^(m-1) is zero, whereas that coefficient of (x-1)^m is -m.

Main declarations: power_not_column, highest_coefficient_in_image,
basic_is_kernel, basic_power_not_kernel.

This elementary P_A is not asserted to be a nonnegative kernel. Its role
is to disprove closure of the class under powers, not to supply a new
positive example.

## Equality only at factorial nodes does not repair the obstruction

For every nonzero P in Q[x,t], FactorialNodePolynomials proves

    P(n,1/n!) != 0 for every sufficiently large natural n.

The formal indexing is n+2. Remove the lowest power of t. The remaining
constant-t coefficient is a nonzero polynomial in n, bounded away from
zero eventually. All higher-t terms tend to zero by factorial domination.
Induction on the t-degree handles the removed powers.

Consequently eventual equality at factorial nodes implies formal polynomial
equality. In particular, for every fixed candidate telescoping kernel,
P_A^m differs from it at every sufficiently late node when m>=2.

Main declarations: eventually_node_ne_zero, eq_of_eventually_node_eq,
eventually_power_ne_kernel.

These assertions concern polynomials on the factorial graph. They are NOT
an irrationality or transcendence theorem for alpha, and they provide no
uniform height/degree bound for a growing family.

## Direct application to the known positive kernel

Let P be the exact PhysicalPositiveKernelExample kernel with A=4, B=5,
and J=2. Its top t-coefficient is S=L_2(upper), a degree-11 polynomial.
The square P^2 has t-degree four and top coefficient S^2.

PhysicalKernelSquare verifies an explicit rational polynomial identity

    S^2 = L_4(V)+R,

where V has degree 18 and

    R(x) = 74679600728482924703/15312500000000000 * x^3
         - 22585727540270736179/45937500000000000 * x^2
         - 1054451535662902537/1837500000000000 * x
         - 60373964036693728681/22968750000000000.

Thus R is nonzero of degree less than four. If S^2 were L_4(H), then
L_4(H-V)=R would contradict the degree increase of L_4. It follows that
P^2 is not any formal polynomial telescoping kernel, and differs from each
fixed candidate at all sufficiently late factorial nodes.

Main declarations: square_column_decomposition, square_column_not_image,
square_not_kernel, eventually_square_ne_kernel.

The certificate was generated by exact SymPy polynomial elimination in
/tmp/physical_square_column.py; its artifacts are
/tmp/physical_square_coeffs.json and /tmp/physical_square_defs.lean.
Lean verifies the polynomial identity independently by evaluation and ring
normalization, then checks the degree argument. No finite approximation to
alpha is used.

## Scope and remaining gap

These results block a naive pointwise-power iteration, including direct
squaring of the specific known positive kernel. They do not exclude
corrected products, other kernels, full-rank matrix constructions, or other
representations of their infinite sums. In particular, failure of a
pointwise telescoping representation is not a theorem that the sum cannot
coincidentally equal a rational linear form in alpha.

The positive-kernel route still lacks a growing family with integral
retained coefficients, nonzero values, and errors tending to zero. The
arithmetic carry and boundary-lattice routes also remain incomplete. No
complete informal proof or disproof is awaiting formalization.
